# Form 1040 line 10 — Adjustments to income from Schedule 1, line 26

`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\10.md`.

## 1. At a glance

Form 1040 line 10 copies the total of **Schedule 1, Part II** ("Adjustments to Income"), which is Schedule 1 line 26.
Adjustments are the deductions taken *before* adjusted gross income (AGI): they are subtracted from total income
(Form 1040 line 9) whether or not the filer itemizes. Each Schedule 1 line has its own rule, limit and, often, its own
form (Form 2106, 8889, 3903, Schedule SE, Form 7206, Form 8853, Form 2555); several have income phase-outs that
depend on a "modified AGI" figured from line 9 and the *other* adjustments.

```text
Form 1040 line 10 = Schedule 1 line 26
Schedule 1 line 26 = lines 11 + 12 + 13 + 14 + 15 + 16 + 17 + 18 + 19a + 20 + 21 + 23 + 25
                     (line 22 is reserved; 19b and 19c are not amounts)
Schedule 1 line 25 = lines 24a + 24b + … + 24k + 24z
Form 1040 line 11a (AGI) = line 9 − line 10
```

Terms used below:

- **Adjustment to income** ("above-the-line deduction") — a deduction subtracted from total income to reach AGI.
- **MAGI** — modified adjusted gross income: AGI (or line 9 less some adjustments) with certain items added back.
  Each deduction that phases out defines its own MAGI.
- **Phase-out** — a range of MAGI over which a deduction shrinks from its full amount to zero.
- **Covered by a retirement plan** (active participant) — an employee who could take part in an employer pension,
  401(k), SEP, SIMPLE, etc., during the year; W-2 box 13 "Retirement plan" shows it.

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 10:** "Adjustments to income from Schedule 1, line 26".

## 3. What goes on this line

The single number from Schedule 1 line 26: the sum of every adjustment in Part II (sections 6.1 – 6.18 below).

## 6. How to figure it, step by step

Order matters: lines 11–19a, 23 and 24a–24k (line 25) are figured first; then the IRA deduction (line 20), whose
worksheet uses them; then student loan interest (line 21), whose worksheet uses line 20. Then add (section 6.19).
Social security benefits interact with line 20 (section 7).

### 6.1 Line 11 — Educator expenses

`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 93`:

> "If you were an eligible educator in 2025, you can deduct on line 11 up to $300 of qualified expenses you paid in
> 2025. If you and your spouse are filing jointly and both of you were eligible educators, the maximum deduction is
> $600. However, neither spouse can deduct more than $300 of their qualified expenses on line 11. An eligible educator
> is a kindergarten through grade 12 teacher, instructor, counselor, principal, or aide who worked in a school for at
> least 900 hours during a school year."

Qualified expenses: professional-development courses and classroom books, supplies, equipment (including computer
equipment, software and services), and COVID-prevention supplies; **not** home-schooling or non-athletic supplies for
health / PE courses. Reduce them by excludable Form 8815 bond interest, nontaxable QTP and Coverdell earnings, and
reimbursements not in W-2 box 1 (`p. 93`).

Steps: per eligible educator, qualified expenses − reductions, capped at $300; add the two spouses on a joint return.

### 6.2 Line 12 — Reservists, performing artists, fee-basis officials (Form 2106)

`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 93`: "Certain business expenses of National Guard and reserve members who traveled more than 100
miles from home … Performing-arts-related expenses as a qualified performing artist. • Business expenses of fee-basis
state or local government officials. For more details, see Form 2106."

Form 2106 (`i2106.pdf p. 3–4`): line 10 = (vehicle + parking/tolls + travel + other − non-meal reimbursements) +
(meals − meal reimbursements) × 50% (80% under DOT hours-of-service rules). Only the eligible part goes to line 12:

- **Reservist:** "the part of the line 10 amount attributable to the expenses for travel more than 100 miles away from
  home … limited to the regular federal per diem rate … and the standard mileage rate … plus any parking fees, ferry
  fees, and tolls" (`i2106.pdf p. 3–4`).
- **Qualified performing artist:** employee of at least two performing-arts employers, $200+ wages from each, expenses
  over 10% of performing-arts gross income, and AGI of $16,000 or less before the deduction; if married, a joint return
  unless apart all year; tests 1–3 per spouse, test 4 on combined AGI (`i2106.pdf p. 4`).
- **Fee-basis official:** the expenses of that job (`i2106.pdf p. 4`).

Form 2106 gets its own forms file.

### 6.3 Line 13 — Health savings account (Form 8889)

`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 93`: "You may be able to take this deduction if contributions (other than employer
contributions, rollovers, and qualified HSA funding distributions from an IRA) were made to your HSA for 2025. See
Form 8889."

Form 8889 line 13 per person (`i8889.pdf p. 4`): limit "$4,300" self-only, "$8,550" family, "+$1,000" at 55+, reduced
by employer contributions, Archer MSA contributions and funding distributions; no deduction for Medicare months or if
"someone else's dependent". Married: "if … either of you has an HDHP with family coverage, you both are treated as
having only the family coverage plan … Complete a separate Form 8889 for each spouse. Combine the amounts on line 13 of
both Forms 8889 and enter this amount on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 13." Form 8889 gets its own forms file.

### 6.4 Line 14 — Moving expenses, Armed Forces (Form 3903)

`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 93`: "You can deduct moving expenses if you are a member of the Armed Forces on active duty and
due to a military order you move because of a permanent change of station … If you are claiming only storage fees
during your absence from the United States, check the box on line 14."

Form 3903 (`i3903.pdf p. 2`): line 5 = line 3 (moving costs) − line 4 (government payments not in W-2 box 1, "box 12
… code P"); if line 4 ≥ line 3 there is no deduction and the excess goes to Form 1040 line 1h. Storage-fees-only
filers (moved in an earlier year, government storage payments in box 1) skip Form 3903 and "check the box for storage
fees next to line 14" (`i3903.pdf p. 2`).

### 6.5 Line 15 — Deductible part of self-employment tax

`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 93`: "If you were self-employed and owe self-employment tax, fill in Schedule SE to figure the
amount of your deduction. The deductible part of your self-employment tax is on line 13 of Schedule SE." (One half of
the SE tax, per person.) A bona fide resident of American Samoa or Puerto Rico who excludes self-employment income keeps
only the part based on non-excluded income (Pub. 570 ch. 4, `p570.pdf`). Schedule SE gets its own forms file.

### 6.6 Line 16 — Self-employed SEP, SIMPLE and qualified plans

`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 93–94`: "If you were self-employed or a partner, you may be able to take this deduction. See Pub.
560 or, if you were a minister, Pub. 517." (Pub. 560 is not in the source set; its limits are left to the Schedule SE /
SE forms files.)

### 6.7 Line 17 — Self-employed health insurance (worksheet or Form 7206)

`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 94`: one of these must be true — net profit on Schedule C or F; a partner with net SE earnings;
an optional SE method used; or "You received wages in 2025 from an S corporation in which you were a more-than-2%
shareholder. Health insurance premiums paid or reimbursed by the S corporation are shown as wages on Form W-2." "The
insurance plan must be established under your business." No deduction for any month "you were also eligible to
participate in any subsidized health plan maintained by your or your spouse's employer".

Worksheet (`p. 94`): line 1 premiums; line 2 "net profit and any other earned income from the business under which the
insurance plan is established, minus any deductions on Schedule 1, lines 15 and 16" (for a more-than-2% shareholder,
"earned income is your Medicare wages (box 5 of Form W-2) from that corporation"); line 3 = smaller of 1 and 2.

Use Form 7206 instead when "You had more than one source of income subject to self-employment tax. • You file Form
2555. • You are using amounts paid for qualified long-term care insurance"; use Pub. 974 when a Marketplace plan and the
premium tax credit are involved (`p. 95`). Form 7206 gets its own forms file.

### 6.8 Line 18 — Penalty on early withdrawal of savings

`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 95`: "The Form 1099-INT or Form 1099-OID you received will show the amount of any penalty you
were charged." Payer instructions: box 2 is "interest or principal forfeited because of an early withdrawal of time
deposits … that is deductible from gross income by the recipient. Do not reduce the amount reported in box 1"
(`i1099int.pdf p. 3`). Pub. 17: "the deductible amount will be shown on Form 1099-INT, box 2" (`p17.pdf p. 63`).

Steps: add the penalty boxes of **your** Forms 1099-INT / 1099-OID (and your spouse's on a joint return). The interest
stays in full on line 2b.

### 6.9 Lines 19a, 19b, 19c — Alimony paid

`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 95`: "If you made payments to or for your spouse or former spouse under a divorce or separation
agreement entered into on or before December 31, 2018, you may be able to take this deduction. You can't take a
deduction … if you entered into your divorce or separation agreement after December 31, 2018, or if … the agreement was
changed after December 31, 2018, to expressly provide that alimony received is not included in your former spouse's
income." "On line 19c, enter the month and year of your original divorce or separation agreement." Line 19b is the
recipient's SSN (form face).

### 6.10 Line 20 — IRA deduction (IRA Deduction Worksheet)

`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 95`: "If you made contributions to a traditional IRA for 2025, you may be able to take an IRA
deduction. But you, or your spouse if filing a joint return, must have had earned income to do so. For IRA purposes,
earned income includes alimony … reported on Schedule 1, line 2a. … earned income includes any nontaxable combat pay
… If you were self-employed, earned income is generally your net earnings from self-employment". The list before the
worksheet (`p. 95`) excludes Roth contributions, elective deferrals, prior-year-deducted contributions, NQDC/§457 income
in W-2 box 1 ("don't include that income on line 8 of the worksheet"), rollovers, trustee fees and reservist
repayments; "You must file a joint return to deduct contributions to your spouse's IRA." A joint filer with both
traditional and Roth contributions uses Pub. 590-A instead of the worksheet.

Covered by a plan (`p. 95–96`): "The "Retirement plan" box in box 13 of your Form W-2 should be checked if you were
covered … You are also covered by a plan if you were self-employed and had a SEP, SIMPLE, or qualified retirement
plan." "If you were covered by a retirement plan and you file Form 2555 or 8815, or you exclude employer-provided
adoption benefits, see Pub. 590-A". Married filing separately: "If you weren't covered by a retirement plan but your
spouse was, you are considered covered by a plan unless you lived apart from your spouse for all of 2025. If you are
married filing separately and you lived apart from your spouse for all of 2025, check the box on line 20. If you don't
check the box on line 20, you may get a math error notice from the IRS." (`p. 96`).

**IRA Deduction Worksheet** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 97–98`), one column per spouse:

1. 1a/1b — covered by a retirement plan? If "No" for everyone: line 7 = $7,000 ($8,000 if 50 or older), go to line 8.
2. Line 2 — top of the range: single / HOH / MFS-lived-apart **$89,000**; QSS **$146,000**; MFJ **$146,000** each
   column, but **$246,000** for a spouse who was not covered; MFS lived together **$10,000**.
3. Line 3 — Form 1040 line 9.
4. Line 4 — "Schedule 1, lines 11 through 19a, plus 23 and 25".
5. Line 5 — line 3 − line 4 (MAGI).
6. Line 6 — if line 5 ≥ line 2: "None of your IRA contributions are deductible." Else line 2 − line 5; if that is
   $10,000 or more (single / HOH / MFS) or $20,000 or more (MFJ / QSS; $10,000 for the not-covered spouse), use the full
   $7,000 / $8,000.
7. Line 7 — line 6 × 70% (80% if 50+) for single / HOH / MFS and the not-covered MFJ spouse; × 35% (40% if 50+) for
   MFJ / QSS. "If the result isn't a multiple of $10, increase it to the next multiple of $10 … If the result is $200 or
   more, enter the result. But if it is less than $200, enter $200."
8. Line 8 — wages (W-2 box 1, less the exceptions), alimony on Schedule 1 line 2a, nontaxable combat pay; line 9 — net
   SE earnings "minus any deductions on Schedule 1, lines 15 and 16"; line 10 = 8 + 9. MFJ with line 10 under
   $14,000 / $15,000 / $16,000 → Pub. 590-A.
9. Line 11 — traditional contributions made for 2025 by April 15, 2026.
10. Line 12 — "the smallest of line 7a, 10, or 11a" (and b). Add 12a + 12b → Schedule 1 line 20. "Or, if you want, you
    can deduct a smaller amount and treat the rest as a nondeductible contribution (see Form 8606)."

Pub. 590-A Worksheet 1-1 MAGI (used when Form 2555 / 8815 / adoption exclusion applies): AGI without line 20, plus line
21, plus "foreign earned income exclusion and/or housing exclusion from Form 2555, line 45", plus "any foreign housing
deduction from Form 2555, line 50", plus Form 8815 line 14, plus Form 8839 line 30 (`p590a.pdf p. 16`). 2025 ranges
(`p590a.pdf p. 2`): covered — $126,000–$146,000 MFJ / QSS, $79,000–$89,000 single / HOH, under $10,000 MFS; spouse
covered, you not — $236,000–$246,000.

### 6.11 Line 21 — Student loan interest (worksheet)

`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 98`: deductible only if "You paid interest in 2025 on a qualified student loan … Your filing status
is any status except married filing separately. • Your modified adjusted gross income (AGI) is less than $100,000 if
single, head of household, or qualifying surviving spouse; $200,000 if married filing jointly … • You, or your spouse if
filing jointly, aren't claimed as a dependent on someone else's … 2025 tax return." Exclude interest paid with tax-free
QTP earnings. Exception: "Use Pub. 970 instead of the worksheet … if you file Form 2555 or 4563, or you exclude income
from sources within Puerto Rico."

**Student Loan Interest Deduction Worksheet** (`p. 99`):

1. Interest paid in 2025 on qualified student loans; "Don't enter more than $2,500".
2. Form 1040 line 9.
3. "Schedule 1, lines 11 through 20, and 23 and 25".
4. Line 2 − line 3 (MAGI).
5. $85,000 (single, HOH, QSS) / $170,000 (MFJ).
6. If line 4 > line 5: line 4 − line 5; else line 8 = 0.
7. Line 6 ÷ $15,000 ($30,000 MFJ), "rounded to at least three places", max 1.000.
8. Line 1 × line 7.
9. Line 1 − line 8 → Schedule 1 line 21. "Don't include this amount in figuring any other deduction".

### 6.12 Line 22 — Reserved

"Line 22 has been reserved for future use." (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 99`).

### 6.13 Line 23 — Archer MSA (Form 8853)

"See Form 8853." (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 99`). Form 8853 line 5; the monthly limit is "65% (0.65) of the annual
deductible" (self-only) or "75% (0.75)" (family), and compensation from the employer (`i8853.pdf p. 4`). Form 8853 gets
its own forms file.

### 6.15 Line 24z

"Line 24z. Leave line 24z blank." (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 100`). (The IRA and student-loan worksheets still say "If the

### 6.16 Line 25

Add lines 24a through 24z.

### 6.17 Line 26

"Add lines 11 through 23 and 25." Enter on Form 1040 line 10.

### 6.18 Rounding

Amounts may be rounded to whole dollars; when several amounts are added for one line, "include cents when adding the
amounts and round off only the total" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`). Each Schedule 1 line is a separate entry, so line 26
should equal the sum of the lines as printed.

### 6.19 Order of work

1. Lines 11–19a, 23, 24a–24k → line 25.
2. Line 20 (IRA worksheet uses 11–19a, 23, 25 and line 9).
3. Line 21 (student-loan worksheet uses 11–20, 23, 25 and line 9).
4. Line 26 → Form 1040 line 10 → line 11a = line 9 − line 10.

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** each spouse's adjustments are added. Per-person limits stay per person: $300 educator cap each;
  each spouse's Form 8889 / 8853 / 2106 / 3903 / Schedule SE; separate IRA worksheet columns; one $2,500 student-loan
  cap for the return.
- **Separate returns:** only the filer's own adjustments, own 1099-INT / 1099-OID penalties and own Forms 1098-E. No
  student loan interest deduction. IRA: the $0–$10,000 range when living together and either spouse is covered; check
  the line 20 box when apart all year. A performing artist must file jointly unless apart all year.
- A dependent's own statements (a child's 1099-INT penalty, a child's 1098-E) belong on the child's own return.

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Form 1040 line 11a / 11b** = line 9 − line 10 (AGI), and from there taxable income and every AGI-based limit.
- Partial sums feed worksheets: Social Security Benefits Worksheet (lines 11–20, 23, 25 — `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 32`),
  IRA worksheet line 4 (11–19a, 23, 25), student loan worksheet line 3 (11–20, 23, 25).
- Line 15 and 16 also reduce earned income for lines 16 / 17 and for IRA compensation; line 17 is excluded from
  Schedule A medical expenses.

## 11. Worked example

Casey Example (000-00-0011), single, age 40, an elementary-school teacher (1,200 hours), covered by the school's
403(b) plan (W-2 box 13 checked).

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| W-2 box 1 | $82,000 |
| Taxable interest (Form 1099-INT box 1) | $500 → Form 1040 line 9 = **$82,500** |
| Form 1099-INT box 2 (CD broken early) | $120 |
| Classroom supplies, unreimbursed | $350 |
| HSA contributions by check (Form 8889 line 13) | $2,000 |
| Traditional IRA contribution for 2025 | $7,000 |
| Form 1098-E box 1 (interest paid) | $1,800 |

1. Line 11: min($350, $300) = **$300**.
2. Line 13: **$2,000**. Line 18: **$120**. Line 25 = 0.
3. IRA worksheet: line 2 = $89,000; line 3 = $82,500; line 4 = $300 + $2,000 + $120 = $2,420; line 5 = $80,080;
   line 6 = $89,000 − $80,080 = $8,920 (under $10,000); line 7 = $8,920 × 0.70 = $6,244 → **$6,250** (next $10);
   line 10 = $82,000; line 11 = $7,000; line 12 = min($6,250, $82,000, $7,000) = **$6,250** → line 20. The other
   $750 is a nondeductible contribution on Form 8606.
4. Student loan worksheet: line 1 = $1,800; line 3 = $300 + $2,000 + $120 + $6,250 = $8,670; line 4 = $73,830, not
   more than $85,000 → line 8 = 0; line 9 = **$1,800** → line 21.
5. Line 26 = $300 + $2,000 + $120 + $6,250 + $1,800 = **$10,470** → **Form 1040 line 10 = $10,470**.
6. AGI (line 11a) = $82,500 − $10,470 = $72,030.
