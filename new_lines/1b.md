# Form 1040 line 1b — Household employee wages not reported on Form(s) W-2

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\1b.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\1b.md`.

---

## 1. At a glance

Form 1040 line 1b is the total of the wages you received **as a household employee** that were **not reported on any
Form W-2** — and, on a joint return, your spouse's too. A household employee is someone paid to do work in or
around another person's home (babysitter, nanny, housekeeper, health aide, yard worker …) when the person paying
has the right to control what work is done and how it is done. A household employer does not have to give a W-2
when the cash wages are under $2,800 for 2025 (and no income tax was withheld), so these wages often arrive with no
form at all. The $2,800 figure explains why a W-2 is missing; it is **not** a test for using line 1b: any household
wages that are not on a W-2 go here, whatever the amount.

```text
Form 1040 line 1b =
    Σ wages received as a household employee (cash and taxable noncash pay)
      that were NOT reported on any Form W-2
    (you; + your spouse on a joint return)
```

Terms used below:

- **Household work** — work done in or around a private home: babysitters, cooks, maids, butlers, drivers,
  nannies, caretakers, health aides, private nurses, cleaning people, housekeepers, yard workers
  (`i1040sh.pdf p. 2`).
- **Household employee** — a worker doing household work for someone who "could control what work they did and how
  they did it" (`i1040sh.pdf p. 2`). A worker who alone controls how the work is done is **self-employed**, not a
  household employee.
- **Cash wages** — pay by cash, check, money order, etc.; it does not include food, lodging, clothing or other
  noncash items (`i1040sh.pdf p. 4`). The $2,800 test counts cash wages only; income tax applies to noncash wages too.

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 1b:** "Household employee wages not reported on Form(s) W-2".

**2025 Instructions for Form 1040 — Line 1b** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`):

> "Enter the total of your wages received as a household employee that was not reported on Form(s) W-2. An employer
> isn't required to provide a Form W-2 to you if they paid you wages of less than $2,800 in 2025. For information on
> employment taxes for household employees, see Tax Topic 756."

**Pub. 17 (2025), Employee Compensation — Form W-2** (`p17.pdf p. 49`):

> "Your employer isn't required to give you a Form W-2 if you perform household work in your employer's home for less
> than $2,800 in cash wages during the calendar year and you have no federal income taxes withheld from your wages.
> Household work is work done in or around an employer's home."

The same paragraph begins "Include your pay on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 1a, even if you don't receive a Form W-2."
For household wages the specific Form 1040 line 1b instruction above sends them to **line 1b**; Pub. 17's general
sentence is read as covering other employees.

**Who is a household employee** (Instructions for Schedule H (2025), `i1040sh.pdf p. 2`):

> "If you hired someone to do household work and you could control what work they did and how they did it, you had a
> household employee. This is true even if you gave the employee freedom of action. What matters is that you had the
> right to control the details of how the work was done."
>
> "Workers you get from an agency aren't your employees if the agency is responsible for who does the work and how it
> is done. Self-employed workers are also not your employees. A worker is self-employed if only the worker can
> control how the work is done. A self-employed worker usually provides their own tools and offers services to the
> general public in an independent business."

The Form 1040 instructions repeat the definition (Schedule 2 line 9, `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 113`): "Any person who
does household work is a household employee if you can control what will be done and how it will be done."

**When the household employer must give a W-2** (`i1040sh.pdf p. 2–3`):

> "You must file Form W-2 for each household employee to whom you paid $2,800 or more of cash wages in 2025 that are
> subject to social security and Medicare taxes. … Even if the wages aren't subject to these taxes, if you withheld
> federal income tax from the wages of any household employee, you must file Form W-2 for that employee."

**Noncash wages** (`i1040sh.pdf p. 4`):

> "Noncash wages paid to household employees aren't subject to social security taxes or Medicare taxes; however, they
> are subject to federal income tax unless a specific exclusion applies."

## 3. What goes on this line

- Cash wages from each household employer that did not give you a W-2 for them — for example, a babysitter paid
  $1,900 by a family in 2025 (below $2,800, so no W-2 is required).
- Household wages of $2,800 or more for which the employer should have, but did not, give a W-2. Line 1b is the
  place for any household wages "not reported on Form(s) W-2" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`); there is no amount
  limit. (Ask the employer for the W-2 first — `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25`, "Missing or Incorrect Form W-2?".)
- The value of taxable noncash pay from a household employer that is not on a W-2 (taxable for income tax,
  `i1040sh.pdf p. 4`).
- On a joint return, the spouse's household wages not on a W-2 (joint return reports both spouses' income,
  `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23` for line 1a; the same joint-return principle applies).

## 4. What does not go here

| Look-alike amount | Where it goes instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Household wages that **are** on a Form W-2 (box 1) | Form 1040 line 1a | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23` |
| Household wages on a missing / incorrect W-2 for which you complete Form 4852 (Substitute W-2) | Form 1040 line 1a (Form 4852 line 7a stands in for W-2 box 1) | `f4852.pdf p. 1`; see `new_lines\1a.md` |
| Pay for work in a home when **you** controlled how the work was done (independent business — e.g., a lawn-care business serving the public) | Schedule C (and Schedule SE) | `i1040sh.pdf p. 2`; `f8919.pdf p. 2` ("For services you performed as an independent contractor. Instead, use Schedule C") |
| Workers placed by an agency that controls the work | The agency is the employer — its W-2 → line 1a | `i1040sh.pdf p. 2` |
| Wages from a firm that treated you as an independent contractor although you were an employee, where Form 8919's conditions are met (reason code A, C, G or H) and social security / Medicare were owed but not withheld | Form 8919 line 6 → Form 1040 line 1g | `f8919.pdf p. 1–2` |
| Tips not reported to the employer | Form 1040 line 1c | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24` |
| Medicaid waiver payments for caring for someone in your home, not on a W-2 | Form 1040 line 1d (and Schedule 1 line 8s) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24` |
| A dependent child's own household wages (e.g., a teenager's babysitting pay) | The child's own return — not the parent's | `p17.pdf p. 10` ("Amounts a child earns by performing services are included in the child's gross income and not the gross income of the parent") |
| Household employment **taxes you owe as an employer** of a household worker | Schedule H → Schedule 2 line 9 (not income) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 113` |

## 5. Where the numbers come from

There is no information return for these wages; the amount comes from the worker's own records.

| Source | Item | How it enters line 1b |
|---|---|---|
| Worker's records (pay stubs, bank deposits, payment log) | Cash wages from each household employer with no W-2 | Added |
| Worker's records | Value of taxable noncash pay (e.g., meals or lodging that are not excluded) from those employers | Added |
| Form W-2 from a household employer | Box 1 | **Not** here — line 1a |
| Form 8958 (married filing separately in a community property state) | Wages allocation | Community household wages are generally split under state law (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`; Pub. 555) |

## 6. How to figure it, step by step

1. **Decide whether each job was household work as an employee.** Was the work done in or around the payer's home,
   and did the payer have the right to control what you did and how you did it? If yes, you were a household
   employee. If only you controlled how the work was done, it is self-employment income (Schedule C), not line 1b
   (`i1040sh.pdf p. 2`).
2. **Check for a W-2.** For each household employer, see whether you got a Form W-2. If you did, its box 1 goes on
   line 1a, not here. If the employer withheld federal income tax, or paid you $2,800 or more in cash wages subject
   to social security and Medicare taxes, it was required to give you a W-2 (`i1040sh.pdf p. 2–3`); ask for it
   (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25`). If it never comes, either complete Form 4852 (line 1a) or report the wages here.
3. **Add the wages not on any W-2** — cash wages plus the value of taxable noncash pay — from every household
   employer. Keep cents while adding.
4. **On a joint return, add your spouse's** amount from step 3.
5. **Round** to whole dollars if you round the return: "include cents when adding the amounts and round off only
   the total" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`).
6. Enter the result on **Form 1040 line 1b**.

There is no IRS worksheet for line 1b, and the $2,800 figure is never compared with the total.

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Wages under $2,800 from one household employer, no income tax withheld | No W-2 is required; report on line 1b | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`; `p17.pdf p. 49` |
| Wages of $2,800 or more, or income tax withheld, but no W-2 received | The employer had to issue a W-2; ask for it; if none, Form 4852 (line 1a) or line 1b | `i1040sh.pdf p. 2–3`; `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25` |
| Social security / Medicare on household wages | Owed by the employer (and withheld from the employee) only when the employer pays one employee $2,800 or more in cash wages in 2025; wages paid to the employer's spouse, the employer's child under 21, the employer's parent (with exceptions) and an employee under 18 (unless household work is the principal occupation of a non-student) are left out of the test. The employee's line 1b amount is income either way | `i1040sh.pdf p. 4–5` ("$2,800 test") |
| Payer did not control the work (self-employed household worker) | Schedule C / Schedule SE, not line 1b | `i1040sh.pdf p. 2`; `f8919.pdf p. 2` |
| Household employer issued a 1099-NEC / 1099-MISC instead of a W-2 | The wages are still household-employee wages "not reported on Form(s) W-2" → line 1b. Form 8919 (→ line 1g) is for an employee owed social security / Medicare who meets one of its reason codes; household wages that fail the $2,800 test carry no social security / Medicare tax | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`; `f8919.pdf p. 1`; `i1040sh.pdf p. 5` |
| Income tax withheld by the household employer | The employer must issue a W-2 (`i1040sh.pdf p. 2–3`); without one, the withholding is claimed using Form 4852 on line 25a | `i1040sh.pdf p. 2–3`; `f4852.pdf p. 1` |
| Asking for withholding | A household worker can ask the employer to withhold income tax; it is withheld only if the employer agrees | `p17.pdf p. 39` |
| Community property state, married filing separately | Follow state law for community income; Form 8958 | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** line 1b includes both spouses' household wages not on a W-2.
- **Separate returns:** each spouse reports only their own household wages. In a community property state, state
  law decides how community wages are split between the two returns (Form 8958; `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`).
- No per-person limit or cap.

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Form 1040 line 1z** = lines 1a through 1h → **line 9** (total income) → **line 11** (AGI).
- Line 1b is **earned income** wherever earned income starts from line 1z: the EIC worksheet (Step 5 line 1, "Enter
  the amount from Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 1z", `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42`), the dependent's standard deduction
  ("your earned income is the total of the amount(s) you reported on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 1z …",
  `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 35`), Schedule 8812, and Form 2441 lines 4/5 ("The amount shown on Form 1040 … line 1z",
  `i2441.pdf p. 4`).
- It is **compensation** for the IRA contribution / deduction limit ("Wages, salaries, tips, professional fees,
  bonuses, and other amounts you receive for providing personal services are compensation", `p590a.pdf p. 6`).
- Through line 1z it enters the Social Security Benefits Worksheet income figure.

## 11. Worked example

Robin Example (000-00-0011) and Casey Example (000-00-0012) file jointly.

| Worker | Payer | Work | Who controlled it | Paid in 2025 | W-2? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Robin | The Lane family | Babysitting in their home | The family | $2,150.25 cash | No (under $2,800) |
| Robin | The Hill family | House cleaning | The family | $3,400.00 cash | No (should have been issued) |
| Robin | Green Lawns (Robin's own lawn-care business, many customers) | Yard work | Robin | $1,200.00 | — |
| Casey | Mr. Oak | Home health aide | Mr. Oak | $6,000.00 | **Yes**, box 1 $6,000 |
| Casey | Ms. Pine | Weekend caregiving | Ms. Pine | $900.00 cash + $100.00 of taxable meals | No |

Steps:

1. Household employee jobs: Robin's Lane and Hill jobs, Casey's Oak and Pine jobs. Robin's lawn-care work is a
   self-employed business — Schedule C, not line 1b.
2. Casey's Oak wages are on a W-2 → line 1a ($6,000). Robin should ask the Hill family for the W-2 they were required
   to issue; none arrived, and Robin reports the wages here instead of using Form 4852.
3. Robin: $2,150.25 + $3,400.00 = $5,550.25.
4. Casey: $900.00 + $100.00 = $1,000.00. Joint total: $6,550.25.
5. Round the total: **Form 1040 line 1b = $6,550**.

Line 1z includes the $6,550, so it counts as earned income for the EIC, Form 2441 and IRA compensation.
