# Form 1040 line 1d — Medicaid waiver payments not reported on Form(s) W-2

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\1d.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\1d.md`.

## 1. At a glance

Some people are paid by a state Medicaid program to care for a disabled or elderly person who lives in the caregiver's
own home. Notice 2014-7 treats those payments as **difficulty-of-care payments**, which are excluded from income. Form
1040 line 1d is where Medicaid waiver payments that did **not** come through W-2 box 1 enter the return:

- **taxable** Medicaid waiver payments not reported in W-2 box 1 (for example, pay for care given outside the
  caregiver's home), always;
- **nontaxable** (excluded) payments not reported in W-2 box 1, **only if** the caregiver chooses to count them as
  earned income for a credit or other tax benefit (the "election");
- payments reported on **Form 1099-MISC or 1099-NEC** to a caregiver who has no home-care business.

Whatever nontaxable amount sits on line 1a (inside W-2 box 1) or on line 1d is then taken back out as a **negative
amount on Schedule 1 line 8s**, so the exclusion does not change total income — it only decides how much of the
payment counts as earned income.

```text
Form 1040 line 1d (per person, then added for a joint return) =
    taxable Medicaid waiver payments not in W-2 box 1
  + Medicaid waiver payments reported on Form 1099-MISC / 1099-NEC (no home-care business)
  + nontaxable payments not in W-2 box 1     ← only if this person elects to include them in earned income

Schedule 1 line 8s (entered as a negative number) =
    − (nontaxable payments included on line 1a  +  nontaxable payments included on line 1d)
```

Terms used below:

- **Medicaid waiver program** — a state program, approved under section 1915(c) of the Social Security Act, that pays
  for home or community-based care of a person who would otherwise need a hospital or nursing facility
  (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 2`).
- **Qualified Medicaid waiver payment** — a payment by a state, a political subdivision or a certified Medicaid
  provider "to an individual care provider for nonmedical support services provided under a plan of care to an
  eligible individual (whether related or unrelated) living in the individual care provider's home"
  (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 5–6`). These are the payments Notice 2014-7 excludes.
- **Difficulty-of-care payment** — under IRC §131(c), compensation to a foster care provider for the extra care a
  person with a physical, mental or emotional handicap needs, given in the provider's home (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 2`;
  `p525.pdf p. 33`).
- **The election** — the caregiver's choice to count nontaxable payments as earned income "for purposes of claiming a
  credit or other tax benefit" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`). It does not make the payments taxable.
- **Box 12 code II** — the W-2 code an employer uses for Medicaid waiver payments it left out of box 1
  (`iw2w3--2025.pdf p. 22`).

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 1d:** "Medicaid waiver payments not reported on Form(s) W-2 (see instructions)"
(`C:\us-tax\docs\IRS-Forms\f1040.pdf p. 1`). **Schedule 1 (2025), line 8s:** "Nontaxable amount of Medicaid waiver
payments included on Form 1040, line 1a or 1d" (`C:\us-tax\docs\IRS-Forms\f1040s1.pdf p. 1`).

**2025 Instructions for Form 1040 — Line 1d, Medicaid Waiver Payments Not Reported on Form(s) W-2, Box 1**
(`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`):

> "Enter your taxable Medicaid waiver payments that were not reported on Form(s) W-2. Also enter the total of your
> taxable and nontaxable Medicaid waiver payments that were not reported on Form(s) W-2, or not reported in box 1 of
> Form(s) W-2, if you choose to include nontaxable payments in earned income for purposes of claiming a credit or
> other tax benefit. If you and your spouse both received nontaxable Medicaid waiver payments during the year, you
> and your spouse can make different choices about including payments in earned income. See the instructions for
> Schedule 1, line 8s."
>
> "If you are a sole proprietor in a business of providing home care services, see the Schedule C instructions for
> how to report these amounts. If you do not have a separate trade or business of providing these services, enter on
> Form 1040, line 1d, your Medicaid waiver payments reported on Form 1099-MISC or Form 1099-NEC. Also, enter your
> nontaxable Medicaid waiver payments on Schedule 1, line 8s."
>
> "Your nontaxable Medicaid waiver payments may have been reported to you on Form(s) W-2, box 12, with Code II."
>
> "If you received nontaxable Medicaid waiver payments, and box 1 of your Form(s) W-2 is blank or has zeros, and you
> are choosing not to include nontaxable payments in earned income for purposes of claiming a credit, do not attach
> any of these Form(s) W-2 to your return."

**Schedule 1, line 8s** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 92`):

> "Certain Medicaid waiver payments you received for caring for someone living in your home with you may be
> nontaxable. Your nontaxable Medicaid waiver payments should be reported to you on Form(s) W-2 in box 12, Code II. If
> nontaxable payments were reported to you in box 1 of Form(s) W-2, report the amount on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line
> 1a. If you did not receive a Form W-2 for nontaxable payments, or you received nontaxable payments that you didn't
> report on line 1a, and choose to include nontaxable amounts in earned income for purposes of claiming a credit or
> other tax benefit, report the amount on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 1d. Then, on line 8s, enter the total amount of
> the nontaxable payments reported on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 1a or 1d, in the entry space in the preprinted
> parentheses (as a negative number)."
>
> "If you do not have a separate trade or business of providing these services, enter any nontaxable Medicaid waiver
> payments on Schedule 1, line 8s. Also, enter your Medicaid waiver payments reported on Form 1099-MISC or Form
> 1099-NEC on Form 1040, line 1d."

**Notice 2014-7 — the exclusion** (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 5`):

> "this notice provides that as of January 3, 2014, the Service will treat qualified Medicaid waiver payments as
> difficulty of care payments under § 131(c) that are excludable under § 131, and this treatment will apply whether
> the care provider is related or unrelated to the eligible individual."

**Notice 2014-7 — the home requirement** (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 6`):

> "Medicaid waiver payments made to a provider for care outside of the home where the provider resides are not
> qualified Medicaid waiver payments and are not excludable under § 131."

**Notice 2014-7 — the limit on the number of people cared for** (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 2`, `p. 7`):

> "In the case of any foster home, difficulty of care payments are not excludable to the extent that the payments are
> for more than 10 qualified foster individuals who have not attained age 19 or 5 qualified foster individuals who
> have attained age 19."
>
> "Because qualified Medicaid waiver payments are difficulty of care payments, they are subject to these limits."

**W-2 reporting by the payer** (`iw2w3--2025.pdf p. 2`, `p. 22`):

> "Report Medicaid waiver payments excluded from box 1 of Form W-2 under Notice 2014-7 in box 12 with code II."
>
> "Code II—Medicaid waiver payments excluded from gross income under Notice 2014-7. Report the amount of Medicaid
> waiver payments not reported in box 1."

**EIC earned income, Step 5 line 2** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42`):

> "2. Enter the Medicaid waiver payment amounts excluded from income on Schedule 1, line 8s, unless you choose to
> include these amounts in earned income, in which case enter -0-."
>
> "If you and your spouse both received Medicaid waiver payments during the year, you and your spouse can make
> different choices about including the full amount of your payments in earned income. Enter only the amount of
> Medicaid waiver payments that you or your spouse, if filing a joint return, do not want to include in earned income."

**Schedule 8812 Earned Income Worksheet, line 4** (`i1040s8.pdf p. 8`) uses the same wording: "Enter the Medicaid
waiver payment amounts excluded from income on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8s, unless you choose to include these
amounts in earned income."

**Home-care business on Schedule C** (`i1040sc.pdf p. 6`):

> "If you receive Medicaid waiver payments on a Form 1099-MISC or Form 1099-NEC that are excludable from gross income
> under Notice 2014-7, report the full amount of the payments as income on Schedule C, line 1. Then report the
> nontaxable and excludable amount as an expense in Part V, Other Expenses, and write "Notice 2014-7" next to the
> amount."

## 3. What goes on this line

- **Taxable Medicaid waiver payments not in W-2 box 1** — for example, payments for care of someone who does not live
  in the caregiver's home (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 6`), or the part of a payment over the §131(c) head-count limit
  (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 2, 7`), when the payer did not put it in box 1 (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`).
- **Medicaid waiver payments reported on Form 1099-MISC or 1099-NEC** to a caregiver with no separate home-care
  business — the 1099 amount goes on line 1d and its nontaxable part on Schedule 1 line 8s
  (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24, p. 92`).
- **Nontaxable payments not in W-2 box 1** (box 12 code II, or no W-2 at all) — **only** when this person elects to
  include them in earned income (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`); the same amount then goes on line 8s as a negative.

## 4. What does not go here

| Look-alike amount | Where it goes instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Medicaid waiver payments already in W-2 box 1 (taxable or not) | Form 1040 line 1a; the nontaxable part is backed out on Schedule 1 line 8s | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 92` |
| Nontaxable payments not in box 1 when the caregiver does **not** elect | Nowhere — they are simply not reported (no line 1d, no line 8s) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24, p. 92` |
| Payments received in a home-care **business** (sole proprietor) | Schedule C line 1 (full amount); the excludable part as a Part V "Notice 2014-7" expense | `i1040sc.pdf p. 6, p. 16`; `p334.pdf p. 25` |
| Ordinary foster care payments for a qualified foster individual | Excluded under §131 (not a Medicaid waiver payment); not reported | `p525.pdf p. 33` |
| Pay for skilled services that only a health professional may perform | Not "personal care services" under the waiver definition; not covered by Notice 2014-7 (taxable as ordinary pay) | `notice-2014-7.pdf p. 2` |

## 5. Where the numbers come from

| Source document / form | Box or line | How it enters |
|---|---|---|
| Form W-2 | Box 12 code II | Nontaxable payments not in box 1 → line 1d **only with the election** (and line 8s) |
| Form W-2 | Box 1 | Waiver pay in box 1 stays on line 1a; its nontaxable part → line 8s |
| Form 1099-MISC / 1099-NEC (caregiver has no business) | The box holding the payment (1099-MISC box 3 or 6, 1099-NEC box 1) | Full amount → line 1d; nontaxable part → line 8s |
| No form | Caregiver's records | Taxable part → line 1d; nontaxable part → line 1d + line 8s only with the election |
| Caregiver's facts | Did the person cared for live in the caregiver's home? | Decides taxable vs nontaxable (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 6`) |
| Caregiver's facts | Number of people cared for, under 19 / 19 or older | More than 10 / 5 → the excess-share is taxable (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 2, 7`) |

## 6. How to figure it, step by step

Do this for each person (you, and your spouse on a joint return) separately — each may make a different election
(`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`).

1. **Is it a home-care business?** If you are a sole proprietor in the business of providing home care, stop: the
   payments go on Schedule C (`i1040sc.pdf p. 6`). Otherwise continue.
2. **Split each payment into qualified and not qualified.** A payment is qualified only if it is a Medicaid waiver
   payment for nonmedical support services under a plan of care for someone **living in your home**
   (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 5–6`). Care in the other person's own home is not qualified (`p. 6`).
3. **Apply the head-count limit.** If the payments are for more than 10 people under 19 or more than 5 people 19 or
   older in your home, the payments for the people over the limit are not excludable (`notice-2014-7.pdf p. 2, 7`).
   The IRS documents give no allocation method when payments cannot be traced to individuals.
4. **Locate each part.** For each payment, note how much is in W-2 box 1, how much is in box 12 code II, and how much
   came on a 1099-MISC / 1099-NEC or with no form.
5. **Line 1d (always):** add the **taxable** parts that are not in W-2 box 1, and the **full** amount of any payment
   reported on Form 1099-MISC or 1099-NEC (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`).
6. **Line 1d (election only):** if you choose to include nontaxable payments in earned income, also add the
   **nontaxable** parts that are not in W-2 box 1 and not on a 1099 (already counted in step 5).
7. **Schedule 1 line 8s:** enter, as a negative number, the total nontaxable amount included on line 1a (inside W-2
   box 1) or on line 1d (from steps 5 and 6) (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 92`).
8. **Joint return:** add both spouses' step 5–6 amounts for line 1d and both step 7 amounts for line 8s.
9. **Round** the total to whole dollars if you round the return (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`).
10. **Credits:** on EIC Step 5 line 2 and Schedule 8812 Earned Income Worksheet line 4, subtract the line 8s amount of
    each person who did **not** choose to include the payments in earned income (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42`;
    `i1040s8.pdf p. 8`). Each worksheet asks the question separately.

There is no IRS worksheet for line 1d itself.

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Election to include nontaxable payments in earned income | Optional, per person, "for purposes of claiming a credit or other tax benefit"; puts the not-in-box-1 nontaxable amount on line 1d and line 8s | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24, p. 92` |
| Different choices per worksheet | EIC Step 5 line 2 and Schedule 8812 line 4 each ask whether to leave the line 8s amount out | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42`; `i1040s8.pdf p. 8` |
| Spouses choose differently | Allowed | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24, p. 42` |
| Related care recipient (a parent, an adult child) | Still qualifies if they live in the caregiver's home | `notice-2014-7.pdf p. 5` |
| Care outside the caregiver's home | Not qualified; taxable | `notice-2014-7.pdf p. 6` |
| More than 10 under 19 / 5 aged 19+ | Payments for the excess people are not excludable ("In the case of any foster home") | `notice-2014-7.pdf p. 2, 7` |
| Home-care business (sole proprietor) | Schedule C line 1 full amount; excludable part as Part V expense marked "Notice 2014-7" | `i1040sc.pdf p. 6, p. 16` |
| Form 1099-MISC / 1099-NEC without a business | Full amount on line 1d; nontaxable part on line 8s | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24, p. 92` |
| Nontaxable payments with W-2 box 1 blank / 0 and no election | Do not attach those W-2s to a paper return | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24` |
| IRA contributions | Excluded difficulty-of-care payments can raise the nondeductible IRA contribution limit (Form 8606) — not a line 1d matter | `p590a.pdf p. 16`; `i8606.pdf p. 5` |
| Social security / Medicare tax | Notice 2014-7 "does not address" FICA or FUTA | `notice-2014-7.pdf p. 8` |
| Effective date | Payments received on or after January 3, 2014 | `notice-2014-7.pdf p. 8` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** line 1d is the sum of both spouses' amounts; line 8s the sum of both spouses' nontaxable amounts.
  Each spouse makes their own election (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`).
- **Separate returns:** each spouse reports only their own payments and makes their own election.
- **Head-count limit:** stated per foster home ("In the case of any foster home", `notice-2014-7.pdf p. 2`), so spouses
  caring in the same home share one limit.

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Form 1040 line 1z** (lines 1a–1h) → line 9 → line 11 (AGI). Line 8s (through Schedule 1 line 10 → Form 1040 line
  8) cancels the nontaxable part, so AGI includes only the taxable part.
- **Earned income:** line 1z starts EIC Step 5 (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42`), the Schedule 8812 Earned Income
  Worksheet (`i1040s8.pdf p. 8`) and the dependent's standard deduction "earned income" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 35`).
- **Social Security Benefits Worksheet** — through line 1z and line 8 (net zero for the nontaxable part).

## 11. Worked example

Dana Example (000-00-0001) and Lee Example (000-00-0002) file jointly. Neither runs a home-care business.

| Person | Document | Amount | Facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dana | W-2 from Sunrise Home Care | Box 1 $0; box 12 code II $18,000 | Care of her mother, who lives in Dana's home |
| Dana | 1099-NEC from County Waiver Program | Box 1 $4,000 | Care of a neighbour in the neighbour's own home |
| Lee | W-2 from Helping Hands Agency | Box 1 $6,000 | Care of their adult son, who lives with them; the employer put it in box 1 |

Dana elects to include her nontaxable payments in earned income; Lee does not.

1. Dana: the 1099-NEC $4,000 is for care outside her home → taxable → line 1d. The code II $18,000 is qualified and
   not in box 1; she elects → line 1d and line 8s. Dana's line 1d = $4,000 + $18,000 = $22,000; her line 8s part =
   −$18,000.
2. Lee: the $6,000 is qualified but is in box 1 → stays on line 1a; line 8s part = −$6,000. Nothing on line 1d.
3. **Form 1040 line 1d = $22,000.** Line 1a = $6,000. **Schedule 1 line 8s = −$24,000.**
4. Income that is actually taxed: $22,000 + $6,000 − $24,000 = **$4,000** (the neighbour's care).
5. EIC Step 5: line 1 (line 1z) $28,000; line 2 = $6,000 (Lee's amount, not included); earned income $22,000.
