# Form 1040 line 1g — Wages from Form 8919, line 6

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\1g.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\1g.md`.

---

## 1. At a glance

Form 1040 line 1g holds pay you received as a **worker the firm treated as an independent contractor, although you
were really its employee**, when the firm withheld neither social security nor Medicare tax and did not put the pay on
a Form W-2. You list each such firm on **Form 8919** (Uncollected Social Security and Medicare Tax on Wages), give a
reason code (A, C, G or H) that says why you are entitled to employee treatment, and enter the pay in column (f).
Form 8919 line 6 — the total of column (f) — goes on Form 1040 line 1g. On a joint return each spouse who needs the
form files a separate Form 8919, and line 1g is the total of both line 6 amounts.

Form 8919 also figures the employee's share of social security (6.2%, up to the 2025 wage base) and Medicare (1.45%)
tax on that pay (lines 7–13). That tax goes to Schedule 2 line 6, which is **not** part of line 1g; it is described
here only so the form makes sense.

```text
Form 1040 line 1g =
    Form 8919 line 6 (you)
  + Form 8919 line 6 (your spouse, joint return only)

Form 8919 line 6 = Σ column (f) of lines 1–5 (and of lines 1–5 of any extra Forms 8919 for more than five firms)
    column (f) = "Total wages received with no social security or Medicare tax withholding and not reported on Form W-2"
```

Terms used below:

- **Firm** — "any individual, business enterprise, company, nonprofit organization, state, or other entity for which
  you performed services" (`f8919.pdf p. 2`).
- **Misclassified worker** — someone the firm paid as an independent contractor (often with a Form 1099-NEC or
  1099-MISC) although the work made them an employee.
- **Form SS-8** — "Determination of Worker Status for Purposes of Federal Employment Taxes and Income Tax
  Withholding": the request a worker (or firm) sends the IRS to rule on employee vs contractor status.
- **Section 530 employee** — a worker the IRS found to be an employee, where the firm was nevertheless granted relief
  from employment taxes under section 530 of the Revenue Act of 1978 (`f8919.pdf p. 2`).
- **Social security wage base** — the most pay subject to the 6.2% social security tax in a year: $176,100 for 2025
  (`f8919.pdf p. 1`, line 7).

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 1g:** "Wages from Form 8919, line 6".

**2025 Instructions for Form 1040 — Line 1g** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`):

> "Wages From Form 8919, Line 6 — Enter the total of your wages from Form 8919, line 6."

**Schedule 2 line 6** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 112`):

> "If you are an employee who received wages from an employer who didn't withhold social security and Medicare tax
> from your wages, use Form 8919 to figure your share of the unreported tax. Include on line 6 the amount from line 13
> of Form 8919. Include the amount from line 6 of Form 8919 on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 1g."

**Form 8919 (2025) — who must file** (`f8919.pdf p. 1`):

> "You must file Form 8919 if all of the following apply. • You performed services for a firm. • You believe your pay
> from the firm wasn't for services as an independent contractor. • The firm didn't withhold your share of social
> security and Medicare taxes from your pay. • One of the reasons listed below under Reason codes applies to you."
>
> "Name of person who must file this form. If married, complete a separate Form 8919 for each spouse who must file
> this form."

**Reason codes** (`f8919.pdf p. 1`):

> "If none of the reason codes apply to you, but you believe you should have been treated as an employee, enter reason
> code G and file Form SS-8 on or before the date you file your tax return.
> A I filed Form SS-8 and received a determination letter stating that I am an employee of this firm.
> C I received other correspondence from the IRS stating that I am an employee.
> G I filed Form SS-8 with the IRS and haven't received a reply.
> H I received a Form W-2 and a Form 1099-MISC and/or 1099-NEC from this firm for 2025. The amount on Form 1099-MISC
> and/or 1099-NEC should have been included as wages on Form W-2. (Don't file Form SS-8 if you select reason code H.)"

**Line 6** (`f8919.pdf p. 1`): "Total wages. Combine lines 1 through 5 in column (f). Enter here and on Form 1040,
1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 1g".

**Purpose and exclusions** (`f8919.pdf p. 2`):

> "Use Form 8919 to figure and report your share of the uncollected social security and Medicare taxes due on your
> compensation if you were an employee but were treated as an independent contractor by your employer. By filing this
> form, your social security earnings will be credited to your social security record."
>
> "Don't use this form: • For services you performed as an independent contractor. Instead, use Schedule C … And use
> Schedule SE … • To figure the social security and Medicare tax owed on tips you didn't report to your employer,
> including any allocated tips shown on your Form(s) W-2 that you must report as income. Instead, use Form 4137".

**Specific instructions** (`f8919.pdf p. 2`):

> "Lines 1 through 5. Complete a separate line for each firm. If you worked as an employee for more than five firms in
> 2025, attach additional Form(s) 8919 with lines 1 through 5 completed. Complete lines 6 through 13 on only one Form
> 8919. The line 6 amount on that Form 8919 should be the combined totals of all lines 1 through 5 of all your Forms
> 8919."
>
> "Column (b). … can be an employer identification number (EIN) or a social security number (SSN) (if the firm is an
> individual). … If you are unable to obtain the number, enter "unknown.""
>
> "Column (c). … Enter only one reason code on each line. … Enter reason code C if you were designated as a "section
> 530 employee" by the IRS. … Enter reason code H if you received both a Form W-2 and a Form 1099-MISC and/or
> 1099-NEC from the firm and the amount on the Form 1099-MISC and/or 1099-NEC should have been included as wages on
> Form W-2 … If reason code H applies to your situation, don't file Form SS-8."
>
> "Column (d). Complete only if reason code A or C is entered in column (c)."
>
> "Line 6. Also enter this amount on Form 8959, line 3, if you are required to file that form."
>
> "Line 8. For railroad retirement (RRTA) compensation, don't include an amount greater than $176,100".

**Pub. 525 (2025)** (`p525.pdf p. 3`): "If you performed services, other than as an independent contractor, and your
employer didn't withhold social security and Medicare taxes from your pay, you must file Form 8919 … These wages must
be included on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 1g." Pub. 17 says the same (`p17.pdf p. 49`).

## 3. What goes on this line

- Pay from each firm for which **all four** "who must file" conditions hold, entered per firm in Form 8919 column (f):
  pay received with no social security or Medicare tax withheld and not reported on a Form W-2 (`f8919.pdf p. 1`).
- Reason code A — the IRS ruled you an employee in a determination letter after a Form SS-8.
- Reason code C — other IRS correspondence says you are an employee (including a "section 530 employee").
- Reason code G — you filed (or will file, by the return's filing date) a Form SS-8 and have no answer yet; also the
  code to use when none of the others fits but you believe you were an employee (`f8919.pdf p. 1–2`).
- Reason code H — the firm gave you a W-2 **and** a 1099-MISC / 1099-NEC, and the 1099 amount should have been on the
  W-2: for example "employee bonuses, awards, travel expense reimbursements not paid under an accountable plan,
  scholarships, and signing bonuses" (`f8919.pdf p. 2`). Only the 1099 part goes in column (f); the W-2 box 1 part
  stays on line 1a.
- On a joint return, both spouses' Form 8919 line 6 amounts.

## 4. What does not go here

| Look-alike amount | Where it goes instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Pay for work genuinely done as an independent contractor | Schedule C, and Schedule SE for self-employment tax | `f8919.pdf p. 2` |
| Tips not reported to the employer, and W-2 box 8 allocated tips | Form 4137 → Form 1040 line 1c (tax on Schedule 2 line 5) | `f8919.pdf p. 2`; `f4137--2025.pdf p. 1` |
| Pay a W-2 reports in box 1 (even if the employer withheld too little) | Form 1040 line 1a | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23` |
| Pay from a W-2 that was never received or is wrong | Form 4852 (substitute W-2) → line 1a | `f4852.pdf p. 1` |
| Household-employee pay with no W-2 | Form 1040 line 1b | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24` |
| The social security / Medicare tax figured on Form 8919 | Schedule 2 line 6 (not income) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 112` |
| Uncollected social security / Medicare on tips or group-term life insurance shown in W-2 box 12 codes A, B, M, N | Schedule 2 line 13 | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 113` |
| A dependent child's own Form 8919 pay | The child's own return | `p17.pdf p. 10` |

| Form 8919 line 6 (each spouse) | Total of column (f) | **Form 1040 line 1g** (sum of both on a joint return) |
| Form W-2 boxes 3 and 7, RRTA compensation, Form 4137 line 10 (same person) | — | Form 8919 line 8 (only for the tax, lines 9–13) |

## 6. How to figure it, step by step

1. **Check that Form 8919 applies to each firm.** All four conditions on `f8919.pdf p. 1` must hold. If the work was
   really contractor work, stop: it is Schedule C income. If the issue is unreported tips, use Form 4137.
2. **Pick one reason code per firm** (column (c)). A or C need the IRS date in column (d). G needs a Form SS-8 filed
   by the date the return is filed ("Don't attach Form SS-8 to your tax return"). H needs both a W-2 and a 1099 from
   that firm; do not file Form SS-8 with H (`f8919.pdf p. 2`).
3. **Fill columns (a), (b), (e)** for each firm: name, EIN / SSN (or "unknown"), and a check if a 1099-MISC / 1099-NEC
   was received.
4. **Enter column (f)** for each firm: the pay received with no social security or Medicare withheld and not on a W-2.
5. **Line 6** = total of column (f) of all firms (lines 1–5 of every Form 8919 you complete; lines 6–13 appear on
   one form only).
6. **Form 1040 line 1g** = your Form 8919 line 6, plus your spouse's Form 8919 line 6 on a joint return.
7. **Round** to whole dollars if you round the return: "include cents when adding the amounts and round off only the
   total" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`).

The tax part of the form (for completeness — the result goes to Schedule 2 line 6, not to line 1g):

| Line | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | $176,100 (2025 social security wage base) | `f8919.pdf p. 1` |
| 8 | W-2 boxes 3 + 7, RRTA compensation subject to 6.2% (no more than $176,100), Form 4137 line 10 | `f8919.pdf p. 1–2` |
| 9 | Line 7 − line 8; if negative, 0 (and line 10 = 0) | `f8919.pdf p. 1` |
| 10 | Smaller of line 6 or line 9 | `f8919.pdf p. 1` |
| 11 | Line 10 × 0.062 | `f8919.pdf p. 1` |
| 12 | Line 6 × 0.0145 (no cap) | `f8919.pdf p. 1` |
| 13 | Line 11 + line 12 → Schedule 2 line 6 | `f8919.pdf p. 1`; `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 112` |

There is no IRS worksheet for line 1g.

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** each spouse who must file completes **a separate Form 8919** (`f8919.pdf p. 1`); line 1g is the
  total of both line 6 amounts. Each spouse's line 8 uses only that spouse's W-2s, RRTA compensation and Form 4137, so
  each has his or her own wage-base room.
- **Separate returns:** each spouse reports only his or her own Form 8919 on his or her own return.
- No per-person dollar limit applies to line 1g; the $176,100 wage base limits only the 6.2% tax (line 10).

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Form 1040 line 1z** = lines 1a through 1h → line 9 (total income) → line 11 (AGI).
- **Earned income** built from line 1z: the earned income credit (EIC), Schedule 8812, the dependent's standard
  deduction ("earned income … line 1z", `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 35`), Form 2441.
- **IRA compensation**: wages for personal services are compensation (`p590a.pdf p. 6`: "Wages, salaries, tips,
  professional fees, bonuses, and other amounts you receive for providing personal services are compensation").
- **Form 8959 line 3** (Additional Medicare Tax wages) (`i8959.pdf p. 3`).
- The tax (line 13) → **Schedule 2 line 6**. That tax is **left out** of the "tax shown on your return" for the
  estimated-tax penalty (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 65`: "minus the total of any amounts shown on … Forms 8828, 4137,
  5329 (Parts III through IX only), and 8919"), and Schedule 2 line 6 is not among the lines Form 2210 line 2 lists
  (`i2210.pdf p. 2`). For the annualized method, Form 2210 line 30 adds the period's Form 8919 line 10
  (`i2210.pdf p. 10`).
- **Schedule SE**: Form 8919 wages subject to social security use up wage-base room before self-employment income. The
  Schedule SE form face (line 8c) is not in `C:\us-tax\docs\books\`, so this link is noted, not quoted.

## 11. Worked example

Robin Example (000-00-0011) and Casey Example (000-00-0012) file jointly.

| Person | Firm | Code | Column (f) | Other facts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Robin | Bright Signs LLC (EIN 00-0000001) | G (Form SS-8 filed March 2026, no reply) | $18,400.60 | Received a 1099-NEC for $18,400.60 |
| Robin | — | — | — | Robin also has a W-2 from Hilltop Inc.: box 1 $160,000, box 3 $165,000 |
| Casey | Delta Couriers Inc. (EIN 00-0000002) | H | $2,500.00 | W-2 box 1 $40,000 from Delta **and** a 1099-NEC $2,500 for a signing bonus |

Steps:

1. Robin's Form 8919: one line, code G, column (f) $18,400.60 → **line 6 = $18,400.60**.
2. Casey's Form 8919: one line, code H, column (e) checked, column (f) $2,500.00 → **line 6 = $2,500.00**. Casey's
   $40,000 W-2 stays on line 1a.
3. **Form 1040 line 1g** = $18,400.60 + $2,500.00 = $20,900.60 → rounded **$20,901**.
4. Tax, for illustration (to Schedule 2 line 6, not line 1g):
   - Robin: line 8 = $165,000; line 9 = $176,100 − $165,000 = $11,100; line 10 = smaller of $18,400.60 and $11,100 =
     $11,100; line 11 = $688.20 → $688; line 12 = $18,400.60 × 0.0145 = $266.81 → $267; line 13 = $955.
   - Casey: line 8 = $40,000 (box 3); line 9 = $136,100; line 10 = $2,500; line 11 = $155; line 12 = $36.25 → $36;
     line 13 = $191.
   - Schedule 2 line 6 = $955 + $191 = **$1,146**.
5. Both 1099-NEC amounts are **not** reported again on Schedule C — they are wages here.
