# Form 1040 line 1h — Other earned income

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\1h.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\1h.md`.

---

## 1. At a glance

Form 1040 line 1h collects pay-like income that is taxed as wages but is **not** in box 1 of a Form W-2 and has no
line of its own among lines 1a–1g. The 2025 instructions name four kinds that "must be included in the total on
line 1h": strike or lockout benefits, excess elective deferrals, disability pensions received before the plan's
minimum retirement age, and corrective distributions of excess deferrals / excess contributions from an employer
plan. Other IRS instructions send two more amounts here: government moving payments that exceed the moving costs
(Form 3903) and, in the IRS e-file specification, compensation from a foreign employer that issues no W-2. Line 1h
has an "enter type" write-in space next to it; a disability pension with a Public Safety Officer (PSO) premium
exclusion also needs "PSO" and the excluded amount written next to the line.

```text
Form 1040 line 1h =
    strike / lockout benefits (not bona fide gifts)
  + excess elective deferrals          (you; + spouse on a joint return; per person, over the 2025 limit,
                                        excluding the part that is designated Roth)
  + disability pensions (Form 1099-R)  paid before the employer plan's minimum retirement age
                                        (taxable amount; less any PSO premium exclusion; never from an IRA)
  + corrective distributions (Form 1099-R) of excess deferrals / excess contributions (+ earnings)
                                        that are taxable in 2025 (never from an IRA)
  + government moving payments above the moving costs, not in W-2 box 1   (Form 3903 line 5)
  + foreign-employer compensation with no W-2                              (IRS e-file "FEC")
```

Terms used below:

- **Elective deferral** — the part of pay an employee chooses to have put into a 401(k), 403(b), SARSEP, SIMPLE,
  the federal Thrift Savings Plan or a section 457(b) plan instead of being paid in cash. Pre-tax deferrals are left
  out of W-2 box 1 and shown in W-2 box 12 (codes D, E, F, G, S; designated Roth: AA, BB, EE).
- **Excess elective deferral** — the amount deferred in the year above the yearly limit. It is taxable in the year
  of the deferral even though the employer left it out of box 1.
- **Designated Roth contribution** — an elective deferral made after tax; it is already inside W-2 box 1.
- **Corrective distribution** — money a plan pays back to fix an excess (excess deferrals, or "excess
  contributions" of a highly paid employee who failed the plan's nondiscrimination test), plus the earnings on it.
  Form 1099-R shows it with code 8 (taxable in 2025) or code P (taxable in 2024 or earlier) in box 7.
- **Minimum retirement age** — "generally the age at which you can first receive a pension or annuity if you aren't
  disabled" (`p525.pdf p. 18`).
- **PSO premium exclusion** — a retired public safety officer can exclude up to $3,000 of plan distributions used
  to pay health or long-term care insurance premiums.

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 1h.** The instructions' heading is "Line 1h Other Earned Income" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf
p. 24`). The face of the 2025 form is not among the source documents; the line has a write-in space for the type
of income (the PSO instruction below refers to "the line next to line 1h").

**2025 Instructions for Form 1040 — Line 1h** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`):

> "If you received scholarship or fellowship grants that were not reported to you on Form W-2, report these amounts
> on Schedule 1, line 8r."
>
> "The following types of income must be included in the total on line 1h.
> • Strike or lockout benefits (other than bona fide gifts).
> • Excess elective deferrals. The amount deferred should be shown in box 12 of your Form W-2, and the "Retirement
> plan" box in box 13 should be checked. If the total amount you (or your spouse if filing jointly) deferred for 2025
> under all plans was more than $23,500 (excluding catch-up contributions, as explained later), include the excess
> on line 1h. This limit is generally (a) $16,500 if you have only SIMPLE plans, and (b) $26,500 for section 403(b)
> plans if you qualify for the 15-year rule in Pub. 571. Although designated Roth contributions are subject to this
> limit, don't include the excess attributable to such contributions on line 1h. They are already included as income
> in box 1 of your Form W-2."

(`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25`, continuing):

> "• A higher limit of $17,600 may apply to participants in certain SIMPLE plans. A higher limit may also apply to
> participants in section 457(b) deferred compensation plans for the 3 years before retirement age. …
> • If you were age 50 or older at the end of 2025, your employer may have allowed an additional deferral (catch-up
> contributions) of up to $7,500 (generally, $3,500 for section 401(k)(11) and SIMPLE plans). If you were age 60 to
> 63 at the end of 2025, your employer may have allowed a catch-up contribution of up to $11,250 ($5,250 for section
> 401(k)(11) and SIMPLE plans). This additional deferral amount isn't subject to the overall limit on elective
> deferrals.
> • A catch-up contribution limit of $3,850 may apply to certain participants in certain SIMPLE plans. …
> You can't deduct the amount deferred. It isn't included as income in box 1 of your Form W-2.
> • Disability pensions shown on Form 1099-R if you haven't reached the minimum retirement age set by your employer.
> But see Insurance Premiums for Retired Public Safety Officers in the instructions for lines 5a and 5b. Disability
> pensions received after you reach minimum retirement age and other payments shown on Form 1099-R (other than
> payments from an IRA) are reported on lines 5a and 5b. Payments from an IRA are reported on lines 4a and 4b.
> • Corrective distributions from a retirement plan shown on Form 1099-R of excess elective deferrals and excess
> contributions (plus earnings). But don't include distributions from an IRA on line 1h. Instead, report
> distributions from an IRA on lines 4a and 4b."

**Lines 5a and 5b** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28`):

> "Don't include the following payments on lines 5a and 5b. Instead, report them on line 1h.
> • Disability pensions received before you reach the minimum retirement age set by your employer.
> • Corrective distributions (including any earnings) of excess elective deferrals or other excess contributions to
> retirement plans. The plan must advise you of the year(s) the distributions are includible in income."

**Insurance Premiums for Retired Public Safety Officers** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28`, continued on `p. 30`):

> "You can exclude from income the smaller of the amount of the premiums paid or $3,000. … If you are retired on
> disability and reporting your disability pension on line 1h, include only the taxable amount on that line and
> enter "PSO" and the amount excluded on the line next to line 1h."

**Pub. 525 (2025)** — excess deferrals (`p525.pdf p. 10`):

> "You must include the excess deferral in your income for the year of the deferral. File Form 1040 or 1040-SR to
> add the excess deferral amount to earned income on line 1h."
>
> "If you take out the excess after the year of the deferral and you receive the corrective distribution by April 15
> of the following year, don't include it in income again in the year you receive it. If you receive it later, you
> must include it in income in both the year of the deferral and the year you receive it (unless the excess deferral
> was a designated Roth contribution). Any income on the excess deferral taken out is taxable in the tax year in
> which you take it out."
>
> "• If the distribution was for a 2025 excess deferral, your Form 1099-R should have code 8 in box 7. Add the excess
> deferral amount to your wages on your 2025 tax return.
> • If the distribution was for a 2025 excess deferral to a designated Roth account, your Form 1099-R should have
> codes B and 8 in box 7. Don't add this amount to your wages on your 2025 return.
> • If the distribution was for a 2024 excess deferral, your Form 1099-R should have code P in box 7. If you didn't
> add the excess deferral amount to your wages on your 2024 tax return, you must file an amended return on Form
> 1040-X. If you didn't receive the distribution by April 15, 2025, you must also add it to your wages on your 2025
> tax return.
> • If the distribution was for the income earned on an excess deferral, your Form 1099-R should have code 8 in
> box 7. Add the income amount to your wages on your 2025 income tax return, regardless of when the excess deferral
> was made."
>
> "Report a loss on a corrective distribution of an excess deferral in the year the excess amount (reduced by the
> loss) is distributed to you. Include the loss as a negative amount on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8z, and
> identify it as "Loss on Excess Deferral Distribution.""
>
> Excess contributions: "If you contributed more to the plan than allowed, the excess contributions may be
> distributed to you. You must include the distribution in your income on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 1h."

**Pub. 525** — overall limit (`p525.pdf p. 9`): "For 2025, you shouldn't have deferred more than a total of
$23,500 of contributions to the plans listed in (1) through (3) [401(k), TSP, SARSEP] … Amounts you defer under a
SIMPLE plan count toward the overall limit ($23,500 for 2025)". The 457 plan limit is separate: "the lesser of your
includible compensation or $23,500 in 2025".

**Pub. 525** — disability pensions (`p525.pdf p. 17–18`): "If you retired on disability, you must include in income
any disability pension you receive under a plan that is paid for by your employer. You must report your taxable
disability payments on line 1h of Form 1040 or 1040-SR until you reach minimum retirement age."

**Pub. 525** — strike benefits (`p525.pdf p. 36`): "Benefits paid to you by a union as strike or lockout benefits,
including both cash and the FMV of other property, are usually included in your income as compensation. You can
exclude these benefits from your income only when the facts clearly show that the union intended them as gifts to
you."

**Form 3903 instructions (2025)** (`i3903.pdf p. 1–2`): "if any reimbursements or allowances (other than dislocation
allowances, temporary lodging expenses, temporary lodging allowances, or move-in housing allowances) exceed the cost
of moving and the excess isn't included in your wages on Form W-2, the excess still must be included in gross income
on Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 1h." Line 5: "Subtract line 3 from line 4 and, if the result is more than
zero, enter it on Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 1h."

**IRS e-file specification, Pub. 4164 (processing year 2026)** (`p4164.pdf p. 235`): "The FECRecord.xsd file
supports entries on Form 1040, Line 1h or Form 1040-NR, Line 1h for Foreign Employer Compensation (FEC) received from
non-U.S. employers who do not have EINs or issue Form W2s. The FEC Record: ■ Must be attached when the "FEC" literal
is included in the Wages Not Shown Schedule".

## 3. What goes on this line

- **Strike or lockout benefits** paid by a union, in cash or property, unless the facts clearly show the union meant
  them as gifts (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`; `p525.pdf p. 36`). They may be reported on Form 1099-MISC box 3 or not
  at all.
- **Excess elective deferrals** for 2025: per person, the 2025 deferrals to all plans above the limit (section 6),
  but not the part of the excess that is designated Roth (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24–25`). The excess is 2025 income
  whether or not it is paid back, and whenever it is paid back (`p525.pdf p. 10`).
- **Disability pensions** shown on Form 1099-R from an employer plan, received before the plan's minimum retirement
  age — the taxable amount, less any PSO premium exclusion (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25, p. 28/30`; `p525.pdf p. 18`).
  Form 1099-R normally carries code 3 ("Disability") in box 7 (`i1099r--2025.pdf p. 19`).
- **Corrective distributions** shown on Form 1099-R (employer plan, not an IRA) of excess deferrals, excess
  contributions and excess aggregate contributions, with their earnings, that are taxable in 2025 — box 7 code 8
  (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25, p. 28`; `i1099r--2025.pdf p. 7–8, p. 19`; `p525.pdf p. 10`).
- **A code P distribution of a 2024 excess deferral received after April 15, 2025** — taxable in both years, so it
  is also added to 2025 wages (`p525.pdf p. 10`).
- **Government moving payments above the moving costs** of an active-duty Armed Forces move, not included in W-2
  box 1 (Form 3903 line 5; `i3903.pdf p. 1–2`).
- **Compensation from a foreign employer that issues no W-2** — e-filed returns mark it with the "FEC" literal and
  attach the FEC record (`p4164.pdf p. 235`). If the person claims the foreign earned income exclusion, the
  exclusion is taken on Form 2555 and Schedule 1 line 8d, not by leaving the wages off line 1h
  (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`: "You must also report earned income, such as wages and tips, from sources outside
  the United States.").

## 6. How to figure it, step by step

There is no IRS worksheet for line 1h. For each person (you, and your spouse on a joint return):

1. **Strike / lockout benefits.** Add union strike or lockout pay (cash plus the fair market value of property)
   unless the union clearly intended it as a gift (`p525.pdf p. 36`).
2. **Excess elective deferrals.**
   1. Add the person's 2025 elective deferrals to 401(k), 403(b), SARSEP, TSP and SIMPLE plans, **including**
      designated Roth deferrals (W-2 box 12 codes D, E, F, S, AA, BB) (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`;
      `iw2w3--2025.pdf p. 20`: "the 2025 limit for elective deferrals and designated Roth contributions is
      $23,500").
   2. Figure the limit: $23,500; $16,500 if the person has only SIMPLE plans; up to $26,500 for a 403(b) under the
      15-year rule; a plan-confirmed higher SIMPLE limit of $17,600. Add the catch-up the person was allowed: age 50+
      at the end of 2025 up to $7,500 ($3,500 SIMPLE / 401(k)(11)), age 60–63 up to $11,250 ($5,250); some SIMPLE
      plans $3,850 (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24–25`).
   3. Excess = total − limit (not below 0). Leave out the part of the excess attributable to designated Roth
      deferrals (it is already in W-2 box 1). The instructions do not give an ordering rule for that split; the plan's
      correction (code 8 versus codes B and 8) shows which deferrals were returned.
   4. Do the same separately for section 457(b) deferrals (codes G, EE) against the 457 limit
      (`p525.pdf p. 9–10`: lesser of includible compensation or $23,500; in the last 3 years before normal retirement
      age the plan may allow "the lesser of: 1. Twice the annual limit ($47,000 for 2025), or 2. The basic annual limit
      plus the amount of the basic limit not used in prior years").
   5. Include the excess on line 1h in 2025 even if it is paid back in 2026 (`p525.pdf p. 10`).
3. **Disability pensions.** For each non-IRA Form 1099-R that pays a disability pension, if the person had not reached
   the plan's minimum retirement age, add the taxable amount (box 2a). If the person is a retired public safety
   officer who elects the premium exclusion, subtract the smaller of the qualifying premiums or $3,000 (one $3,000
   limit per person for the year, shared with any exclusion on line 5b) and write "PSO" and the excluded amount next
   to line 1h (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28/30`).
4. **Corrective distributions.** For each non-IRA Form 1099-R with code 8, add the taxable amount (excess deferral
   for 2025 and/or the earnings; excess contributions plus earnings). With codes B and 8 add only the earnings. Do not
   add a code-8 excess-deferral amount that you already counted as an excess in step 2 — the excess is income once
   (`p525.pdf p. 10`: "Add the excess deferral amount to your wages on your 2025 tax return"). For code P, add it
   only if received after April 15, 2025. A loss goes to Schedule 1 line 8z, not here.
5. **Form 3903.** If the government's moving payments not in W-2 box 1 (line 4) exceed the moving costs (line 3), add
   line 4 − line 3 (`i3903.pdf p. 2`).
6. **Foreign employer without a W-2.** Add the compensation; on an e-filed return use the "FEC" literal and the FEC
   record (`p4164.pdf p. 235`).
7. **Add** the amounts of both spouses on a joint return, keeping cents, and **round** the total
   (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`: "include cents when adding the amounts and round off only the total").
8. Enter the total on **Form 1040 line 1h** and describe the types in the write-in space.

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Strike pay intended as a gift | Not income | `p525.pdf p. 36` |
| Excess partly designated Roth | Roth counts toward the limit; excess attributable to Roth not on line 1h | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24` |
| Only SIMPLE plans | Limit $16,500 (higher $17,600 in certain plans) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24–25` |
| SIMPLE plus 401(k) etc. | SIMPLE deferrals count toward the overall $23,500 | `p525.pdf p. 9` |
| 403(b) 15-year rule | Limit up to $26,500 (increase is the least of $3,000, $15,000 less prior increases, $5,000 × years − prior deferrals) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`; `p525.pdf p. 9` |
| 457(b) last 3 years before retirement age | Lesser of $47,000 or the basic limit plus unused prior-year limit; no age-50 catch-up with it | `p525.pdf p. 10` |
| Age 50+ / 60–63 catch-up | Not subject to the overall limit | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25` |
| Excess paid back by April 15, 2026 | Still 2025 income; not income again in 2026; earnings taxable when paid | `p525.pdf p. 10` |
| Excess not paid back | 2025 income now and taxed again when later distributed | `p525.pdf p. 10` |
| Code P received after April 15, 2025 | Also add to 2025 wages | `p525.pdf p. 10` |
| Loss on corrective distribution | Schedule 1 line 8z (negative) | `p525.pdf p. 10` |
| Corrective distribution | Not subject to the 10% additional tax; cannot be rolled over | `p525.pdf p. 10`; `i1099r--2025.pdf p. 7` |
| Disability pension, PSO | Taxable amount only; "PSO" and amount excluded next to line 1h | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28/30` |
| Plan without disability retirement | Payments are a pension (lines 5a / 5b) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30` |
| IRA payment with code 3 or code 8 | Lines 4a / 4b, never line 1h | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25` |
| Foreign employer, no W-2 | Line 1h, "FEC" on e-file | `p4164.pdf p. 235` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** line 1h includes both spouses' amounts. The deferral limit, catch-up and PSO $3,000 cap are
  **per person** ("If the total amount you (or your spouse if filing jointly) deferred…", `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 24`).
- **Separate returns:** each spouse reports only their own W-2 deferrals, own Forms 1099-R and own strike pay.
- A dependent's own strike pay, disability pension or excess deferral is the dependent's income, never the parent's
  (`p17.pdf p. 10`, the rule for a child's earnings).

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Form 1040 line 1z** = lines 1a through 1h → line 9 → line 11 (AGI).
- **Earned income** figures that start from line 1z: EIC Worksheet, Step 5 line 1 "Enter the amount from Form 1040 or
  1040-SR, line 1z" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42`); Form 2441 lines 4 / 5 "The amount shown on Form 1040 … line 1z,
  minus any amount … Excluded as foreign earned income … on Form 2555, line 43" (`i2441.pdf p. 4`).
- A disability pension before minimum retirement age is earned income for the EIC (`p525.pdf p. 18`).

## 11. Worked example

Dana Sample (000-00-0001, age 45) and Riley Sample (000-00-0002, age 48) file jointly.

| Document | Facts |
|---|---|
| Dana — W-2 from Harbor Labs | Box 12 D $20,000 |
| Dana — W-2 from North Pier Co. (second job) | Box 12 D $6,000 |
| Dana — Form 1099-R from Harbor Labs 401(k), box 7 code 8, not IRA | Box 2a $1,200: excess **contributions** (failed nondiscrimination test) plus earnings |
| Riley — Form 1099-R from City Transit pension, box 7 code 3, not IRA | Box 2a $9,000; plan minimum retirement age 55 |
| Riley — Form 1099-R from a traditional IRA, box 7 code 3 | Box 2a $4,000 |
| Riley — Form 1099-MISC box 3 from the drivers' union | $1,800 strike benefits (not a gift) |

Steps:

1. Strike benefits: Riley $1,800.
2. Excess deferrals: Dana's deferrals $20,000 + $6,000 = $26,000; limit $23,500 (age 45, no catch-up) →
   excess **$2,500** (the plan will return it by April 15, 2026 on a 2026 Form 1099-R code P; it is still 2025
   income). Riley has none.
3. Disability pension: Riley is 48, under 55 → **$9,000**. The IRA payment with code 3 goes to lines 4a / 4b.
4. Corrective distribution: Dana's code-8 excess contributions plus earnings **$1,200** (not the same money as the
   $2,500 excess deferral).
5. Total: $1,800 + $2,500 + $9,000 + $1,200 = **$14,500 → Form 1040 line 1h**, with the types written next to the
   line. Line 1z includes it.
