# Form 1040 line 1i — Nontaxable combat pay election

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\1i.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\1i.md`.

---

## 1. At a glance

Pay that a member of the U.S. Armed Forces receives for service in a combat zone is excluded from income, so it is
**not** in W-2 box 1 and not on line 1a. The employer reports it in W-2 box 12 with code Q. For the earned income
credit (EIC) a service member may **elect** to treat this pay as earned income. Line 1i is where that election is
shown: if you elect, you enter **all** of your nontaxable combat pay on line 1i. The amount is a memo figure — it is
printed in the inner column of the form and is **not** added into line 1z, total income or adjusted gross income.

```text
Form 1040 line 1i =
      (all of your W-2 box 12 code Q,           if YOU elect)
    + (all of your spouse's W-2 box 12 code Q,  if a joint return and YOUR SPOUSE elects)
    (blank if nobody elects; not part of line 1z)
```

Terms used below:

- **Nontaxable combat pay** — military pay excluded from gross income because it was earned while serving in a combat
  zone (Pub. 3). Shown in W-2 box 12, code Q.
- **EIC (earned income credit)** — the refundable credit on Form 1040 line 27a. It grows with earned income up to a
  plateau and then shrinks, so adding combat pay can raise **or lower** the credit.
- **Election** — a choice the filer makes; here it is "all or nothing" per person.

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), page 1:** "i Nontaxable combat pay election (see instructions) . . . 1i", printed in the inner
column, directly above "z Add lines 1a through 1h" (form face, `docs\IRS-Forms\f1040.pdf` p. 1).

**2025 Instructions for Form 1040 — Line 1i** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25`):

> "Nontaxable Combat Pay Election. If you elect to include your nontaxable combat pay in your earned income when
> figuring the EIC, enter the amount on line 1i. See the instructions for line 27a."

**EIC, Step 5 — Earned Income worksheet** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42`):

> "1. Enter the amount from Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 1z … 4. Enter all of your nontaxable combat pay if you elect
> to include it in earned income. Also enter the amount of your nontaxable combat pay on line 1i of Form 1040 or
> 1040-SR. See Combat pay, nontaxable, later … 5. Add lines 3 and 4. This is your earned income"
>
> "Electing to include nontaxable combat pay may increase or decrease your EIC. Figure the credit with and without
> your nontaxable combat pay before making the election."

**Combat pay, nontaxable** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 43`):

> "If you were a member of the U.S. Armed Forces who served in a combat zone, certain pay is excluded from your
> income. See Combat Zone Exclusion in Pub. 3. You can elect to include this pay in your earned income when figuring
> the EIC. The amount of your nontaxable combat pay should be shown in box 12 of Form(s) W-2 with code Q. If you are
> filing a joint return and both you and your spouse received nontaxable combat pay, you can each make your own
> election. In other words, if one of you makes the election, the other one can also make it but doesn't have to.
> If you elect to use your nontaxable combat pay in figuring your EIC, enter that amount on line 1i."

**Credit figured by the IRS** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 43`): "2. Be sure you enter the nontaxable combat pay you elect
to include in earned income by entering that amount on line 1i."

**W-2 code Q** (General Instructions for Forms W-2 and W-3 (2025), `iw2w3--2025.pdf p. 21`): "Code Q—Nontaxable
combat pay. If you are a military employer, report any nontaxable combat pay in box 12."

## 3. What goes on this line

- The **whole** nontaxable combat pay (W-2 box 12 code Q) of each person who elects. The instructions say "Enter all
  of your nontaxable combat pay" — there is no partial election (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42`).
- On a joint return, each spouse decides separately; line 1i holds the combat pay of whichever spouse(s) elected
  (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 43`).

## 4. What does not go here

| Look-alike amount | Where it goes instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Taxable military pay (in W-2 box 1) | Form 1040 line 1a | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23` |
| Nontaxable combat pay of a person who does **not** elect | Nowhere on page 1; still counted where the law always counts it (below) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42–43` |
| Combat pay for the additional child tax credit (ACTC) | Schedule 8812 line 18b — **always all** combat pay, election or not | `i1040s8.pdf p. 3`, `p. 8` |
| Combat pay for the child and dependent care credit / benefits exclusion | Form 2441 lines 4–5 and 18–19 — a **separate** election made there | `i2441.pdf p. 4–5` |
| Combat pay as IRA compensation | IRA Deduction Worksheet line 8 — **always** counted | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 95, p. 98`; `p590a.pdf p. 7` |
| Combat pay in the Schedule A optional sales tax table income | Schedule A sales tax worksheet income — always counted | `i1040sca_2025.pdf p. 5` |
| Combat-zone service as such (deadline extension) | The "Combat zone" box at the top of Form 1040 (not an amount) | form face, `docs\IRS-Forms\f1040.pdf` p. 1 |

## 5. Where the numbers come from

| Source document / form | Box or line | How it enters line 1i |
|---|---|---|
| Form W-2 | Box a (employee SSN) | Decides whose combat pay it is |
| Form W-2 | Box 12, code Q | The amount (all of it, per electing person) |
| Filer's choice | Election, per person | Yes → the person's whole code Q total; No → nothing |

## 6. How to figure it, step by step

1. For each person on the return (you; your spouse only on a joint return), add every W-2 box 12 code Q amount
   issued to that person (box a).
2. Figure the EIC both with and without that person's combat pay (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42` caution).
3. If the person elects, carry their **whole** code Q total to EIC Step 5 line 4 and to line 1i. If not, nothing.
4. Line 1i = total for the electing person(s). Round to whole dollars if the return is rounded
   (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`).
5. Do **not** add line 1i into line 1z (line 1z = "Add lines 1a through 1h").

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Both spouses received combat pay (joint return) | Each makes their own election | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 43` |
| Election lowers the EIC | Allowed but usually not wanted — figure both ways | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42` |
| IRS figures the EIC | Still enter the elected amount on line 1i | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 43` |
| Schedule 8812 (ACTC) | All combat pay counts, whether or not elected for the EIC; "This amount will be reported either on line 1i … or should be shown in Form W-2, box 12, with code Q" | `i1040s8.pdf p. 3, p. 8` |
| Form 2441 | Separate election; "even if you elect not to include it in earned income for the earned income credit (EIC)" | `i2441.pdf p. 4, p. 5` |
| IRA deduction | "If you were a member of the U.S. Armed Forces, earned income includes any nontaxable combat pay you received" | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 95`; `p590a.pdf p. 7` |
| Credit figured with Schedule 8812 Credit Limit Worksheet B | Earned income from Worksheet B "plus all of your nontaxable combat pay if you did not elect to include it in earned income for the EIC" | `i1040s8.pdf p. 7` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** two separate elections; line 1i is the sum of the electing spouses' code Q.
- **Separate returns:** only your own combat pay. (The EIC itself is generally not allowed on a separate return; the
  limited exception follows the EIC rules on line 27a.)
- **Single / head of household / qualifying surviving spouse:** only your own combat pay.

## 9. Forms to attach or keep

No form is attached for line 1i. The W-2 (code Q) is attached to a paper return as usual. Schedule EIC is attached
when the EIC is claimed with a qualifying child (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 43`).

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **EIC (Form 1040 line 27a):** Step 5 line 4 / Worksheet B earned income (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 42`; Worksheet B line 4a "Enter your earned income from Step 5", `p. 47`).
- **Schedule 8812 line 18b** may show the same amount, but there all combat pay counts regardless of the election.
- **Not** into line 1z, line 9, line 11 or taxable income.

## 11. Worked example

Sam Example (000-00-0001) and Riley Example (000-00-0002) file jointly with one qualifying child.

| W-2 | Box 1 | Box 12 code Q |
|---|---|---|
| Sam — Department of the Army | $14,000.00 | $9,000.00 |
| Riley — Department of the Navy | $20,000.00 | $4,000.00 |

1. Sam's code Q total $9,000; Riley's $4,000.
2. They figure the EIC both ways. Earned income without combat pay is $34,000, already in the range where the
   credit for a joint return with one child shrinks as income rises (EIC Table, `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf`). Adding either spouse's combat pay pushes earned income further into the
   phase-out and lowers the credit, so neither elects. **Line 1i is blank.**
3. If instead their wages were $5,000 and $3,000 (earned income $8,000, in the phase-in range), adding Sam's $9,000
   raises earned income to $17,000 and the credit reaches its maximum. Sam elects; Riley's $4,000 would change
   nothing, and Riley does not elect → **line 1i = $9,000**.
4. In either case Schedule 8812 line 18b = $13,000 (all combat pay of both spouses), and line 1z is unchanged.

## 12. 2025 amounts used

Line 1i has no dollar thresholds. Rounding: under 50 cents dropped, 50–99 cents up (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`).
