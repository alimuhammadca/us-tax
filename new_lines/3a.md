# Form 1040 line 3a — Qualified dividends

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\3a.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\3a.md`. Companion lines: `3b.md` (ordinary dividends — line 3a is a part of it) and
`3c.md` (the child-dividends checkbox).

---

## 1. At a glance

Form 1040 line 3a is the part of your ordinary dividends (line 3b) that qualifies for the lower tax rates that
apply to net capital gain (0%, 15% or 20%). It usually starts from box 1b of each Form 1099-DIV and the qualified
dividends on Schedules K-1, but some amounts a payer shows in box 1b are **not** qualified in your hands — the
dividends you received as a nominee, dividends on stock you did not hold long enough, dividends offset by related
payments (short sales), payments in lieu of dividends you knew were not qualified, and dividends from a surrogate
foreign corporation. If you elect on Form 8814 to report your child's interest and dividends, Form 8814 line 9 is
added here (and to line 3b), and box 1 on line 3c is checked. Line 3a is **not** added into total income — it is
already inside line 3b. Its job is to decide how the tax on line 16 is figured.

```text
Form 1040 line 3a =
    Σ Form 1099-DIV box 1b                (your forms; + your spouse's on a joint return)
  + Σ Schedule K-1 qualified dividends    (Form 1065 box 6b, Form 1120-S box 5b, Form 1041 box 2b), less PTEP
  + qualified dividends with no Form 1099-DIV
  − box 1b amounts that are not qualified dividends:
        nominee dividends; holding period not met (61 of 121 days; preferred 91 of 181 days);
        related-payment (short-sale) obligations; payments in lieu known to be nonqualified;
        surrogate foreign corporation dividends
  + Form 8814 line 9 for each child whose income you elect to report     (check line 3c box 1)
  and never more than Form 1040 line 3b
```

Terms used below:

- **Qualified dividend** — an ordinary dividend from a domestic corporation or a qualified foreign corporation that
  meets the holding-period rule and is not on the exception list (`i1099div.pdf p. 2`).
- **Ex-dividend date** — "the first date following the declaration of a dividend on which the purchaser of a stock
  isn't entitled to receive the next dividend payment" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 26`).
- **Qualified foreign corporation** — a foreign corporation incorporated in a U.S. possession, or eligible for a
  comprehensive U.S. income tax treaty with an information-exchange program, or (for that dividend) whose stock is
  readily tradable on an established U.S. securities market; never a PFIC (passive foreign investment company) or a
  post-2017 surrogate foreign corporation (`i1099div.pdf p. 2`).
- **Surrogate foreign corporation** — a foreign parent created in a corporate inversion (section 7874).

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 3a:** "Qualified dividends" (label from the instructions and the semantic field map
`pdfs\f1040_field_mapping_semantic.csv`, field `line3a_qualified_dividends`; the 2025 form itself is not in
`docs\books`).

**2025 Instructions for Form 1040 — Line 3a** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25–26`):

> "Enter your total qualified dividends on line 3a. Qualified dividends are also included in the ordinary dividend
> total required to be shown on line 3b. Qualified dividends are eligible for a lower tax rate than other ordinary
> income. Generally, these dividends are shown in box 1b of Form(s) 1099-DIV. If you are including your child's
> qualified dividends in the total on line 3a, check box 1 on line 3c. For more information, see the Instructions
> for Form 8814. See Pub. 550 for the definition of qualified dividends if you received dividends not reported on
> Form 1099-DIV."

> "Exception. Some dividends may be reported as qualified dividends in box 1b of Form 1099-DIV but aren't qualified
> dividends. These include the following.
> • Dividends you received as a nominee. See the Schedule B instructions.
> • Dividends you received on any share of stock that you held for less than 61 days during the 121-day period that
> began 60 days before the ex-dividend date. … When counting the number of days you held the stock, include the day
> you disposed of the stock but not the day you acquired it. … Also, when counting the number of days you held the
> stock, you can't count certain days during which your risk of loss was diminished.
> • Dividends attributable to periods totaling more than 366 days that you received on any share of preferred stock
> held for less than 91 days during the 181-day period that began 90 days before the ex-dividend date. … Preferred
> dividends attributable to periods totaling less than 367 days are subject to the 61-day holding period rule just
> described.
> • Dividends on any share of stock to the extent that you are under an obligation (including a short sale) to make
> related payments with respect to positions in substantially similar or related property.
> • Payments in lieu of dividends, but only if you know or have reason to know that the payments aren't qualified
> dividends.
> • Dividends from a corporation that first became a surrogate foreign corporation after December 22, 2017, other
> than a foreign corporation that is treated as a domestic corporation under section 7874(b)."

The instructions then give three examples (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 26`) — for instance "Example 1. You bought 5,000
shares of XYZ Corp. common stock on July 8. … The ex-dividend date was July 16. … you sold the 5,000 shares on
August 11. You held your shares of XYZ Corp. for only 34 days of the 121-day period … You have no qualified
dividends from XYZ Corp." — and end: "Use the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet or the Schedule D
Tax Worksheet, whichever applies, to figure your tax."

**What box 1b holds** (`i1099div.pdf p. 4`):

> "Box 1b. Qualified Dividends. Enter the portion of the dividends in box 1a that qualifies for the reduced capital
> gains rates. Include dividends for which it is impractical to determine if the section 1(h)(11)(B)(iii) holding
> period requirement has been met." … "Qualified dividends cannot be less than zero."

Box 6 (`i1099div.pdf p. 5`): "Do not include any investment expenses in box 1b."

**Payer's exception list** (`i1099div.pdf p. 2`) adds: "Dividends paid by a regulated investment company (RIC) that
are not treated as qualified dividend income under section 854", "Dividends paid by a real estate investment trust
(REIT) that are not treated as qualified dividend income under section 857(c)", and "Deductible dividends paid on
employer securities" (section 404(k), also `p. 2`: "these dividends are not eligible for the reduced capital gains
rates"). These the payer leaves out of box 1b.

**Schedule K-1 qualified dividends:** partnership box 6b "Report any qualified dividends on Form 1040 or 1040-SR,
line 3a" and leave out the PTEP part (`i1065sk1.pdf p. 15`); S corporation box 5b, same (`i1120ssk.pdf p. 7`);
estate or trust box 2b "This amount is reported on line 3a of Form 1040 or 1040-SR" (`i1041sk1.pdf p. 2`).

**Child's qualified dividends** (`i8814.pdf p. 3`):

> "Enter the child's qualified dividends on Form 8814, line 2b. But don't include this amount on the parent's Form
> 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 3a. Instead, include the amount from Form 8814, line 9, on Form 1040, 1040-SR, or
> 1040-NR, line 3a. (The amount on Form 8814, line 9, may be less than the amount on Form 8814, line 2b, because
> lines 7 through 12 of the form divide the $2,700 base amount on Form 8814, line 5, between the child's qualified
> dividends, capital gain distributions, and other interest and dividend income, reducing each of those amounts.)"

**Form 4952 line 4g election does not change line 3a** (`f4952--2025.pdf p. 4`):

> "Don't reduce the amount of qualified dividends on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 3a (or Form 1041, line 2b(2)), by
> any part of the amount on line 4g."

## 3. What goes on this line

- Box 1b of every Form 1099-DIV issued to you (and your spouse on a joint return), less the exceptions in section 4.
- Qualified dividends on Schedule K-1: Form 1065 box 6b, Form 1120-S box 5b, Form 1041 box 2b, less PTEP.
- Qualified dividends with no Form 1099-DIV (judged under Pub. 550's definition — Pub. 550 is not in `docs\books`;
  the definition used here is the one in `i1099div.pdf p. 2`).
- Form 8814 line 9 of each child whose income you elect to report.

## 6. How to figure it, step by step

1. **Start with the same statements as line 3b** (yours; plus your spouse's on a joint return).
2. **Add box 1b** of each Form 1099-DIV and the K-1 qualified dividends (6b / 5b / 2b), leaving out PTEP.
3. **Add qualified dividends that had no Form 1099-DIV.**
4. **Take out the exceptions** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 26`):
   a. nominee dividends;
   b. common stock: for each dividend, count the days you held the share in the 121-day window that starts 60 days
      before the ex-dividend date (count the sale day, not the purchase day; skip days your risk of loss was
      diminished). Under 61 days → not qualified;
   c. preferred stock with dividends for periods over 366 days: under 91 days of the 181-day window starting 90 days
      before the ex-dividend date → not qualified (other preferred dividends use step b);
   d. dividends you must pay back as related payments (short sales);
   e. payments in lieu of dividends you know or should know are not qualified;
   f. dividends from a post-2017 surrogate foreign corporation;
   g. dividends from a foreign corporation that is not a qualified foreign corporation (`i1099div.pdf p. 2`).
5. **Add Form 8814 line 9** for each elected child (line 9 = (line 4 − $2,700) × line 2b ÷ line 4, line 4 being the
   child's taxable interest + ordinary dividends + capital gain distributions; nothing when line 4 is $2,700 or
   less) (`i8814.pdf p. 3–4`). Check line 3c box 1.
6. **Check the result is not more than line 3b** — qualified dividends "are also included in the ordinary dividend
   total" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25`), so line 3a can never exceed line 3b.
7. **Round** to whole dollars if you round the return (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`).
8. Enter on **Form 1040 line 3a**. Do not reduce it for a Form 4952 line 4g election (`f4952--2025.pdf p. 4`).

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Holding period (common) | 61 days in the 121-day window around the ex-dividend date; examples 1–3 | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 26` |
| Holding period (preferred, periods over 366 days) | 91 days in the 181-day window | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 26` |
| Payer could not tell the holding period | Payer includes the dividend in box 1b anyway; you must apply the rule | `i1099div.pdf p. 4` |
| Mutual-fund dividends | The fund reports the qualified part (e.g. 2 cents of 10 cents a share); your own holding period still applies — Example 3 | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 26` |
| Form 8814 child | Only line 9 goes on line 3a; check line 3c box 1 | `i8814.pdf p. 3–4` |
| Form 4952 line 4g election | Line 3a is not reduced; the elected amount is taken out inside the Schedule D Tax Worksheet (line 3), and the Schedule D Tax Worksheet must be used | `f4952--2025.pdf p. 4`; `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 36` |
| Foreign tax credit | Foreign-source qualified dividends may need an adjustment on Form 1116 (see the Form 1116 docs) | `i1065sk1.pdf p. 15` ("Caution: If you have any foreign source qualified …") |
| Kiddie tax child's own return | The child reports his or her own qualified dividends on the child's line 3a; Form 8615 uses them | `p17.pdf p. 57` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** both spouses' qualified dividends, each spouse's exceptions taken out. No per-person limit.
- **Separate returns:** each spouse's own statements; in a community property state the community share
  (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`).
- The line 3a ≤ line 3b rule holds for the return as a whole.

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Line 16:** "Use the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet … if … You reported qualified dividends on
  Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 3a" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 36`); worksheet line 2 = "the amount from Form 1040 or
  1040-SR, line 3a" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 38`); the Schedule D Tax Worksheet when Schedule D or Form 4952 line 4g
  requires it.
- Form 4952 (qualified dividends are excluded from investment income unless elected on line 4g), Form 6251 (AMT
  Part III), Form 8615 on a child's own return, Form 1116 adjustments.
- **Not** to line 9 — line 3b carries the money into total income.

## 11. Worked example

Same household as `3b.md`: Pat and Lee Sample, joint return, Form 8814 elected for their son Fred.

1. Box 1b: Pat Fund A $900; Lee Stock B $600; Lee Bank C $0 → $1,500.
2. K-1 box 6b (Pat): + $400 → $1,900.
3. No qualified dividends without a form.
4. Exceptions: Pat bought part of the Fund A shares 20 days before the ex-dividend date and sold them 14 days after
   it (34 days held, under 61) — the fund's statement shows $100 of box 1b on those shares → − $100 → $1,800. The
   Bank C nominee $150 had no box 1b amount, so nothing more.
5. Form 8814 line 9 for Fred: $78 (`i8814.pdf p. 4`). + $78 → **$1,878**. Line 3c box 1 checked.
6. Check: $1,878 ≤ line 3b $2,628.
7. **Form 1040 line 3a = $1,878.** It goes to line 2 of the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet (or
   Schedule D Tax Worksheet, since Fund A's $300 capital gain distribution and Fred's $22 go to Schedule D line 13).
