# Form 1040 line 4a — IRA distributions

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\4a.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\4a.md`. The taxable part is Form 1040 line 4b (`C:\us-tax\new_lines\4b.md`); the
checkboxes are line 4c (`C:\us-tax\new_lines\4c.md`).

---

## 1. At a glance

Form 1040 line 4a is the **gross** (total, before tax withheld) amount of the IRA distributions received in 2025 by
**you** — and, on a joint return, by **your spouse** — but only when part of it is **not** taxable or one of the IRS
"Exceptions" applies. When every IRA distribution is fully taxable and no exception applies, line 4a is left
**blank** and the whole amount goes on line 4b alone. Line 4a is a disclosure line: it is **not** added into total
income (Form 1040 line 9 adds line 4b, not line 4a).

```text
Form 1040 line 4a =
    blank                                  if every IRA distribution is fully taxable and no Exception 1-4 applies
    Σ Form 1099-R box 1 (IRA/SEP/SIMPLE)   otherwise — every IRA distribution of you (+ your spouse if MFJ),
  + amounts with no Form 1099-R that the instructions put on line 4a
      (Form 4852 line 8a for a missing / incorrect IRA 1099-R; a 2025 contribution returned by the due date with its
       earnings when the 1099-R comes in 2026; a recharacterization made in 2025)
```

Terms used below:

- **IRA** — individual retirement arrangement. For lines 4a and 4b it "includes a traditional IRA (which includes a
  traditional IRA that receives contributions from a simplified employee pension (SEP) arrangement), Roth IRA …, and a
  SIMPLE IRA" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 26`).
- **Gross distribution** — Form 1099-R box 1, "the total amount of any distribution from your IRA before income tax or
  other deductions were withheld" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 26`).
- **Rollover** — money taken out of one IRA or plan and put into another, generally within 60 days; not taxed.
- **QCD** — qualified charitable distribution: a transfer straight from the IRA trustee to a charity by an owner at
  least 70½.
- **HFD** — HSA funding distribution: a once-in-a-lifetime trustee-to-trustee transfer from an IRA to a health savings
  account.
- **Basis** — after-tax money already in the IRA (nondeductible contributions); it comes back tax-free and is tracked
  on Form 8606.

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 4a:** "IRA distributions" — next to "b Taxable amount" (`C:\us-tax\docs\IRS-Forms\f1040.pdf
p. 1`).

**2025 Instructions for Form 1040 — Lines 4a and 4b, IRA Distributions** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 26`):

> "You should receive a Form 1099-R showing the total amount of any distribution from your IRA before income tax or
> other deductions were withheld. This amount should be shown in box 1 of Form 1099-R."

(`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27`):

> "If the distribution from your IRA is fully taxable, enter the total distribution on line 4b; don't make an entry
> on line 4a."

> "Exception 1. Enter the total distribution on line 4a if you rolled over part or all of the distribution from one:
> • Roth IRA to another Roth IRA, or • IRA (other than a Roth IRA) to a qualified plan or another IRA (other than a
> Roth IRA)."

> "Exception 2. If any of the following apply, enter the total distribution on line 4a and see Form 8606 and its
> instructions to figure the amount to enter on line 4b." (six cases: nondeductible contributions to any traditional
> IRA; a Roth IRA distribution; a 2025 conversion to a Roth IRA; a 2024 or 2025 contribution returned by the due date;
> an earlier year's excess contribution returned in 2025; a recharacterization).

> "Exception 3. If all or part of the distribution is a qualified charitable distribution (QCD), enter the total
> distribution on line 4a."

> "Exception 4. If all or part of the distribution is a health savings account (HSA) funding distribution (HFD), enter
> the total distribution on line 4a."

> "More than one distribution. If you (or your spouse if filing jointly) received more than one distribution, figure
> the taxable amount of each distribution and enter the total of the taxable amounts on line 4b. Enter the total
> amount of those distributions on line 4a."

**Publication 590-B (2025)**, Reporting taxable distributions (`p590b.pdf p. 22`):

> "Report fully taxable distributions, including early distributions, on Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 4b (no
> entry is required on line 4a). If only part of the distribution is taxable, enter the total amount on Form 1040,
> 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 4a, and enter the taxable part on Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 4b."

**Instructions for Form 8606 (2025)** — amounts that go on line 4a without a taxable amount:

> Return of contributions: "you must include the amount of the distribution of the returned contributions you made in
> 2025 and any related earnings on your 2025 Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 4a. Also include the related
> earnings on … line 4b." (`i8606.pdf p. 7`)

> Recharacterization: "If the recharacterization occurred in 2025, include the amount transferred from the traditional
> IRA on 2025 Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 4a. If the recharacterization occurred in 2026, report the amount
> transferred only in the attached statement, and not on your 2025 or 2026 tax return." (`i8606.pdf p. 6`)

> Line 18: "If your entry on line 18 is zero or less, don't include the result on 2025 Form 1040 … line 4b. Include the
> full amount of the distribution on 2025 Form 1040 … line 4a." Line 19: "If … you don't have an amount to enter on
> line 19, don't complete Part III; your Roth IRA distribution(s) isn't taxable. Instead, include your total Roth IRA
> distribution(s) on 2025 Form 1040 … line 4a." (`i8606.pdf p. 13–14`)

## 3. What goes on this line

When any exception applies or any part is not taxable, the **total** of:

- Form 1099-R box 1 of every 1099-R with the "IRA/SEP/SIMPLE" box checked issued to you (and your spouse on a joint
  return) — traditional, SEP, SIMPLE and Roth IRAs, including inherited IRAs.
- Distributions rolled over (Exception 1), including a traditional-IRA amount paid directly to an employer plan
  (1099-R code G, box 2a "0 (zero)", `i1099r--2025.pdf p. 13`).
- QCDs and HFDs (Exceptions 3 and 4).
- Distributions covered by Form 8606 (Exception 2): with basis, Roth IRA distributions, Roth conversions, returned
  contributions, recharacterizations made in 2025.
- Form 4852 line 8a for an IRA 1099-R that was never received or is wrong (`f4852.pdf p. 1`).
- A 2025 contribution withdrawn in 2026 by the due date, with its earnings (no 2025 Form 1099-R) (`i8606.pdf p. 7`).

## 4. What does not go here

| Look-alike amount | Where it goes instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Fully taxable IRA distributions with no exception | Line 4b only; line 4a blank | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27` |
| Distributions from 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457(b) plans, pensions, annuities (1099-R with the IRA/SEP/SIMPLE box **not** checked) | Lines 5a / 5b | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28` |
| A dependent child's own IRA distribution (1099-R in the child's name) | The child's own return. Lines 4a/4b take only "you (or your spouse if filing jointly)" | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27` |
| Your spouse's IRA distributions on a separate return | Your spouse's own return | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27` |
| A 2024 contribution returned in 2025 (1099-R code P) | The 2024 return (original or amended): "don't report the distribution on your 2025 tax return" | `i8606.pdf p. 7`; `i1099r--2025.pdf p. 20` |
| A recharacterization made in 2026 for 2025 | Only the attached statement — "not on your 2025 or 2026 tax return" | `i8606.pdf p. 6` |
| A trustee-to-trustee transfer from one IRA to another | Not a distribution; no Form 1099-R ("Generally, do not report a transfer between trustees … including a trustee-to-trustee transfer from one IRA to another IRA") | `i1099r--2025.pdf p. 7` |
| Federal tax withheld (1099-R box 4) | Form 1040 line 25b | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf` line 25b |
| The additional 10% / 25% tax on an early distribution | Form 5329 → Schedule 2 line 8 | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27` |

| Form 1099-R | Recipient's TIN | Decides whose distribution it is: yours, your spouse's (joint only), or someone else's |
| Form 1099-R | Box 1, gross distribution | Added |
| Form 1099-R | Box 2a / 2b | Whether the distribution is fully taxable (decides blank vs filled) |
| Form 1099-R | Box 7 codes | Identify Roth (J, Q, T), direct payment to a plan (G), corrective (8, P), recharacterization (N, R), QCD (Y) (`i1099r--2025.pdf p. 19–20`) |
| Taxpayer's records | Rollover, QCD, HFD, conversion, return of contribution | Make an exception apply, so the total goes on line 4a |
| Form 4852 | Line 8a, with line 8j "IRA/SEP/SIMPLE" | Added in place of a missing / incorrect IRA 1099-R |
| Form 8606 | Lines 18 / 19 | When the Roth part is not taxable, the full distribution still goes on line 4a |

## 6. How to figure it, step by step

1. **Collect the IRA Forms 1099-R that belong on this return** — IRA/SEP/SIMPLE box checked, recipient TIN = you, or
   your spouse on a joint return.
2. **Leave out** 1099-Rs for income of another year (code P) and recharacterizations made in 2026.
3. **Decide whether any exception applies** (rollover, Form 8606 case, QCD, HFD) or whether any part is not taxable.
4. If **none** applies and every distribution is fully taxable: leave line 4a **blank** and put the total on line 4b.
5. Otherwise **add box 1** of every IRA distribution (and Form 4852 line 8a substitutes, and amounts the Form 8606
   instructions put on line 4a). Keep cents; round only the total (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`).
6. Enter the total on **Form 1040 line 4a**. Line 4b is figured separately (`new_lines\4b.md`).

There is no IRS worksheet for line 4a.

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** line 4a combines both spouses' IRA distributions ("If you (or your spouse if filing jointly)
  received more than one distribution", `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27`). Line 4a is blank only if **every** distribution of
  both spouses is fully taxable with no exception.
- **Separate returns:** only your own 1099-Rs. IRAs are individual accounts (`i8606.pdf p. 2`: an IRA is "for the
  exclusive benefit of you or your beneficiaries"), so they are not split as community income.
- No limit or cap applies to line 4a itself.

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- Nowhere in the arithmetic: line 9 adds line 4b, not line 4a (`f1040.pdf p. 1`: "Add lines 1z, 2b, 3b, 4b, 5b, 6b,
  7a, and 8").
- It is matched by the IRS against Forms 1099-R and tells the reader that the difference between 4a and 4b is explained
  by line 4c / Form 8606.

## 11. Worked example

Pat Example (000-00-0011, age 74) and Robin Example (000-00-0012, age 60) file jointly.

| Document | Box 1 | Box 2a | Box 7 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pat — traditional IRA 1099-R | $20,000 | $20,000 | 7, Y | $5,000 of it went straight to a charity (QCD) |
| Robin — traditional IRA 1099-R | $8,000 | $8,000 | 7 | Ordinary withdrawal, no basis |
| Their son Lee (000-00-0013) — IRA 1099-R | $1,500 | $1,500 | 1 | Lee's own return |

1. Pat's and Robin's 1099-Rs are on this return; Lee's is not.
2. Pat's QCD is Exception 3, so line 4a cannot be blank.
3. Line 4a = $20,000 + $8,000 = **$28,000** (line 4b = $15,000 + $8,000 = $23,000; line 4c box 2 checked).

Had there been no QCD, both distributions would be fully taxable: line 4a blank, line 4b $28,000.
