# Form 1040 line 4b — IRA distributions, taxable amount

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\4b.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\4b.md`. The gross amount is line 4a (`new_lines\4a.md`); the checkboxes are line 4c
(`new_lines\4c.md`). Form 8606 and Form 5329 get their own files; they are covered here only as far as they produce
line 4b.

---

## 1. At a glance

Form 1040 line 4b is the part of the 2025 IRA distributions of **you** (and, on a joint return, **your spouse**) that
is taxable. It starts from each distribution and takes out, in this order of thinking:

- what was **rolled over** (Exception 1),
- what is a **QCD** (Exception 3, up to $108,000 a person, reduced by deductible IRA contributions made at 70½ or
  older),
- what is an excluded **HFD** (Exception 4),
- and, when Form 8606 is required (Exception 2), the **nontaxable part** figured on Form 8606: recovered **basis**
  (Part I), the nontaxable part of a **Roth conversion** (Part II) and the tax-free part of a nonqualified **Roth IRA
  distribution** (Part III).

Line 4b is added into total income (Form 1040 line 9).

```text
For each person, then added together:

  no exception, no basis              taxable = Σ 1099-R box 1   (fully taxable; line 4a blank)

  exceptions 1, 3, 4 only, no basis   taxable = Σ distributions − rollovers − excludable QCD − excluded HFD   (≥ 0)

  Exception 2 (Form 8606)             taxable = Form 8606 line 15c   (traditional/SEP/SIMPLE distributions, Part I)
                                              + Form 8606 line 18    (2025 Roth conversions, Part II)
                                              + Form 8606 line 25c   (nonqualified Roth distributions, Part III)
                                              + the taxable part of any distribution Form 8606 does not cover
                                                (for example, returned-contribution earnings; an inherited IRA
                                                 without basis)
                                    with Form 8606 line 7 already leaving out rollovers, QCDs, HFDs, conversions,
                                    returned contributions and recharacterizations
  + Form 8915-F amounts for qualified disaster distributions (taxable in 2025 under its rules)

Form 1040 line 4b = Σ of the persons' taxable amounts, rounded
```

Terms used below:

- **Traditional IRA** — any IRA that is not a Roth IRA, including traditional SEP and SIMPLE IRAs (`i8606.pdf p. 1`).
- **Basis** — "the total of all your nondeductible contributions and your nontaxable amounts included in rollovers
  made to these IRAs minus the total of all your nontaxable distributions" (`i8606.pdf p. 3`).
- **Pro-rata rule** — with basis, every non-excluded traditional IRA distribution is part basis, part taxable, in the
  ratio of total basis to (year-end value + distributions + conversions) — Form 8606 lines 6–10.
- **Qualified Roth distribution** — made after the 5-year period and on or after age 59½, after death, for
  disability or for a first home (`i8606.pdf p. 3`); not taxable.
- **Roth ordering** — a nonqualified Roth distribution comes first from regular contributions, then conversions and
  rollovers (oldest first, taxable part first), then earnings (`p590b.pdf p. 35`).
- **QCD, HFD, rollover** — as defined in `new_lines\4a.md`.
- **Recharacterization** — moving a contribution (plus its earnings) to the other type of IRA by trustee-to-trustee
  transfer; it is treated as made to the second IRA (`i8606.pdf p. 6`).

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 4b:** "b Taxable amount" (`C:\us-tax\docs\IRS-Forms\f1040.pdf p. 1`).

**2025 Instructions for Form 1040, Lines 4a and 4b** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27`):

> "If the distribution from your IRA is fully taxable, enter the total distribution on line 4b; don't make an entry
> on line 4a."

> Exception 1 (rollover): "If the total distribution was rolled over, enter -0- on line 4b. If the total distribution
> wasn't rolled over, enter the part not rolled over on line 4b unless Exception 2 applies to the part not rolled
> over. Generally, a rollover must be made within 60 days after the day you received the distribution."

> Exception 2: "If any of the following apply, enter the total distribution on line 4a and see Form 8606 and its
> instructions to figure the amount to enter on line 4b. 1. You received a distribution from an IRA (other than a
> Roth IRA) and you made nondeductible contributions to any of your traditional IRAs for 2025 or an earlier year. …
> 2. You received a distribution from a Roth IRA. But if either (a) or (b) below applies, enter -0- on line 4b; you
> don't have to see Form 8606 or its instructions. a. Distribution code T is shown in box 7 of Form 1099-R and you made
> a contribution (including a conversion) to a Roth IRA for 2020 or an earlier year. b. Distribution code Q is shown in
> box 7 of Form 1099-R. 3. You converted part or all of a traditional IRA or traditional SIMPLE IRA to a Roth IRA in
> 2025. 4. You had a 2024 or 2025 IRA contribution returned to you, with the related earnings or less any loss, by the
> due date (including extensions) of your tax return for that year. 5. You made excess contributions to your IRA for an
> earlier year and had them returned to you in 2025. 6. You recharacterized part or all of a contribution to a Roth
> IRA as a contribution to a traditional IRA, or vice versa."

> Exception 3 (QCD): "If the total amount distributed is a QCD, enter -0- on line 4b. If only part of the distribution
> is a QCD, enter the part that is not a QCD on line 4b unless Exception 2 applies to that part. … You must have been
> at least age 70 1/2 when the distribution was made. Generally, your total QCDs for the year can't be more than
> $108,000. This includes any amount (up to $54,000) of a one-time QCD to a split-interest entity (SIE). If you file a
> joint return, the same rules apply to your spouse. The amount of the QCD is limited to the amount that would
> otherwise be included in your income. If your IRA includes nondeductible contributions, the distribution is first
> considered to be paid out of otherwise taxable income."

> Exception 4 (HFD): "If the total amount distributed is an HFD and you elect to exclude it from income, enter -0- on
> line 4b. If only part of the distribution is an HFD and you elect to exclude that part from income, enter the part
> that isn't an HFD on line 4b unless Exception 2 applies to that part. … You can't exclude more than the limit on HSA
> contributions or more than the amount that would otherwise be included in your income. If your IRA includes
> nondeductible contributions, the HFD is first considered to be paid out of otherwise taxable income."

> "More than one distribution. … figure the taxable amount of each distribution and enter the total of the taxable
> amounts on line 4b."

> "You must start receiving at least a minimum amount from your traditional IRA by April 1 of the year following the
> year you reach age 73." / "You may have to pay an additional tax if you received an early distribution from your IRA
> and the total wasn't rolled over. See the instructions for Schedule 2, line 8."

**Form 8606 (2025) face** (illustrated in `p590b.pdf p. 20–21`): line 15c "Taxable amount. … If more than zero, also
include this amount on 2025 Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 4b"; line 18 "Taxable amount. Subtract line 17 from
line 16. If more than zero, also include this amount on … line 4b"; line 25c "Taxable amount. … If more than zero,
also include this amount on … line 4b". Line 15b: "Enter the amount on line 15a attributable to qualified disaster
distributions, if any, from 2025 Form(s) 8915-F."

**Publication 590-B (2025), QCDs** (`p590b.pdf p. 14`):

> "The maximum annual exclusion for QCDs is $108,000. Any QCD in excess of the $108,000 exclusion limit is included in
> income as any other distribution." "Offset of QCDs by amounts contributed after age 70½. … the amount of QCDs that
> you can exclude from income is reduced by the excess of the aggregate amount of IRA contributions you deducted for
> the taxable year and any prior year that you were age 70½ or older over the amount of the IRA contributions that
> were used to reduce the excludable amount of QCDs in all earlier years."

QCD Adjustment Worksheet: line 4 "Enter the total amounts of qualified charitable distributions made during the
current year, not to exceed $108,000"; line 5 "Subtract line 3 from line 4. This is the amount of your excludable
qualified charitable distribution" (`p590b.pdf p. 14`).

## 3. What goes on this line

- The taxable amount of every traditional, SEP and SIMPLE IRA distribution of you (and your spouse if MFJ) — the whole
  box 1 when you have no basis and no exception, normally equal to box 2a ("report the total amount distributed from a
  traditional IRA in box 2a. This will be the same amount reported in box 1", `i1099r--2025.pdf p. 13`).
- The part **not** rolled over, **not** a QCD, **not** an excluded HFD.
- QCD amounts over $108,000 and QCD amounts offset by post-70½ deductible contributions (they are ordinary
  distributions).
- Form 8606 line 15c (taxable traditional distributions after basis), line 18 (taxable 2025 Roth conversions), line 25c
  (taxable nonqualified Roth distributions).
- Earnings on a contribution returned before the due date (`i8606.pdf p. 7`).
- Taxable distributions from **inherited** IRAs (a beneficiary's own Form 8606 per decedent when the IRA had basis,
  `i8606.pdf p. 2, 8`).
- Required minimum distributions (RMDs) and early distributions — their taxable amount goes here like any other.
- Form 4852 line 8b for a missing / incorrect IRA 1099-R.
- The 2025 taxable amount of qualified disaster distributions figured on Form 8915-F.

## 6. How to figure it, step by step

1. **Gather each person's IRA Forms 1099-R** (as in `new_lines\4a.md` step 1). Work each person separately; add the
   results at the end.
2. **No exception and no basis:** taxable = box 1 (box 2a). Done for that distribution.
3. **Rollovers (Exception 1):** subtract the amount rolled over within 60 days (one IRA-to-IRA rollover per 1-year
   period; a direct payment to an employer plan, code G, counts as rolled over).
4. **QCDs (Exception 3):** for an owner at least 70½ on the date of the transfer,
   - take the QCDs of the year, **not more than $108,000** (QCD Adjustment Worksheet line 4);
   - subtract the post-70½ deductible IRA contributions of this and earlier years not already used (worksheet lines
     1–3);
   - the result (not below zero) is excluded; the rest is an ordinary distribution. The QCD cannot exceed what would
     otherwise be taxable.
5. **HFDs (Exception 4):** subtract the HFD you elect to exclude, not more than the HSA contribution limit for your
   coverage ($4,300 self-only / $8,550 family, plus the age-55 catch-up) and not more than the taxable amount
   (`i8889.pdf p. 4, 7`).
6. **Form 8606 needed (Exception 2)?** If yes, the rest of the taxable amount comes from Form 8606:
   - **Part I** (traditional IRAs with basis): line 7 = 2025 traditional distributions **excluding** rollovers,
     conversions, recharacterizations, QCDs, HFDs, returned contributions and returned excess contributions
     (`i8606.pdf p. 10`); line 10 = basis ÷ (year-end value + line 7 + line 8), at most 1.000; line 15a = line 7 −
     nontaxable part; line 15b = the part from qualified disaster distributions (goes to Form 8915-F); **line 15c** =
     15a − 15b (reduced via the Line 15c Worksheet for repaid birth/adoption, emergency, domestic-abuse, terminal-illness
     distributions).
   - **Part II** (2025 conversions): line 16 = amount converted; line 17 = its basis; **line 18** = 16 − 17.
   - **Part III** (nonqualified Roth distributions): line 19 = Roth distributions excluding rollovers,
     recharacterizations, returned contributions, QCDs, HFDs, and (if a Roth contribution was made 1998–2020)
     distributions at 59½+ or on death / disability (`i8606.pdf p. 13–14`); line 20 first-home expenses (≤ $10,000
     lifetime); line 22 regular-contribution basis; line 24 conversion / rollover basis; line 25a = 19 − 20 − 22 − 24
     (not below 0); line 25b disaster share; **line 25c** = 25a − 25b.
   - Line 4b (for this person) = line 15c + line 18 + line 25c, plus the taxable part of anything Form 8606 does not
     cover (earnings on a returned contribution; an inherited IRA without basis; a separate Form 8606 per decedent for
     an inherited IRA with basis).
7. **Roth code Q** (or code T with a Roth contribution for 2020 or earlier): taxable 0; no Form 8606.
8. **Qualified disaster distributions:** figure the 2025 taxable amount on Form 8915-F and include it.
9. **Add the persons' amounts**, keep cents, round the total (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`). Enter on **line 4b**.

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rollover into a qualified plan or completed in 2026 | Exclude; attach a statement | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27` |
| Second IRA-to-IRA rollover within 1 year | Not a rollover; taxable (and an excess contribution). "You can make only one rollover from an IRA to another (or the same) IRA in any 1-year period regardless of the number of IRAs you own … trustee-to-trustee transfers between IRAs aren't limited" | `p590a.pdf p. 25` |
| QCD before 70½ | Not a QCD; taxable | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27` |
| QCD from an ongoing SEP or SIMPLE IRA | Not a QCD | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27` |
| QCD over $108,000 | Excess taxable (and deductible as a gift if itemizing) | `p590b.pdf p. 14` |
| QCD and basis in the IRA | QCD comes first out of the taxable part (Form 8606 line 7 leaves the QCD out) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27`; `p590b.pdf p. 14` (Amy example: 4b = 0) |
| One-time QCD to a split-interest entity | Up to $54,000 inside the $108,000; statement; line 4c box 3 "SIE" | `p590b.pdf p. 14` |

| Earlier-year excess returned after the due date, 3 conditions met | Not taxable; no Form 8606 | `i8606.pdf p. 7–8` |

| Inherited IRA with basis | Separate Form 8606 per decedent | `i8606.pdf p. 2, 8` |
| Qualified disaster distribution | Form 8915-F decides the 2025 taxable amount; Form 8606 line 15b / 25b carves it out | `i8606.pdf p. 10–11, 14–15` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** both spouses' taxable amounts are added. Every limit is **per person**: the $108,000 QCD cap ("If
  you file a joint return, the same rules apply to your spouse"), the QCD offset, the HFD, and Form 8606 — "If both you
  and your spouse are required to file 2025 Form 8606, file a separate 2025 Form 8606 for each of you"
  (`i8606.pdf p. 8`).
- **Separate returns:** only your own IRA distributions and your own Form 8606.

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Form 1040 line 9** (total income) → line 11 (AGI) → taxable income.
- Social Security Benefits Worksheet (line 6b), modified AGI for the IRA deduction, Roth contribution limit and
  credits.
- Form 5329: line 1 (early distributions includible in income) and line 11 (traditional IRA distributions included in
  income).
- Net investment income tax: IRA distributions are **not** net investment income.

## 11. Worked example

Dana Sample (000-00-0021, age 72, single).

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Traditional IRA 1099-R, box 1 = box 2a, code 7 | $60,000 |
| — of which sent directly to a charity (QCD, Dana 72) | $10,000 |
| — of which rolled over to another IRA within 60 days | $5,000 |
| Deductible IRA contribution made in 2025 (Dana still working) | $2,000 |
| Nondeductible basis from 2024 Form 8606 line 14 | $9,000 |
| Value of all traditional IRAs on 12/31/2025 | $135,000 |
| Roth IRA 1099-R, code Q | $4,000 |

1. QCD Adjustment Worksheet: line 4 = min($10,000, $108,000) = $10,000; line 3 = $2,000; excludable QCD = **$8,000**.
   The other $2,000 is an ordinary distribution.
2. Basis exists → Form 8606 Part I. Line 7 = $60,000 − $5,000 rollover − $8,000 QCD = **$47,000**.
3. Line 9 = $135,000 + $47,000 + 0 = $182,000. Line 10 = $9,000 ÷ $182,000 = 0.04945.
4. Line 12 = $47,000 × 0.04945 = $2,324. Line 15a = $47,000 − $2,324 = $44,676; no disaster → **line 15c = $44,676**.
5. Roth code Q → nontaxable, no Part III.
6. **Line 4b = $44,676**; line 4a = $60,000 + $4,000 = $64,000; line 4c boxes 1 and 2 checked (plus the breakout
   statement, because two exceptions besides Exception 2 apply).
