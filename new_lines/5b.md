# Form 1040 line 5b — Pensions and annuities, taxable amount

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\5b.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\5b.md`. Which payments belong on lines 5a/5b at all is in `C:\us-tax\new_lines\5a.md`;
the checkboxes in `C:\us-tax\new_lines\5c.md`.

---

## 1. At a glance

Form 1040 line 5b is the **taxable part** of the pensions and annuities reported on line 5a (or, when all of them are
fully taxable, their total). It is figured **per pension or annuity** and the results are added. The payer's Form
1099-R box 2a is the starting point, but the filer replaces it with a lower figure when the law allows: the
**Simplified Method** or **General Rule** (recovering after-tax cost tax free), a **rollover**, the **retired public
safety officer (PSO) exclusion** of up to $3,000 of insurance premiums. A lump sum taxed under **Form 4972** Part III
is left out entirely. Line 5b is part of total income (line 9).

```text
for each pension / annuity (per Form 1099-R or RRB-1099-R, after the line 5a routing):
    taxable_i =
        fully taxable                 → box 1 (= box 2a)
        partially taxable             → box 1 − tax-free part
                                        (Simplified Method Worksheet, or General Rule — Pub. 939;
                                         box 2a may be used, "but you may be able to report a lower taxable amount")
        rollover                      → box 1 − after-tax contributions (box 5) − amount rolled over
                                        (a rollover to a Roth IRA / designated Roth account is generally taxable)
    PSO election (per retired officer)  → taxable − min(premiums paid, $3,000), not below 0
Form 1040 line 5b = Σ taxable_i  (− the Form 4972 Part III lump sum; − box 3 with Part II only)
```

Terms used below:

- **Cost** (investment in the contract) — "your net investment in the plan as of the annuity starting date. It
  doesn't include pre-tax contributions. Your net investment may be shown in box 9b of Form 1099-R"
  (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30`).
- **Annuity starting date** — "the later of the first day of the first period for which you received a payment or the
  date the plan's obligations became fixed" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30`).
- **Simplified Method** — the IRS worksheet that divides the cost by a fixed number of expected monthly payments.
- **General Rule** — the older method of Pub. 939: an exclusion ratio = cost ÷ expected return, applied to each
  payment.
- **Rollover** — "a tax-free distribution of cash or other assets from one retirement plan that is contributed to
  another plan within 60 days of receiving the distribution" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30`).
- **PSO** — an eligible retired public safety officer (law enforcement officer, firefighter, chaplain, rescue squad or
  ambulance crew member).
- **NUA** — net unrealized appreciation in employer securities (Form 1099-R box 6).

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 5b:** "b Taxable amount . . . . . . 5b" (`docs/IRS-Forms/f1040.pdf p. 1`).

**Partially Taxable Pensions and Annuities** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28`):

> "Enter the total pension or annuity payments (from Form 1099-R, box 1) on line 5a. If your Form 1099-R doesn't show
> the taxable amount, you must use the General Rule explained in Pub. 939 to figure the taxable part to enter on
> line 5b. But if your annuity starting date (defined later) was after July 1, 1986, see Simplified Method, later, to
> find out if you must use that method to figure the taxable part. You can ask the IRS to figure the taxable part for
> you for a $1,000 fee. … If your Form 1099-R shows a taxable amount, you can report that amount on line 5b. But you
> may be able to report a lower taxable amount by using the General Rule or the Simplified Method or if the exclusion
> for retired public safety officers, discussed next, applies."

**Insurance Premiums for Retired Public Safety Officers** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28`):

> "If you are an eligible retired public safety officer (law enforcement officer, firefighter, chaplain, or member of
> a rescue squad or ambulance crew who is retired because of disability or because you reached normal retirement
> age), you can elect to exclude from income distributions made from your eligible retirement plan that are used to
> pay the premiums for coverage by an accident or health plan or a long-term care insurance contract. … The
> distribution must be from the plan maintained by the employer from which you retired as a public safety officer.
> … You can exclude from income the smaller of the amount of the premiums paid or $3,000. You can make this election
> only for amounts that would otherwise be included in your income. The amount excluded from your income can't be used
> to claim a medical expense deduction. An eligible retirement plan is a governmental plan that is a qualified trust
> or a section 403(a), 403(b), or 457(b) plan. … If you make this election, reduce the otherwise taxable amount of your
> pension or annuity by the amount excluded. The amount shown in box 2a of Form 1099-R doesn't reflect the exclusion."

**Simplified Method** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30`):

> "You must use the Simplified Method if either of the following applies. 1. Your annuity starting date was after
> July 1, 1986, and you used this method last year to figure the taxable part. 2. Your annuity starting date was after
> November 18, 1996, and both of the following apply. a. The payments are from a qualified employee plan, a qualified
> employee annuity, or a tax-sheltered annuity. b. On your annuity starting date, either you were under age 75 or the
> number of years of guaranteed payments was fewer than 5."

> "If you received U.S. Civil Service retirement benefits and you chose the alternative annuity option, see Pub. 721 …
> Do not use the Simplified Method Worksheet in these instructions."

**Age at annuity starting date** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30`): "If you are the retiree, use your age on the annuity
starting date. If you are the survivor of a retiree, use the retiree's age on their annuity starting date. But if your
annuity starting date was after 1997 and the payments are for your life and that of your beneficiary, use your
combined ages on the annuity starting date."

**Rollovers** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30`):

> "However, a rollover to a Roth IRA or a designated Roth account is generally not a tax-free distribution. … Enter on
> line 5a the distribution from Form 1099-R, box 1. From this amount, subtract any contributions (usually shown in
> box 5) that were taxable to you when made. From that result, subtract the amount of the rollover. Enter the
> remaining amount on line 5b. If the remaining amount is zero and you have no other distribution to report on
> line 5b, enter -0- on line 5b. Also check box 1 on line 5c."

**Form 1099-R box 2a** (`i1099r--2025.pdf p. 11`): "When determining the taxable amount to be entered in box 2a, do
not reduce the taxable amount by any portion of the $3,000 exclusion … for retired public safety officers … Enter 0
(zero) in box 2a for: • A direct rollover (other than an IRR) from a qualified plan, a section 403(b) plan, or a
governmental section 457(b) plan to another such plan or to a traditional IRA; • A direct rollover from a designated
Roth account to a Roth IRA". Box 2b (`p. 13`): "Enter an "X" in this box if you are unable to reasonably obtain the
data needed to compute the taxable amount." Box 6 (`p. 15`): "Include the NUA in box 1 but not in box 2a".

## 3. What goes on this line

- The taxable part of every pension and annuity listed in `5a.md` §3, figured one by one.
- A fully taxable pension: its whole box 1 (with line 5a blank if all are fully taxable).
- The part of a rollover distribution that was not rolled over (and is not a return of after-tax contributions).
- A rollover from a pre-tax plan to a Roth IRA or designated Roth account — generally taxable
  (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30`; the payer reports such an amount in box 2a, code G with a taxable box 2a).
- The non-qualified distribution earnings of a designated Roth account (code B; `i1099r--2025.pdf p. 12` example:
  box 1 $5,000, box 2a $300).
- NUA in employer securities only if the filer elects to include it (`f4972.pdf p. 3`: "Normally, NUA in employer
  securities received as part of a lump-sum distribution isn't taxable until the securities are sold. However, you
  can elect to include NUA in taxable income in the year received.").
- The ordinary-income portion (box 2a − box 3) of a lump sum taxed under Form 4972 Part II only (`f4972.pdf p. 2`).

| Amount rolled over (60 days or direct) to a non-Roth plan / IRA | Not taxable; line 5c box 1 | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30` |
| PSO premiums (up to $3,000) | Excluded; line 5c box 2; cannot also be a medical deduction | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28` |
| Disability pension before minimum retirement age; code 8 corrective distribution | Line 1h | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25, p. 28` |
| A PSO exclusion on a line-1h disability pension | Next to line 1h ("PSO" and the amount) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28, p. 30` |
| IRA distributions | Line 4b | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25` |
| Lump sum under Form 4972 Part III; capital-gain part under Part II | Form 4972 (tax added on Form 1040 line 16) | `f4972.pdf p. 2` |
| The 10% additional tax on an early distribution | Schedule 2 line 8 / Form 5329 (it uses line 5b as its base) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 112` |

## 6. How to figure it, step by step

Do steps 1–5 **for each pension or annuity separately** ("figure the taxable part of each separately",
`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 29`), then step 6.

1. **Fully taxable?** If you never contributed after-tax money or you already recovered all of your cost before 2025,
   the whole box 1 is taxable (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28`).
2. **Partially taxable — choose the method.**
   - Must use the **Simplified Method** when the annuity started after July 1, 1986 and you used it last year, or it
     started after November 18, 1996 from a qualified plan / qualified annuity / tax-sheltered annuity and you were under
     75 (or guaranteed payments < 5 years) (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30`).
   - Otherwise (for example a commercial nonqualified annuity, code D) the **General Rule** of Pub. 939
     (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28`).
   - If box 2a shows a taxable amount you may use it, or a lower worksheet result (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28`).
3. **Simplified Method Worksheet** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 29`):

   | Line | What |
   |---|---|
   | 1 | Total payments from Form 1099-R box 1 (also on line 5a) |
   | 2 | Your cost at the annuity starting date (beneficiary of an employee who died before August 21, 1996: include the death benefit exclusion, up to $5,000) |
   | 3 | Table 1 (by age at the starting date; column by starting date before Nov 19, 1996 / after Nov 18, 1996), or Table 2 (combined ages) if the starting date was after 1997 and payments are for your life and your beneficiary's. If you did the worksheet last year, skip line 3 and enter last year's line 4 on line 4 |
   | 4 | Line 2 ÷ line 3 |
   | 5 | Line 4 × the number of months for which this year's payments were made (starting date before 1987: enter on line 8, skip 6–7) |
   | 6 | Amount recovered tax free in years after 1986 (last year's line 10) |
   | 7 | Line 2 − line 6 |
   | 8 | Smaller of line 5 or line 7 |
   | 9 | **Taxable amount** = line 1 − line 8, not below 0. "If your Form 1099-R shows a larger amount, use the amount on this line instead" |
   | 10 | Line 6 + line 8 (recovered through 2025; for next year) — not for a starting date before 1987 |
   | 11 | Line 2 − line 10 (cost still to recover) |

   Table 1 (age at starting date → number): before Nov 19, 1996: 55 or under 300, 56–60 260, 61–65 240, 66–70 170,
   71 or older 120; after Nov 18, 1996: 360, 310, 260, 210, 160. Table 2 (combined ages): 110 or under 410,
   111–120 360, 121–130 310, 131–140 260, 141 or older 210.
4. **General Rule** (Pub. 939, not in the 2025 source set): exclusion ratio = cost ÷ expected return, times the
   payments; the instructions only point to Pub. 939 (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28`).
5. **Rollover.** For a distribution rolled over in whole or part: box 1 − after-tax contributions (box 5) − the amount
   rolled over = taxable; 0 if fully rolled over; check line 5c box 1. A rollover to a Roth IRA or designated Roth
   account is generally taxable (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30`).
6. **PSO exclusion** (only an eligible retired PSO, governmental plan of the employer he or she retired from, premiums
   for accident / health / long-term care coverage): subtract the smaller of the premiums or $3,000 from the
   otherwise taxable amount (not below 0); check line 5c box 2 (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 28`).
7. **Form 4972.** Take out a Part III lump sum entirely; with Part II only keep box 2a − box 3 (`f4972.pdf p. 2`).
8. **Add** the taxable parts with cents, round the total if you round (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`), enter on **line 5b**.
   A full rollover with nothing else: enter -0- (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30`).

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** each spouse's pensions are figured separately (each annuity on its own worksheet); line 5b is the
  sum. Each retired PSO has his or her own $3,000 limit (the exclusion is for "your eligible retirement plan").
- **Separate returns:** only your own Forms 1099-R / RRB-1099-R. Community property: Pub. 555 / Form 8958 (not quoted
  here).

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Form 1040 line 9** (total income) → line 11 (AGI) → taxable income.
- Form 5329 Part I / Schedule 2 line 8 (early distributions: "The taxable amount of the distribution is the part of the
  distribution you reported on … line 4b or 5b, or on Form 4972", `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 112`).
- Form 8960 line 3 (net investment income) for annuities that are not from a qualified, 403(b), IRA or 457(b) plan —
  code D: "include on Form 8960, line 3, the taxable amount reported in box 2a of Form 1099-R" (`i8960.pdf p. 7–8`).
- Every AGI-based figure (Social Security Benefits Worksheet, credits, Schedule 1-A phase-outs, medical floor).

## 11. Worked example

Chris Example (000-00-0004), single, retired city firefighter.

| Document | Box 1 | Box 2a | Other | Code |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| City Fire Pension (governmental qualified plan) | $24,000.00 | blank, box 2b checked | Box 9b $31,200 | 7 |
| Former employer 401(k) | $40,000.00 | $40,000.00 | — | 7 |

Facts: annuity starting date July 1, 2020, Chris was 62, single-life annuity of $2,000 a month (6 payments in 2020,
12 a year since). Chris completed the worksheet every year. $3,600 of health premiums were paid from the pension. Of
the $40,000 401(k) check, Chris deposited $25,000 into a traditional IRA within 60 days.

Pension 1 — Simplified Method:

1. Line 1 = $24,000. Line 2 = $31,200. Line 3: Table 1, after Nov 18, 1996, age 61–65 → 260.
2. Line 4 = $31,200 ÷ 260 = $120. Line 5 = $120 × 12 = $1,440.
3. Line 6 = recovered 2020–2024 = 6 × $120 + 4 × $1,440 = $6,480. Line 7 = $31,200 − $6,480 = $24,720.
4. Line 8 = smaller of $1,440 and $24,720 = $1,440. Line 9 = $24,000 − $1,440 = $22,560.
5. Line 10 = $6,480 + $1,440 = $7,920; line 11 = $31,200 − $7,920 = $23,280 (for next year).
6. PSO: smaller of $3,600 and $3,000 = $3,000 → taxable $19,560.

Pension 2 — rollover: $40,000 − $0 (box 5) − $25,000 = $15,000.

**Form 1040 line 5a = $64,000; line 5b = $19,560 + $15,000 = $34,560**; line 5c boxes 1 and 2 checked.
