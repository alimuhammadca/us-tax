# Form 1040 line 6a — Social security benefits

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\6a.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\6a.md`. The taxable part is on line 6b (`6b.md`); the two checkboxes are lines 6c (`6c.md`)
and 6d (`6d.md`).

---

## 1. At a glance

Form 1040 line 6a is the **net** social security benefits of the year: the total of **box 5** of every Form SSA-1099
(Social Security Benefit Statement) and every Form RRB-1099 (Payments by the Railroad Retirement Board) issued to
**you** — and, on a joint return, to **your spouse**. Box 5 is already "box 3 minus box 4": gross benefits paid in
2025 less benefits repaid in 2025. Line 6a is entered **even when none of the benefits are taxable**; it is the
starting figure (line 1) of the Social Security Benefits Worksheet that produces line 6b. Line 6a itself is never
added into total income — only line 6b is.

```text
Form 1040 line 6a =
    Σ SSA-1099 box 5            (net benefits: box 3 gross − box 4 repaid)
  + Σ RRB-1099 box 5            (net SSEB portion of tier 1 railroad retirement benefits)
    — each statement of you, + your spouse if married filing jointly;
    — a negative box 5 on one statement offsets a positive one on another;
    — Supplemental Security Income (SSI) is not part of it (it is never on an SSA-1099).
```

Terms used below:

- **SSA-1099** — the statement the Social Security Administration sends each beneficiary: box 3 benefits paid,
  box 4 benefits repaid, box 5 net benefits, box 6 voluntary federal income tax withheld.
- **RRB-1099** — the statement the Railroad Retirement Board sends for the **social security equivalent benefit
  (SSEB) portion of tier 1** railroad retirement benefits, "the part of tier 1 benefits that a railroad employee or
  beneficiary would have been entitled to receive under the social security system" (`p17.pdf p. 64`). Other
  railroad benefits (non-SSEB tier 1, tier 2, vested dual benefits, supplemental annuity) are pensions, reported on
  Form RRB-1099-R and lines 5a/5b.
- **SSI** — Supplemental Security Income, a needs-based payment administered by the SSA. Not a social security
  benefit for these rules and never taxable.
- **Lump-sum (retroactive) payment** — benefits paid in 2025 that belong to earlier years. They are part of box 5 and
  of line 6a; only the taxable part (line 6b) can be refigured with the lump-sum election (line 6c).

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 6a:** "Social security benefits" (caption from the assignment and the semantic field map
`pdfs\f1040_field_mapping_semantic.csv`, field `line6a_social_security_benefits`; the 2025 form PDF is not in
`docs\books`).

**2025 Instructions for Form 1040 — Lines 6a and 6b** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31`):

> "You should receive a Form SSA-1099 showing in box 3 the total social security benefits paid to you. Box 4 will
> show the amount of any benefits you repaid in 2025. If you received railroad retirement benefits treated as social
> security, you should receive a Form RRB-1099."
>
> "Use the Social Security Benefits Worksheet in these instructions to see if any of your benefits are taxable."

**Social Security Benefits Worksheet, line 1** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 32`):

> "Enter the total amount from box 5 of all your Forms SSA-1099 and RRB-1099. Also enter this amount on Form 1040 or
> 1040-SR, line 6a."

**Pub. 17 (2025), chapter 7** (`p17.pdf p. 64`):

> "Social security benefits include monthly retirement, survivor, and disability benefits. They don't include
> Supplemental Security Income (SSI) payments, which aren't taxable."
>
> "You may receive more than one of these forms for the same year. You should add the amounts shown on all the Forms
> SSA-1099 and Forms RRB-1099 you receive for the year to determine the total amounts received and repaid, and taxes
> withheld for that year."

(`p17.pdf p. 65`):

> "Who is taxed. Benefits are included in the taxable income (to the extent they are taxable) of the person who has
> the legal right to receive the benefits. For example, if you and your child receive benefits, but the check for
> your child is made out in your name, you must use only your part of the benefits to see whether any benefits are
> taxable to you. One-half of the part that belongs to your child must be added to your child's other income to see
> whether any of those benefits are taxable to your child."
>
> "Repayment of benefits. Any repayment of benefits you made during 2025 must be subtracted from the gross benefits
> you received in 2025. It doesn't matter whether the repayment was for a benefit you received in 2025 or in an
> earlier year. … Your gross benefits are shown in box 3 of Form SSA-1099 or RRB-1099. Your repayments are shown in
> box 4. The amount in box 5 shows your net benefits for 2025 (box 3 minus box 4)."
>
> "Benefits not taxable. Report your net benefits (the total amount from box 5 of all your Forms SSA-1099 and
> RRB-1099) on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 6a. Enter -0- on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 6b."

**Repayments more than gross benefits** (`p17.pdf p. 68`):

> "If this occurred, your net benefits in box 5 will be a negative figure (a figure in parentheses) and none of your
> benefits will be taxable. Don't use a worksheet in this case. If you receive more than one form, a negative figure
> in box 5 of one form is used to offset a positive figure in box 5 of another form for that same year."
>
> "Joint return. If you and your spouse file a joint return, and your Form SSA-1099 or RRB-1099 has a negative figure
> in box 5, but your spouse's doesn't, subtract the amount in box 5 of your form from the amount in box 5 of your
> spouse's form."

**Disability payments for terrorist-attack injuries** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31`):

> "Don't include in your income any disability payments (including Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI)
> payments) you receive for injuries incurred as a direct result of a terrorist attack directed against the United
> States (or its allies) … If these payments are incorrectly reported as taxable on Form SSA-1099, don't include the
> nontaxable portion of income on your tax return."

## 3. What goes on this line

- Box 5 of every SSA-1099 of you (and your spouse on a joint return): monthly retirement, survivor and disability
  benefits, including any lump-sum (retroactive) payment received in 2025 for earlier years (`p17.pdf p. 65`,
  Worksheet 7-1 line A: "Include the full amount of any lump-sum benefit payments received in 2025, for 2025 and
  earlier years").
- Box 5 of every RRB-1099 (net SSEB portion of tier 1) of you and, on a joint return, your spouse
  (`p17.pdf p. 64`).
- A negative box 5 on one form offsets a positive box 5 on another (also between spouses on a joint return)
  (`p17.pdf p. 68`).
- Benefits a child has the legal right to receive belong on the **child's** return, even if the check is in the
  parent's name (`p17.pdf p. 65`).

## 4. What does not go here

| Look-alike amount | Where it goes instead | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Supplemental Security Income (SSI) | Nowhere — not taxable, not a social security benefit | `p17.pdf p. 64` |
| SSDI paid for injuries from a terrorist attack (only the part before full retirement age) | Left out of line 6a | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31` |
| Non-SSEB tier 1, tier 2, vested dual benefit, supplemental annuity (Form RRB-1099-R) | Lines 5a / 5b (pensions, Pub. 575) | `p17.pdf p. 64` |
| Benefits reported on Form SSA-1042S / RRB-1042S (nonresident aliens) | Pub. 519 rules, not this chapter | `p17.pdf p. 64` |
| Foreign social security benefits | Taxed as annuities (lines 5a/5b) unless a treaty treats them as U.S. social security | `p17.pdf p. 64` |
| SSA / RRB lump-sum **death** benefit | Not taxable | `p17.pdf p. 66` |
| A lump-sum payment for accrued annual leave on a disability retirement | Wages (salary payment), not a disability benefit | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31` |
| A child's benefits (child has the legal right to them) | The child's own return | `p17.pdf p. 65` |
| Federal income tax withheld (SSA-1099 box 6, RRB-1099 box 10) | Line 25b | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf` line 25b |

## 5. Where the numbers come from

| Source document | Box | How it enters line 6a |
|---|---|---|
| SSA-1099 | Box 2 (beneficiary's SSN) | Decides whose benefits they are (you, your spouse on a joint return, or someone else) |
| SSA-1099 | Box 5 (net benefits) | Added (may be negative) |
| SSA-1099 | Box 3 / box 4 | Only explain box 5 (box 5 = box 3 − box 4); box 4 > box 3 makes box 5 negative |
| RRB-1099 | Recipient's identification number | Whose benefits they are |
| RRB-1099 | Box 5 (net SSEB) | Added |
| Taxpayer's records | SSDI for terrorist-attack injuries | Left out (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31`) |

## 6. How to figure it, step by step

1. **Collect the statements that belong on this return.** Use the beneficiary on box 2 (SSA-1099) or the recipient
   (RRB-1099). On a joint return take yours and your spouse's; on any other return take only your own. A child's
   benefits are the child's (`p17.pdf p. 65`).
2. **Take box 5 of each.** Do not refigure it from boxes 3 and 4 — box 5 already is box 3 minus box 4
   (`p17.pdf p. 65`).
3. **Leave out** any SSDI for terrorist-attack injuries that the SSA-1099 shows as benefits (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf
   p. 31`), and never add SSI.
4. **Add** all the box 5 amounts, letting negative figures offset positive ones (`p17.pdf p. 68`). Keep cents while
   adding and round only the total if you round (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`).
5. **If the total is negative** (repayments exceed benefits), none of the benefits are taxable and no worksheet is
   used (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31`; `p17.pdf p. 68`). The 2025 instructions do not say what figure to print on
   line 6a in that case. The excess repayment may be deductible (see section 7).
6. Enter the total on **Form 1040 line 6a** and as **line 1 of the Social Security Benefits Worksheet**.

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Repayments more than benefits (box 5 total negative) | None taxable; no worksheet. If the negative figure represents benefits taxed in an earlier year and is more than $3,000: Schedule A line 16 deduction, or a credit under I.R.C. 1341 — whichever gives less tax. Pub. 17 places the credit on Schedule 3 line 13z with "I.R.C. 1341"; the 2025 Schedule 3 instructions put a claim-of-right repayment credit on **line 13b** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 117`), which is the later and form-specific source. $3,000 or less: "a miscellaneous itemized deduction and can no longer be deducted" | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31`; `p17.pdf p. 68` |
| Lump-sum payment for earlier years | All of it on line 6a; the taxable part may be refigured with the lump-sum election (line 6c) | `p17.pdf p. 65–66` |
| Child's benefits paid in the parent's name | The child's income, not the parent's | `p17.pdf p. 65` |
| SSDI for terrorist-attack injuries | Not included; if the SSA-1099 reports it as taxable, leave the nontaxable part out (the IRS may send a notice) | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31` |
| Disability benefits generally | Stay on line 6a | `p17.pdf p. 64` |
| Only income is social security | Benefits generally not taxable; a return may not be required | `p17.pdf p. 64` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** both spouses' box 5 amounts are added, "even if your spouse didn't receive any benefits"
  their income enters the worksheet (`p17.pdf p. 64`). A negative box 5 of one spouse is subtracted from the other's
  (`p17.pdf p. 68`).
- **Separate returns:** each spouse reports only the benefits they have the legal right to receive.
- There is no per-person limit on line 6a.

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Social Security Benefits Worksheet line 1** → **Form 1040 line 6b** (taxable part). Line 6a itself is not part
  of line 9.
- Figures built from "nontaxable social security" (line 6a − line 6b): Schedule R line 13a, the premium tax credit
  household income, and the optional sales-tax table income.

## 11. Worked example

Alex Sample (000-00-0001) and Jordan Sample (000-00-0002) file jointly.

| Document | Box 3 | Box 4 | Box 5 | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alex — SSA-1099 | $14,400.00 | $0 | $14,400.00 | Includes a $2,400 retroactive payment for 2024 |
| Jordan — RRB-1099 | $6,000.00 | $500.00 | $5,500.00 | Net SSEB |
| Jordan — SSI notice | — | — | — | $1,200 SSI — not a social security benefit |
| Taylor Sample (000-00-0003, dependent son) — SSA-1099 | $4,000.00 | $0 | $4,000.00 | Survivor benefit; Taylor's own income |

Steps:

1. Statements for this return: Alex's SSA-1099 and Jordan's RRB-1099. Taylor's SSA-1099 is his (`p17.pdf p. 65`).
2. Box 5 amounts: $14,400.00 and $5,500.00. SSI is ignored.
3. Total: **$19,900.00** → **Form 1040 line 6a = $19,900**. The $2,400 for 2024 stays in (line 6c may lower its tax).
