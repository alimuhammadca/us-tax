# Form 1040 line 6c — Lump-sum election method (checkbox)

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\6c.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\6c.md`. The amounts are on lines 6a (`6a.md`) and 6b (`6b.md`).

---

## 1. At a glance

Line 6c holds no amount. It is a checkbox: "If you elect to use the lump-sum election method, check here". It
applies when the 2025 benefits on line 6a include a **lump-sum (retroactive) payment for an earlier year**. Normally
all 2025 benefits — including the back payment — are taxed using 2025 income. The election instead figures the
taxable part of the back payment **with the income of the year it was for**, and adds that to the taxable part of
the 2025 benefits figured with 2025 income. The filer elects only when that total is lower; then the lower figure
goes on line 6b and the box is checked. No earlier-year return is amended.

```text
normal   = Social Security Benefits Worksheet on all of line 6a with 2025 income      (6b.md)
election = worksheet on (line 6a − amounts for earlier years) with 2025 income
         + for each earlier year: taxable benefits refigured with that year's income and the earlier-year amount
                                  added, less the taxable benefits already reported for that year
line 6c checked and line 6b = election   if the filer elects (worth it only when election < normal)
```

(The exact worksheet lines are in Pub. 915 Worksheets 2, 3 and 4, which are **not** in `docs\books`; the shape
above follows the description in Pub. 17 and is not a line-by-line quotation.)

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 6c:** "If you elect to use the lump-sum election method, check here (see instructions)"
(caption from the assignment; field `line6c_lump_sum_election` in `pdfs\f1040_field_mapping_semantic.csv`).

**Line 6c** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31`):

> "Check the box on line 6c if you elect to use the lump-sum election method for your benefits. If any of your
> benefits are taxable for 2025 and they include a lump-sum benefit payment that was for an earlier year, you may be
> able to reduce the taxable amount with the lump-sum election. See Lump-Sum Election in Pub. 915 for details."

The worksheet repeats it as a tip (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 32`).

**Pub. 17 (2025)** (`p17.pdf p. 66`):

> "Lump-sum election. You must include the taxable part of a lump-sum (retroactive) payment of benefits received in
> 2025 in your 2025 income, even if the payment includes benefits for an earlier year."
>
> "This type of lump-sum benefit payment shouldn't be confused with the lump-sum death benefit that both the SSA and
> RRB pay to many of their beneficiaries. No part of the lump-sum death benefit is subject to tax."
>
> "Generally, you use your 2025 income to figure the taxable part of the total benefits received in 2025. However,
> you may be able to figure the taxable part of a lump-sum payment for an earlier year separately, using your income
> for the earlier year. You can elect this method if it lowers your taxable benefits."
>
> "Making the election. If you received a lump-sum benefit payment in 2025 that includes benefits for one or more
> earlier years, follow the instructions in Pub. 915 under Lump-Sum Election to see whether making the election will
> lower your taxable benefits. That discussion also explains how to make the election."
>
> "Because the earlier year's taxable benefits are included in your 2025 income, no adjustment is made to the
> earlier year's return. Don't file an amended return for the earlier year."

"Which worksheet to use … 3. You received a lump-sum payment for an earlier year. In this situation, also complete
Worksheet 2 or 3 and Worksheet 4 in Pub. 915." (`p17.pdf p. 66`)

## 3. When the box is checked

All of these hold:

1. Line 6a includes a lump-sum benefit payment received in 2025 that was **for an earlier year** (`p17.pdf p. 66`).
2. Some benefits are taxable for 2025 under the normal worksheet (otherwise there is nothing to reduce;
   `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31`).
3. The filer **elects** the method — it is optional, and the IRS describes it as worthwhile only "if it lowers your
   taxable benefits" (`p17.pdf p. 66`).

## 4. When the box stays blank

| Situation | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| No back payment for an earlier year | Nothing to elect | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31` |
| Lump-sum **death** benefit | Not taxable; not this election | `p17.pdf p. 66` |
| Election would not lower line 6b | The filer does not elect | `p17.pdf p. 66` |
| No benefits taxable under the normal worksheet | Nothing to reduce | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31` |

## 5. Where the numbers come from

| Item | Source |
|---|---|
| Amount of the 2025 payment that belongs to each earlier year | SSA-1099 / RRB-1099 (the SSA statement describes back payments by year; the RRB-1099 has boxes for earlier years) — the 2025 sources used here do not quote those box descriptions |
| Each earlier year's income, tax-exempt interest, adjustments, filing status, benefits and taxable benefits as reported | That year's return |

## 6. Married filing jointly / separately

The earlier year's figures are the ones of that year's return (its filing status and base amount). The sources used
here do not give more detail; Pub. 915 does.

## 7. Forms to attach or keep

Pub. 915 Worksheets 2/3/4 are kept with the records; only the checkbox and the line 6b figure go on the return.
No amended return for the earlier year (`p17.pdf p. 66`).

## 8. Where it goes next

The checkbox changes no arithmetic by itself; the election's figure becomes line 6b and flows to line 9.

## 9. Worked example (shape only)

Dana Sample (single, 000-00-0006) received $11,250 of benefits in 2025, of which $2,250 is a back payment for
October–December 2024. Under the normal worksheet with 2025 income, line 6b is $9,562.50. Refigured with the election
— the 2025 part ($9,000) on 2025 income gives $7,650, and adding the $2,250 to 2024 (Dana had no 2024 benefits and
$23,000 of 2024 income; $1,125 + $23,000 = $24,125 is under the $25,000 base) gives $0 more — the election figure
is $7,650. Dana elects: **line 6c checked**, line 6b $7,650, line 6a stays $11,250. (Figures follow the app's QA
scenario sc_00237 run C, which was worked from Pub. 915; they are not an IRS example from the sources here.)
