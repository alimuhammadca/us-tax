# Form 1040 line 6d — Married filing separately and lived apart all year (checkbox)

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\6d.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\6d.md`. The worksheet that uses this fact is in `6b.md`.

---

## 1. At a glance

Line 6d holds no amount. It is a checkbox for a filer who is **married filing separately (MFS)** and **lived apart
from the spouse for all of 2025**. That fact decides the base amount of the Social Security Benefits Worksheet:
$25,000 (lived apart all year, box checked) or $0 with 85% of provisional income taxable (lived together at any
time, box blank). The IRS warns that leaving it blank may bring a math error notice.

```text
line 6d checked  ⇔  filing status = married filing separately  AND  lived apart from spouse for all of 2025
```

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 1040 (2025), line 6d:** "If you are married filing separately and lived apart from your spouse the entire
year, check here" (caption from the assignment; field `line6d_mfs_lived_apart_all_year` in
`pdfs\f1040_field_mapping_semantic.csv`).

**Line 6d** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 31`):

> "If you are married filing separately and you lived apart from your spouse for all of 2025, check the box on
> line 6d. If you don't check the box on line 6d, you may get a math error notice from the IRS."

**Social Security Benefits Worksheet** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 32`): "If you are married filing separately and you
lived apart from your spouse for all of 2025, check the box on line 6d." and at line 9: "If you are married filing
separately and you lived apart from your spouse for all of 2025, be sure you checked the box on line 6d."

**Pub. 17 (2025)** (`p17.pdf p. 65`): "Reporting on Form 1040 or 1040-SR. Report your net benefits … on line 6a and
the taxable part on line 6b. If you are married filing separately and you lived apart from your spouse for all of
2025, check the box on line 6d." Also under "Benefits not taxable": "If you are married filing separately and lived
apart from your spouse the entire year, check the box on line 6d."

## 3. When the box is checked

1. Filing status is married filing separately, and
2. the filer lived apart from the spouse **for all of 2025** — living together on any day of 2025 fails the test
   (the worksheet's alternative is "lived with your spouse at any time in 2025", `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 32`).

The instruction itself does not add a condition about having benefits; it appears under lines 6a–6d and is used
only by the Social Security Benefits Worksheet.

## 4. When the box stays blank

| Situation | Effect | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Any filing status other than MFS (including head of household for a married person treated as unmarried) | Box not used; their own base amount applies | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 32` |
| MFS and lived with the spouse at any time in 2025 | Base $0: 85% of worksheet line 7 (Form 1040 worksheet numbering) up to 85% of benefits | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 32`; `p17.pdf p. 66` |

## 5. Where the fact comes from

The filer's own knowledge of where each spouse lived during 2025. No statement reports it.

## 6. Married filing jointly / separately

Only separate returns use it. Each spouse filing separately answers for their own return; the fact is the same for
both (they either lived together at some time in 2025 or did not).

## 7. Forms to attach

None.

## 8. Where it goes next

It selects the $25,000 base / $9,000 band on the Social Security Benefits Worksheet (`6b.md`) instead of the $0
path. It changes no amount directly.

## 9. Worked example

Morgan Sample (000-00-0007) files separately; Morgan moved out in December 2024 and did not live with the spouse at
any time in 2025. Benefits $20,000, other income $15,000. Worksheet line 8 = $10,000 + $15,000 = $25,000, not more
than the $25,000 base: line 6b = 0, **line 6d checked**. Had Morgan lived with the spouse in January 2025, line 6d
would be blank and line 6b = smaller of 85% × $25,000 and 85% × $20,000 = $17,000.
