`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\forms\2106.md`.

## 1. At a glance

Since 2018 an employee can no longer deduct unreimbursed job expenses as a miscellaneous itemized deduction. Form 2106
survives for **four groups only**: Armed Forces reservists, qualified performing artists, fee-basis state or local
government officials, and employees with impairment-related work expenses. The form adds up the job expenses
(Part I, Step 1), takes off the employer's reimbursements that were not put in W-2 box 1 (Step 2), limits meals to
50% (Step 3), and gives a total on line 10. For the first three groups the part of line 10 that belongs to the
qualifying activity is an **adjustment to income on Schedule 1 line 12**; for the fourth it is an **itemized deduction
on Schedule A line 16**. Part II figures the vehicle expense that goes on line 1 — by the 2025 standard mileage rate of
70 cents a mile or by actual expenses plus depreciation.

```text
Column A (other than meals) = line 1 vehicle + line 2 parking/tolls/local transport + line 3 overnight travel
                              + line 4 other job expenses                                     (line 6, col. A)
Column B (meals)            = line 5 meals                                                    (line 6, col. B)
line 8  = line 6 − line 7 reimbursements not in W-2 box 1, per column, not below 0
          (column A excess of line 7 over line 6 → income on Form 1040 line 1a)
line 9  = col. A: line 8;  col. B: line 8 × 50% (80% if subject to DOT hours-of-service limits)
line 10 = line 9 col. A + line 9 col. B
          → Schedule 1 line 12 (reservist > 100-mile travel, performing artist, fee-basis official)
          → Schedule A line 16 (impairment-related work expenses)
line 1  = Part II line 22 (business miles × $0.70) or line 29 (actual expenses × business % + depreciation)
```

Terms used below:

- **Reservist** — a member of a reserve component: "the Army, Navy, Marine Corps, Air Force, or Coast Guard Reserve;
  the Army National Guard of the United States; the Air National Guard of the United States; or the Reserve Corps of
  the Public Health Service" (`i2106.pdf p. 3`).
- **Qualified performing artist** — an employee who meets the four tests in section 7.
- **Fee-basis official** — someone "employed by a state or political subdivision of a state and … compensated, in
  whole or in part, on a fee basis" (`i2106.pdf p. 4`).
- **Impairment-related work expenses** — "allowable expenses of an individual with physical or mental disabilities for
  attendant care at their place of employment … [and] other expenses in connection with the place of employment that
  enable the employee to work" (`i2106.pdf p. 4`).
- **Accountable plan** — an employer reimbursement arrangement under which the employee accounts for the expenses and
  returns any excess; its payments are not in W-2 box 1 (`i2106.pdf p. 3`).
- **Tax home** — "your regular or main place of business or post of duty regardless of where you maintain your family
  home" (`i2106.pdf p. 2`).
- **M&IE** — the federal per diem rate for meals and incidental expenses.

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 2106 (2025) heading** (`f2106.pdf p. 1`): "Employee Business Expenses (for use only by Armed Forces reservists,
qualified performing artists, fee-basis state or local government officials, and employees with impairment-related
work expenses) … Attach to Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR."

**Purpose** (`i2106.pdf p. 1`):

> "Use Form 2106 if you were an Armed Forces reservist, a qualified performing artist, a fee-basis state or local
> government official, or an employee with impairment-related work expenses. Employees who do not fit into one of the
> listed categories may not use the Form 2106 due to the termination of miscellaneous itemized deductions subject to
> the 2% floor under section 67(a)."

> "Excess reimbursements. If you are not a member of the Armed Forces reserves, a qualified performing artist, a
> fee-basis state or local government official, or an employee with impairment-related work expenses, and receive
> reimbursements in excess of your expenses from your employer's nonaccountable plan, the excess reimbursements should
> be included as wages on your Form W-2 and your income tax return."

**Form line 8** (`f2106.pdf p. 1`): "Subtract line 7 from line 6. If zero or less, enter -0-. However, if line 7 is
greater than line 6 in Column A, report the excess as income on Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 1a."

**Form line 10** (`f2106.pdf p. 1`): "Add the amounts on line 9 for both columns and enter the total here. Also, enter
the total on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 12. Employees with impairment-related work expenses, see the instructions for
rules on where to enter the total on your return."

**Schedule 1 line 12** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 93`):

> "Include the following deductions on line 12. • Certain business expenses of National Guard and reserve members who
> traveled more than 100 miles from home to perform services as a National Guard or reserve member.
> • Performing-arts-related expenses as a qualified performing artist. • Business expenses of fee-basis state or local
> government officials. For more details, see Form 2106."

**Line 10 — reservist** (`i2106.pdf p. 3`):

> "If you qualify, complete Form 2106 and include the part of the line 10 amount attributable to the expenses for
> travel more than 100 miles away from home in connection with your performance of services as a member of the
> reserves on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 12 … The amount of expenses you can deduct on Schedule 1 (Form 1040),
> line 12, is limited to the regular federal per diem rate (for lodging, meals, and incidental expenses) and the
> standard mileage rate (for car expenses), plus any parking fees, ferry fees, and tolls."

**Line 10 — impairment-related work expenses** (`i2106.pdf p. 4`): "If you qualify, enter the part of the line 10
amount attributable to impairment-related work expenses on Schedule A (Form 1040), line 16." Pub. 463 adds: "After you
complete Form 2106, enter your impairment-related work expenses from Form 2106, line 10, on Schedule A (Form 1040),
line 16, and identify the type and amount of this expense on the line next to line 16" (`p463.pdf p. 51`).

**Line 9** (`i2106.pdf p. 3`): "Generally, you can deduct only 50% of your business meal expenses, including meals
incurred while away from home on business." Pub. 463: "If you are subject to the Department of Transportation's
'hours of service' limits, you can deduct 80% of your business-related meal expenses" (`p463.pdf p. 15`).

**What's New** (`i2106.pdf p. 1`): "Standard mileage rate. The 2025 per mile rate for business use of your vehicle is
70 cents (0.70)."

## 3. What goes on this form

- The job expenses of a person in one of the four groups, in the activity that qualifies: vehicle (line 1),
  parking / tolls / local transport (line 2), overnight travel without meals (line 3), other job expenses — "business
  gifts, education (tuition, fees, and books), trade publications, etc." (line 4), meals (line 5) (`i2106.pdf p. 2–3`).
- Employer and third-party reimbursements for those expenses that were **not** in W-2 box 1, including amounts in
  "Form W-2, Box 12, code L" (line 7, `i2106.pdf p. 3`).
- The vehicle details (Part II) when a vehicle was used for the job.
- A **rural mail carrier** does not use the form: "If you were a rural mail carrier, do not use Form 2106" (`i2106.pdf
  p. 2`).

| Reimbursements already in W-2 box 1 | Not on line 7 (they are already wages) | `i2106.pdf p. 3` |
| Educator expenses already on Schedule 1 line 11 | Not on line 4 | `i2106.pdf p. 2` |
| Meals, taxes, interest | Not on line 4; meals on line 5; taxes / interest see Schedule A and Schedule 1-A | `i2106.pdf p. 2` |
| State and local personal property tax on the vehicle; qualified passenger-vehicle loan interest | Schedule A line 5c; Schedule 1-A | `i2106.pdf p. 5` |
| Commuting | Not deductible | `i2106.pdf p. 2, p. 4` |
| Entertainment, membership dues, facilities paid after 2017 | Not deductible (§274) | `i2106.pdf p. 2` |
| Reservist travel of 100 miles or less from home | Not an adjustment to income ("You can't deduct expenses of travel that doesn't take you more than 100 miles from home as an adjustment to gross income") | `p463.pdf p. 51` |
| Statutory employee expenses | Schedule C | `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 25` |

## 6. How to figure it, step by step (every line of the 2025 form)

**Before you begin — who must file.** The flowchart on `i2106.pdf p. 1` asks: (A) were you one of the four groups;
(B) did you have job-related business expenses; (C) were you reimbursed for any (counting only reimbursements not in
W-2 box 1); (D) are you claiming vehicle, travel, transportation, meals or entertainment expenses; (E)/(G) did you use
a vehicle that was used for business in a prior year with actual expenses or non-straight-line depreciation; (F) are
deductible expenses more than reimbursements. If you were reimbursed and expenses do not exceed reimbursements,
"Don't file Form 2106". "If line 4 is your only entry, don't complete Form 2106 unless you are claiming" the
performing-artist, fee-basis or impairment-related expenses (`i2106.pdf p. 2`).

**Part I, Step 1 — Enter your expenses** (column A other than meals, column B meals):

1. **Line 1 — Vehicle expense** from Part II line 22 (standard mileage) or line 29 (actual). A rural mail carrier's
   qualified reimbursement is treated as the expense (`i2106.pdf p. 2`).
2. **Line 2 — Parking fees, tolls, and transportation** (trains, buses, etc.) "that didn't involve overnight travel or
   commuting to and from work" (`f2106.pdf p. 1`).
3. **Line 3 — Travel expense while away from home overnight**: lodging, airfare, car rental — "Don't include meals".
   Only away from the tax home; no deduction for temporary employment of more than 1 year (except certified federal
   crime investigators); incidental expenses optionally $5 a day when no meals were paid (`i2106.pdf p. 2`).
4. **Line 4 — Other business expenses** not on lines 1–3, without meals, taxes or interest (`i2106.pdf p. 2`).
5. **Line 5 — Meals**: actual cost or the standard meal allowance (federal M&IE rate; rates before October 1, 2025 may
   be used for the whole year for travel within the United States, used consistently) (`i2106.pdf p. 3`).
6. **Line 6 — Total expenses**: column A = lines 1–4; column B = line 5.

**Step 2 — Reimbursements:**

7. **Line 7 — Reimbursements received from employer** (or a third party) for Step 1 expenses, including W-2 box 12
   code L, **excluding** amounts in W-2 box 1. A single reimbursement covering meals and other expenses is split by the
   Reimbursement Allocation Worksheet: line 4 of the worksheet = meals ÷ total expenses, "rounded to three places";
   column B gets reimbursement × that ratio, column A the rest (`i2106.pdf p. 3`). If no reimbursement, skip line 7.

**Step 3 — Expenses to deduct:**

8. **Line 8** = line 6 − line 7 per column; if zero or less enter 0. If column A of line 7 exceeds column A of line 6,
   the excess is income on **Form 1040 line 1a** (`f2106.pdf p. 1`). "If both columns of line 8 are zero, you can't
   deduct employee business expenses. Stop here and attach Form 2106 to your return."
9. **Line 9** — column A: line 8; column B: line 8 × 50% (80% for an individual subject to the Department of
   Transportation hours-of-service limits, `p463.pdf p. 15, p. 50`).
10. **Line 10** = line 9 column A + line 9 column B. Enter on Schedule 1 line 12 for a reservist (only the part for
    travel more than 100 miles from home, up to the federal per diem and standard mileage rate plus parking, ferry
    fees and tolls), a qualified performing artist (performing-arts part) or a fee-basis official (part for that job);
    for impairment-related work expenses enter that part on Schedule A line 16 (`i2106.pdf p. 3–4`).

**Part II — Vehicle expenses.** Use the standard mileage rate in 2025 only if you owned the vehicle and used the rate in
its first year in service, or leased it and use the rate for the whole lease; "You can't use actual expenses for a
leased vehicle if you previously used the standard mileage rate"; not for five or more vehicles used at the same time
(`i2106.pdf p. 4–5`). One column per vehicle; a second page 2 for more than two vehicles.

*Section A — General information*

11. **Line 11** — date placed in service (date business use began, for a converted personal car).
12. **Line 12** — total miles in 2025 (after the change to business use if no earlier records).
13. **Line 13** — business miles, without commuting.
14. **Line 14** — business-use percentage = line 13 ÷ line 12 (× months of business use ÷ 12 after a conversion).
15. **Line 15** — average daily round-trip commuting distance (temporary work locations, out-of-area travel and
    home-as-principal-place-of-business travel are not commuting, `i2106.pdf p. 4–5`).
16. **Line 16** — commuting miles (days commuted × line 15 if actual miles are unknown).
17. **Line 17** — other miles = line 12 − (line 13 + line 16).
18. **Lines 18–21** — Yes/No: vehicle available for personal use off duty; another vehicle available; evidence to
    support the deduction; is it written.

*Section B — Standard mileage rate*

22. **Line 22** = line 13 × $0.70; also enter on line 1 (`f2106.pdf p. 2`). Parking and tolls are added on line 2.

*Section C — Actual expenses*

23. **Line 23** — gasoline, oil, repairs, insurance, tires, license plates; not personal property tax or loan interest.
24. **Line 24a** — rentals; **24b** — lease inclusion amount (leases of 30 days or more when the fair market value
    exceeded $62,000 for a 2025 lease; computed with Pub. 463); **24c** = 24a − 24b.
25. **Line 25** — value of an employer-provided vehicle, only if 100% of its annual lease value was in W-2 box 1.
26. **Line 26** = lines 23 + 24c + 25.
27. **Line 27** = line 26 × line 14.
28. **Line 28** — depreciation: line 38, or Form 4562 lines 28 + 29 allocable to the vehicle.
29. **Line 29** = lines 27 + 28; also enter on line 1.

*Section D — Depreciation of vehicles (owned vehicles on actual expenses)*

30. **Line 30** — cost or other basis (not reduced by earlier depreciation; reduced by casualty loss, clean-fuel
    deduction, gas-guzzler tax and vehicle credits; increased by sales tax and improvements; the smaller of adjusted
    basis or fair market value for a converted personal vehicle) (`i2106.pdf p. 5`).
31. **Line 31** — §179 deduction and special depreciation allowance for this year. §179 only if 2025 is the first
    year and line 14 is more than 50%; SUV cost limited to $31,300; the special allowance (40% for property acquired
    before January 20, 2025, 100% for property acquired after January 19, 2025) by the Worksheet for the Special
    Depreciation Allowance; §179 limited to income from the job and other active businesses (`i2106.pdf p. 5–6`).
32. **Line 32** = line 30 × line 14, less §179 and special allowance claimed this year or earlier.
33. **Line 33** — method and percentage from the chart (`i2106.pdf p. 7`): placed in service Jan. 1 – Sept. 30, 2025:
    200 DB 20.0% / 150 DB 15.0% / SL 10.0%; Oct. 1 – Dec. 31, 2025: 5.0% / 3.75% / 2.5%. Declining-balance columns only
    if business use is more than 50%; straight line if the standard rate was used in the first year.
34. **Line 34** = line 32 × line 33 (sale during the year: 50% for vehicles placed in service in Jan.–Sept. after
    2019, or the month-of-disposal percentage 12.5% / 37.5% / 62.5% / 87.5%) (`i2106.pdf p. 7–8`).
35. **Line 35** = lines 31 + 34.
36. **Line 36** — the §280F limit: placed in service in 2025, first year $20,200 with the special allowance or $12,200
    without; skip lines 36–37 for a vehicle not subject to the limits (`i2106.pdf p. 8`).
37. **Line 37** = line 36 × line 14.
38. **Line 38** = smaller of line 35 or line 37 (line 35 if lines 36–37 skipped); also enter on line 28.

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Reservist | Only travel more than 100 miles from home; capped at federal per diem (lodging, M&IE) and the standard mileage rate plus parking, ferry fees, tolls; deductible "whether or not you itemize" | `i2106.pdf p. 3–4`; `p463.pdf p. 51` |
| Qualified performing artist | All four: (1) services in the performing arts as an employee for at least two employers; (2) wages of $200 or more from at least two of them; (3) performing-arts expenses more than 10% of gross income from the performing arts; (4) AGI of $16,000 or less before these expenses. Married: joint return unless apart all year; (1)–(3) each spouse separately, (4) combined AGI | `i2106.pdf p. 4`; `p463.pdf p. 51` |
| Fee-basis official | Expenses for services in that job | `i2106.pdf p. 4` |
| Impairment-related work expenses | To Schedule A line 16 (itemized), not subject to the 2% floor | `i2106.pdf p. 4`; `p463.pdf p. 51`; `i1040sca_2025.pdf p. 12` |
| Rural mail carrier | Do not use Form 2106 | `i2106.pdf p. 2` |
| Two or more activities using the standard mileage rate | A separate form or schedule per business | `i2106.pdf p. 5` |
| Business use drops to 50% or less in a later year | Recapture on Form 4797 Part IV | `i2106.pdf p. 7` |
| Election out of the special allowance | Statement attached; applies to the whole property class | `i2106.pdf p. 6` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- Each spouse who qualifies completes **their own** Form 2106 (the form carries one name and SSN, `f2106.pdf p. 1`);
  the line 10 amounts are added on Schedule 1 line 12.
- Performing artist: a married person must file jointly unless the spouses lived apart for all of 2025; tests (1)–(3)
  per spouse, test (4) on combined AGI (`i2106.pdf p. 4`).

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Line 10** → **Schedule 1 line 12** (reservist, performing artist, fee-basis official) → Schedule 1 line 26 →
  Form 1040 line 10 → line 11 (AGI).
- **Line 10** (impairment-related part) → **Schedule A line 16** → Form 1040 line 12e when itemizing.
- **Line 8 excess** (column A) → **Form 1040 line 1a** (income).
- Through AGI: every AGI-based figure (performing-artist test (4) itself uses AGI before the deduction).

## 11. Worked example

Sam Sample (000-00-0001), Army Reserve, drives to a two-week training site 300 miles from home in 2025 and stays in
lodging there. Records:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Business miles 600 (own car, standard rate used since the first year) | 600 × $0.70 = $420 (Part II line 22 → line 1) |
| Tolls on the way | $30 (line 2) |
| Lodging, within the federal per diem lodging rate | $800 (line 3) |
| Meals, within the M&IE rate | $400 (line 5) |
| Unit reimbursement for lodging, not in W-2 box 1 | $200 (line 7, column A) |

1. Line 6: column A = 420 + 30 + 800 = **$1,250**; column B = **$400**.
2. Line 7: column A $200; column B $0.
3. Line 8: column A 1,250 − 200 = **$1,050**; column B **$400**.
4. Line 9: column A $1,050; column B 400 × 50% = **$200**.
5. Line 10 = 1,050 + 200 = **$1,250**. All of it is for travel more than 100 miles from home and within the per diem
   and mileage limits, so **Schedule 1 line 12 = $1,250**, and Form 2106 is attached.

If the unit had instead paid $1,400 for column A expenses, line 8 column A would be 0 and the $150 excess would be
income on Form 1040 line 1a.
