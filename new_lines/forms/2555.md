## 1. At a glance

A U.S. citizen or resident alien whose **tax home** is in a foreign country and who passes either the **bona fide
residence test** or the **physical presence test** may exclude foreign earned income from gross income — up to
**$130,000** for 2025, prorated for a part-year qualifying period — and may also exclude (employer-paid) or deduct
(self-paid) a **housing amount**: reasonable foreign housing expenses above a base amount, limited by a location
limit. The exclusions are elections, figured per person (each spouse files their own Form 2555). The total exclusion
(line 45) goes on **Schedule 1 line 8d as a negative amount**; the housing deduction (line 50) goes on **Schedule 1
line 24j**. The income itself stays where it is reported (W-2 wages on Form 1040 line 1a). Tax on the rest of the
income is figured with the **Foreign Earned Income Tax Worksheet**, which taxes it at the rates that would apply if
the excluded amount were still in income.

```text
line 27 = foreign earned income (Part IV: wages + business share + noncash + allowances + other − §119 meals/lodging)
line 30 = min(qualified housing expenses (28), housing limit (29b: $39,000, or Notice 2025-16 locality / daily))
line 33 = line 30 − base housing (32: $56.99 × qualifying days; $20,800 for 365)           (≤ 0 → stop VI and IX)
line 36 = housing exclusion = line 33 × (employer-provided amounts 34 ÷ line 27, max 1.000), ≤ line 34
line 40 = $130,000 × (qualifying days ÷ days in 2025)
line 42 = FEIE = min(line 40, line 27 − line 36)
line 45 = (line 36 + line 42) − deductions allocable to the excluded income (44)   →  Schedule 1 line 8d (negative)
line 50 = housing deduction = min(line 33 − line 36, line 27 − line 43) + 2024 carryover  →  Schedule 1 line 24j
```

Terms used below:

- **Tax home** — your regular or principal place of business, employment or post of duty; it must be in a foreign
  country, and you cannot have your abode in the United States (combat-zone exception).
- **Bona fide residence test** — a U.S. citizen (or a resident alien who is a citizen / national of a treaty country)
  who is a bona fide resident of a foreign country for an uninterrupted period that includes an entire tax year.
- **Physical presence test** — a U.S. citizen or resident alien present in a foreign country for at least 330 full days
  in any 12 consecutive months.
- **Qualifying period** — the days during which you meet the tax home test and one of the two tests.
- **FEIE** — foreign earned income exclusion (line 42). **Housing exclusion** (line 36) — for employer-provided
  amounts; **housing deduction** (line 50) — for self-employed / self-paid amounts.

## 2. The IRS wording

**Purpose** (`i2555.pdf p. 1`): "If you qualify, you can use Form 2555 to figure your foreign earned income exclusion
and your housing exclusion or deduction. You cannot exclude or deduct more than the amount of your foreign earned
income for the year." What's New: "For 2025, the maximum exclusion amount has increased to $130,000."

**Who qualifies** (`i2555.pdf p. 1`): "You may qualify … if both of the following apply. • You meet the tax home test
(discussed later). • You meet either the bona fide residence test or the physical presence test." "Income from working
abroad as an employee of the U.S. Government does not qualify for either of the exclusions or the housing deduction.
Don't file Form 2555." "You aren't considered to have a tax home in a foreign country for any period during which your
abode is in the United States, unless you are serving in support of the U.S. Armed Forces in an area designated as a
combat zone."

**Choosing the exclusion** (`i2555.pdf p. 2–3`): "Once you choose to claim the exclusion(s), that choice remains in
effect for that year and all future years unless it is revoked. … If you revoke your choice, you can't claim the
exclusion(s) for your next 5 tax years without the approval of the IRS."

**Consequences** (`i2555.pdf p. 3`): "You can't take the additional child tax credit if you claim either of the
exclusions or the housing deduction." "You can't take the earned income credit if you claim either of the exclusions
or the housing deduction." "You can't take a credit or deduction for foreign income taxes paid or accrued on income that
is excluded." "If you claim either of the exclusions, special rules apply in figuring the amount of your IRA
deduction."

**Tax on income not excluded** (`i2555.pdf p. 3`): "If you claim either of the exclusions or the housing deduction,
you must figure the tax on your nonexcluded income using the tax rates that would have applied had you not claimed the
exclusions. See the Instructions for Form 1040 and complete the Foreign Earned Income Tax Worksheet … When figuring
your alternative minimum tax on Form 6251, you must use the Foreign Earned Income Tax Worksheet in the Instructions for
Form 6251."

**Line 45** (`i2555.pdf p. 7`): "Enter the amount from line 45 on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 8d. Reduce the other
items of additional income by the negative amount on line 8d …" **Schedule 1 line 8d** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 91`):
"Enter the amount of your foreign earned income and housing exclusion from Form 2555, line 45. Enter the amount in the
preprinted parentheses (as a negative number)." **Line 24j** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 100`): "Enter the housing
deduction from Form 2555."

**Line 16** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 36`): "If you claimed the foreign earned income exclusion, housing exclusion, or
housing deduction on Form 2555, you must figure your tax using the Foreign Earned Income Tax Worksheet." The Schedule D
Tax Worksheet and the Qualified Dividends and Capital Gain Tax Worksheet paragraphs both end: "But if you are filing
Form 2555, you must use the Foreign Earned Income Tax Worksheet instead."

## 3. What goes on this form

- Wages, salaries, professional fees and other pay for personal services **performed in a foreign country** during the
  qualifying period, received in 2025 (cash basis), including noncash income and allowances or reimbursements
  (`i2555.pdf p. 4`).
- A reasonable share of business profit for services where capital is also material (≤ 30% of net profit after the
  employer-equivalent part of SE tax; the 30% rule does not apply when capital is not a factor) (`i2555.pdf p. 4`).
- Pay received in 2025 for 2024 services, to the extent it would have been excludable in 2024 (by statement, on the
  line 45 dotted line) (`i2555.pdf p. 4`).
- Qualified foreign housing expenses of you and your spouse and dependents living with you, and of a second foreign
  household kept because of adverse conditions (`i2555.pdf p. 4–5`).

## 6. How to fill it in, line by line

Every line of the 2025 form (`f2555.pdf`, docs\IRS-Forms), with its instruction (`i2555.pdf`).

**Part I — General Information**

1. Line 1: foreign address including country (`i2555.pdf p. 3`). Line 2: occupation. Line 3: employer's name.
   Line 4a / 4b: employer's U.S. / foreign address. Line 5: employer type (a foreign entity, b U.S. company, c self,
   d foreign affiliate of a U.S. company, e other).
2. Line 6a: last year Form 2555 / 2555-EZ was filed; 6b: check if never filed to claim an exclusion; 6c / 6d: any
   revocation and its type and year (a revocation bars the exclusion for 5 years without IRS consent, `i2555.pdf p. 3`).
3. Line 7: country of citizenship / nationality. Line 8a / 8b: separate foreign residence for the family because of
   adverse conditions, with city, country and days. Line 9: tax home(s) and dates established.
   "You must complete either Part II or Part III of Form 2555, but not both parts" (`i2555.pdf p. 3`).

**Part II — Bona fide residence test** (only U.S. citizens and resident aliens who are citizens / nationals of treaty
countries)

4. Line 10: dates bona fide residence began and ended ("Continues" if still a resident) (`i2555.pdf p. 3`). The period
   must be uninterrupted and include an entire tax year; once met, the qualifying period can start before and end
   after that year.
5. Line 11: kind of living quarters. Line 12a / 12b: family living with you, who and when.
6. Line 13a / 13b: statement of nonresidence to the foreign authorities; required to pay their income tax? "If you
   answered 'Yes' to 13a and 'No' to 13b, you don't qualify as a bona fide resident."
7. Line 14: days in the United States or its territories, with income earned there on business (column (d)); that
   income is **not** included in Part IV.
8. Line 15a–15e: contract terms, visa type, visa limits, U.S. home kept and its occupants.

**Part III — Physical presence test**

9. Line 16: the 12-month period (both dates; 365 / 366 days, part of it in 2025; "Don't enter 'Continues'")
   (`i2555.pdf p. 4`). The test is "at least 330 full days during any period of 12 months in a row. A full day means
   the 24-hour period that starts at midnight" (`i2555.pdf p. 3`).
10. Line 17: principal country of employment. Line 18: travel during the period — country, dates, full days present,
    days in the U.S. on business, and income earned in the U.S. on business (column (f), not in Part IV).

**Part IV — All taxpayers: 2025 foreign earned income** (U.S. dollars at the exchange rate when received)

11. Line 19: wages, salaries, bonuses, commissions. Line 20a / 20b: allowable share of income for personal services in
    a business / partnership (the 30% rule, `i2555.pdf p. 4`). Line 21a–21d: noncash income (home, meals, car, other).
    Line 22a–22f: allowances (cost of living, family, education, home leave, quarters, other); 22g = total. Line 23:
    other foreign earned income.
12. Line 24 = lines 19 through 21d + 22g + 23. Line 25: meals and lodging excludable under §119 (employer's convenience,
    business premises, required lodging) (`i2555.pdf p. 4`). Line 26 = line 24 − line 25 = **2025 foreign earned
    income**.

**Part V — All taxpayers**

13. Line 27 = line 26. Claiming the housing exclusion or deduction? Yes → Part VI; No → Part VII.

**Part VI — Housing exclusion and/or deduction**

14. Line 28: qualified housing expenses for the qualifying period (`i2555.pdf p. 4–5`; see §4 for what is excluded).
15. Line 29a: location, only if listed in Notice 2025-16. Line 29b: limit on housing expenses — "$39,000 (30% of
    $130,000) if your qualifying period includes all of 2025 (or $106.85 per day if the number of days … is less than
    365)", or the Notice 2025-16 locality figure (full-year, or daily × days); with several locations, add a worksheet
    per location (`i2555.pdf p. 5–6`, Limit on Housing Expenses Worksheet).
16. Line 30 = smaller of line 28 or line 29b.
17. Line 31: days in the qualifying period that fall within 2025 (`i2555.pdf p. 6`).
18. Line 32: "Multiply $56.99 by the number of days on line 31. If 365 is entered on line 31, enter $20,800 here."
19. Line 33 = line 30 − line 32. "If the result is zero or less, don't complete the rest of this part or any of Part
    IX."
20. Line 34: employer-provided amounts (wages, in-kind compensation, rent paid by the employer, reimbursements); if all
    foreign earned income is self-employment income, enter 0 on line 36 and skip 34–35 (`i2555.pdf p. 6`).
21. Line 35 = line 34 ÷ line 27, decimal rounded to at least three places, not more than 1.000.
22. Line 36: **housing exclusion** = line 33 × line 35, not more than line 34.

**Part VII — Foreign earned income exclusion** (figured separately for each spouse; community property splitting does
not affect it, `i2555.pdf p. 6`)

23. Line 37: $130,000. Line 38: line 31 (if Part VI done) or the qualifying days in 2025.
24. Line 39: 1.000 if line 38 equals the days in the year; otherwise line 38 ÷ days in the year (at least three places).
25. Line 40 = line 37 × line 39. Line 41 = line 27 − line 36. Line 42: **FEIE** = smaller of line 40 or line 41.

**Part VIII — Total exclusions**

26. Line 43 = line 36 + line 42.
27. Line 44: deductions allowed in figuring AGI that are allocable to the excluded income (for example the deductible
    part of SE tax and Schedule C expenses), only those "definitely related"; attach the computation
    (`i2555.pdf p. 6–7`). Items related to partly excluded income are prorated by excluded ÷ total foreign earned
    income; the §119 exclusion and the housing deduction are not "definitely related".
28. Line 45 = line 43 − line 44 → **Schedule 1 line 8d** (negative). Complete the Foreign Earned Income Tax Worksheet.

**Part IX — Housing deduction** (only if line 33 > line 36 **and** line 27 > line 43)

29. Line 46 = line 33 − line 36. Line 47 = line 27 − line 43. Line 48 = smaller of 46 and 47.
30. Line 49: carryover from 2024 (Housing Deduction Carryover Worksheet: smaller of [2024 line 46 − 2024 line 48] and
    [2025 line 47 − 2025 line 48]); unused excess carries one year only (`i2555.pdf p. 7`).
31. Line 50: **housing deduction** = line 48 + line 49 → **Schedule 1 line 24j**. Complete the Foreign Earned Income
    Tax Worksheet.

**Foreign Earned Income Tax Worksheet — Form 1040 line 16** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 37`; not completed when line 15
is zero)

1. Line 1: Form 1040 line 15.
2. Line 2a: your (and your spouse's if joint) Form 2555 **lines 45 and 50**. 2b: itemized deductions or exclusions you
   couldn't claim because related to excluded income. 2c = 2a − 2b (not below 0).
3. Line 3 = line 1 + line 2c.
4. Line 4: tax on line 3 with the Tax Table, Tax Computation Worksheet, QDCG Worksheet, Schedule D Tax Worksheet or
   Form 8615, whichever applies (never a second FEITW). If the QDCG or Schedule D Tax Worksheet is used, enter line 3
   on its line 1 and work through its line 4 (line 10 for Schedule D); **capital gain excess** = that line − Form 1040
   line 15. If positive, complete the worksheet again with its line 3 (Schedule D: line 9) reduced by the excess, then
   its line 2 (line 6) by any rest, Schedule D line 18 reduced, and the excess entered as a loss on line 16 of the
   Unrecaptured Section 1250 Gain Worksheet.
5. Line 5: tax on line 2c (Tax Table under $100,000, else Tax Computation Worksheet).
6. Line 6 = line 4 − line 5 (not below 0) → Form 1040 line 16.

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- Each spouse who qualifies and chooses the exclusion completes their own Form 2555 and Part VII; the joint return
  excludes the sum (`i2555.pdf p. 6`).
- **Same household, joint return:** figure housing jointly (one spouse claims, aggregating both spouses' expenses,
  using the shorter qualifying period if they differ) or separately (each with their own base; no expense counted
  twice). **Separate returns, same household:** housing must be figured separately (`i2555.pdf p. 5`).
- **Separate households:** each may claim their own household only if the tax homes are not within reasonable
  commuting distance and neither household is within commuting distance of the other's tax home; otherwise only one
  spouse claims (`i2555.pdf p. 5`).
- Community property laws do not change the exclusion amount (`i2555.pdf p. 6`).
- The Foreign Earned Income Tax Worksheet adds both spouses' lines 45 and 50 on a joint return
  (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 37`).

## 11. Worked example

Jordan Sample (000-00-0007), single, U.S. citizen, has been a bona fide resident of Germany since 2022 (line 10:
"Continues") and works there all year for a German company that issues no W-2. 2025 salary $150,000 (no other
allowances). Rent and utilities $45,000 in a city not listed in Notice 2025-16. U.S. bank interest $30,000. Standard
deduction.

| Line | Figure | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| 19 = 24 = 26 = 27 | salary | $150,000 |
| 28 | housing expenses | $45,000 |
| 29b | not a listed location, 365 days | $39,000 |
| 30 | min(45,000, 39,000) | $39,000 |
| 31, 32 | 365 days | 365; $20,800 |
| 33 | 39,000 − 20,800 | $18,200 |
| 34, 35 | all employer pay; 150,000 ÷ 150,000 | $150,000; 1.000 |
| 36 | housing exclusion | **$18,200** |
| 37–40 | $130,000 × 1.000 | $130,000 |
| 41 | 150,000 − 18,200 | $131,800 |
| 42 | FEIE = min(130,000, 131,800) | **$130,000** |
| 43, 44, 45 | 18,200 + 130,000; none allocable | **$148,200** → Schedule 1 line 8d (−148,200) |
| Part IX | line 33 (18,200) is not more than line 36 (18,200) | no housing deduction |

Form 1040: line 1h $150,000 (foreign pay, no W-2); line 2b $30,000; Schedule 1 line 8d −$148,200 → line 8
−$148,200; line 9 $31,800; AGI $31,800; standard deduction $15,750; line 15 **$16,050**.

Foreign Earned Income Tax Worksheet: line 1 $16,050; 2a $148,200; 2b 0; 2c $148,200; line 3 $164,250; line 4 (Tax
Computation Worksheet, single, 24% row: × 0.24 − $7,153.00, `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 80`) = 39,420 − 7,153 = $32,267;
line 5 = 148,200 × 0.24 − 7,153 = $28,415; line 6 = **$3,852** → Form 1040 line 16. (Without the worksheet, tax on
$16,050 alone would be far lower; the worksheet keeps the remaining $16,050 in the 24% bracket.)

Part-year variant: had Jordan's bona fide residence begun August 14, 2025 (and continued through January 31, 2027 —
`i2555.pdf p. 6`), line 31 / 38 = 140 days; line 39 = 140 ÷ 365 = 0.384 (three places) and line 40 = $49,920 (with
more decimals, 0.38356 → $49,863 — both are allowed by "rounded to at least three places").
