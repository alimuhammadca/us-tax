# Form 4684 — Casualties and Thefts

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\forms\4684.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\forms\4684.md`. How a casualty gain enters Schedule D is in
`C:\us-tax\new_lines\forms\schedule-d.md` (lines 4 / 11, 28% Rate Gain Worksheet line 3); how a business casualty
enters Form 4797 is in the Form 4797 files. This file covers every line of the 2025 Form 4684: Section A
(personal-use property), Section B (business and income-producing property), Section C (Ponzi-type schemes) and
Section D (election to deduct a disaster loss in the preceding year), plus Worksheet 1-1.

The 2025 Form 4684 itself is in `docs\IRS-Forms\f4684.pdf` (not in `docs\books`); cited as `f4684.pdf p. N`. Its
instructions are `i4684.pdf` in `docs\books`.

## 1. At a glance

Form 4684 figures the gain or loss when property is damaged, destroyed or stolen. Each property's loss is the
smaller of its basis and its drop in value, less insurance or other reimbursement; a reimbursement above basis is a
gain. **Personal-use property** (Section A) is deductible for 2025 only for a **federally declared disaster** (or up to
personal casualty gains), and only after **$100 per casualty** (**$500** for a *qualified disaster loss*) and, except
for qualified disaster losses, **10% of AGI**. **Business and income-producing property** (Section B) has no $100 or
10% reduction; it nets into Form 4797 (business), Schedule A line 16 (income-producing) or Schedule D.

```text
Section A, per casualty (one Form 4684 through line 12 each), per property column A–D:
  line 4  = line 3 − line 2   when reimbursement (3) > basis (2)            (gain; skip 5–9)
  line 7  = line 5 − line 6   (FMV before − FMV after)
  line 8  = min(line 2, line 7)
  line 9  = max(0, line 8 − line 3)
  line 10 = Σ line 9;  line 11 = $100 ($500 qualified disaster);  line 12 = max(0, line 10 − line 11)
Section A, once for all casualties:
  line 13 = Σ line 4 of all Forms 4684
  line 14 = Σ line 12 of all Forms 4684   (Worksheet 1-1 when some losses are not federally declared disaster losses)
  line 15 = line 13 − line 14 if > 0  → Schedule D (short-term → line 4, long-term → line 11); stop
          = 0 if equal; stop
          = min(line 14 − line 13, line 12 of the qualified-disaster Forms) if a $500 loss exists → Schedule A line 16
          = 0 otherwise
  line 16 = line 14 − (line 13 + line 15);  line 17 = 10% × Form 1040 line 11b;  line 18 = max(0, 16 − 17) → Schedule A line 15
Section B Part I (per casualty, per property): 22 gain; 25 = 23 − 24; 26 = min(20, 25) (= 20 if destroyed/stolen);
  27 = max(0, 26 − 21); 28 = Σ 27
Section B Part II: held ≤ 1 year (29–32): 31 = (b)(i) + (c) → Form 4797 line 14; 32 = (b)(ii) → Schedule A line 16
  held > 1 year (33–39): 36 = gains, 37 = losses;  37 > 36 → 38a = (b)(i) + 36 → Form 4797 line 14, 38b = (b)(ii) → Schedule A line 16
                                                    37 ≤ 36 → 39 = 36 + 37 → Form 4797 line 3 (section 1231)
```

Terms used below:

- **Casualty or theft** — losses "from fire, storm, shipwreck, or other casualty, or theft" (`i4684.pdf p. 3`); a
  scam loss is a theft only if it is "criminal conduct classified as theft under applicable state law"
  (`i4684.pdf p. 10`). The fuller definition is in Pub. 547, which is not in `docs\books`.
- **Personal-use property** — property not used in a trade or business or for income-producing purposes
  (Section A heading, `f4684.pdf p. 1`).
- **Income-producing property** — "property held for investment, such as stocks, notes, bonds, gold, silver, vacant
  lots, and works of art" (`i4684.pdf p. 11`).
- **Federally declared disaster** — "a disaster determined by the President of the United States to warrant
  assistance by the federal government under the … Stafford Act … includes (a) a major disaster declaration or (b) an
  emergency declaration" (`i4684.pdf p. 2`).
- **Federal casualty loss** — a casualty or theft loss of personal-use property attributable to a federally declared
  disaster (`i4684.pdf p. 2`).
- **Disaster loss** — a loss attributable to a federally declared disaster in an area eligible for public or
  individual assistance; it may be claimed for any property and may be elected into the preceding year
  (`i4684.pdf p. 2, 4`).
- **Qualified disaster loss** — a personal-use loss attributable to one of the listed major disasters (for 2025: a
  **major disaster** declared between January 1, 2020 and September 2, 2025 with an incident period that began
  December 28, 2019 – July 4, 2025 and ended by August 3, 2025; not COVID-19-only declarations); $500 instead of $100,
  no 10% of AGI, and claimable with the standard deduction (`i4684.pdf p. 2–3, 5`).
- **FMV** — fair market value: "the price at which the property would be sold between a willing buyer and a willing
  seller" (`i4684.pdf p. 8`).
- **Section 1231** — the netting of gains and losses on business property held more than one year on Form 4797
  Part I.

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 4684 (2025)** (`f4684.pdf p. 1–3`), heading: "Attach to your tax return. Use a separate Form 4684 for each
casualty or theft." Section A heading: "For tax years beginning after 2017, if you are an individual, casualty or theft
losses of personal-use property are deductible only if the loss is attributable to a federally declared disaster. You
must use a separate Form 4684 (through line 12) for each casualty or theft event involving personal-use property."

**Purpose** (`i4684.pdf p. 2`): "Use Form 4684 to report gains and losses from casualties and thefts. Attach Form 4684
to your tax return."

**Which sections** (`i4684.pdf p. 6–7`): "Use Section A to figure casualty or theft gains and losses for property that
isn't used in a trade or business or for income-producing purposes. … Use Section B to figure casualty or theft gains
and losses for property that is used in a trade or business or for income-producing purposes. If property is used
partly in a trade or business and partly for personal purposes, such as a personal home with a rental unit, figure
the personal part in Section A and the business part in Section B." Section C: Ponzi-type schemes under Revenue
Procedure 2009-20; Section D: the preceding-year election.

**The 2025 limit** (`i4684.pdf p. 1`): "casualty or theft losses of personal-use property not connected with a trade or
business or a transaction entered into for profit are deductible only if the loss is attributable to a federally
declared disaster. … Personal casualty and theft losses attributable to a federally declared disaster are subject to
the $100 per casualty and 10% of your adjusted gross income (AGI) reductions unless they are attributable to a
qualified disaster loss. Personal casualty and theft losses attributable to a qualified disaster loss are not subject
to the 10% of the AGI reduction and the $100 reduction is increased to $500."

**The gain exception** (`i4684.pdf p. 1–2`): "An exception … applies if you have personal casualty gains for the tax
year. In this case, you will reduce your personal casualty gains by any casualty losses not attributable to a
federally declared disaster. Any excess gain is used to reduce losses from a federally declared disaster."

**Schedule A** (`i1040sca_2025.pdf p. 11`): line 15 — "Only enter the amount from Form 4684, line 18, on line 15.
Don't enter a net qualified disaster loss from Form 4684, line 15, on line 15. Instead, enter that amount, if any, on
line 16." Line 16 lists "Casualty and theft losses of income-producing property (including losses from financial
scams) from Form 4684, lines 32 and 38b, or Form 4797, line 18a."

## 6. How to figure it, line by line

### Top of Section A — federally declared disaster box

"If the casualty or theft loss is attributable to a federally declared disaster, check here and enter the DR- or EM-
declaration number assigned by FEMA" (`f4684.pdf p. 1`); format "DR" or "EM" and four digits, e.g. "DR-4865"
(`i4684.pdf p. 7`).

### Section A — one Form 4684 per casualty (lines 1–12)

1. **Line 1** — type of property, location (city, state, ZIP) and date acquired, one line per property A–D. With the
   disaster box checked, "enter the ZIP code for the property most affected on the line for Property A". Use extra
   sheets for more than four properties (`i4684.pdf p. 7`).
2. **Line 2** — "Cost or other basis usually means original cost plus improvements. Subtract any postponed gain from
   the sale of a previous main home" (`i4684.pdf p. 7`).
3. **Line 3** — insurance or other reimbursement "you received or expect to receive for each property. Include your
   insurance coverage whether or not you are filing a claim for reimbursement" (example: $2,000 car, $500 deductible,
   no claim filed → $1,500) (`i4684.pdf p. 7`). A lump-sum reimbursement is divided by FMV; grants count only if they
   must be used to repair or replace (`i4684.pdf p. 7–8`). For a destroyed main home with a partly excludable gain,
   reduce line 3 by the exclusion and attach a statement (`i4684.pdf p. 8`).
4. **Line 4** — "Gain from casualty or theft. If line 3 is more than line 2, enter the difference here and skip lines 5
   through 9 for that column." If you chose not to claim insurance, figure the gain "only from the amount of
   reimbursement you actually received … but don't enter less than zero"; a reimbursement received after the year is
   a gain in the year received (`i4684.pdf p. 8`).
5. **Lines 5 and 6** — FMV immediately before and after; after a theft FMV is zero if not recovered; adjust the after
   value for a general market decline; repairs can evidence the loss only if the five listed conditions are met;
   personal real estate is one item (buildings, trees, shrubs together), other items separately (`i4684.pdf p. 8`).
   With a Rev. Proc. 2018-08 safe harbor, leave 5 and 6 blank, enter the decrease on line 7 and attach a statement.
6. **Line 7** — line 5 − line 6.
7. **Line 8** — smaller of line 2 or line 7.
8. **Line 9** — line 8 − line 3; zero or less → 0.
9. **Line 10** — sum of line 9, columns A–D.
10. **Line 11** — "$100 ($500 if qualified disaster loss rules apply)". Instruction: "If you sustained a qualified
    disaster loss … add the amounts on line 4 of all Forms 4684. Compare the sum with the amount on line 10. If the
    amount on line 10 is larger, enter $500 … If the amount on line 10 is smaller, or if you are reporting a disaster
    loss, enter $100 and complete the remainder of the form without applying the special rules for qualified disaster
    losses" (`i4684.pdf p. 8`).
11. **Line 12** — line 10 − line 11; zero or less → 0. "Caution: Use only one Form 4684 for lines 13 through 18."

### Section A — once (lines 13–18)

12. **Line 13** — "Add the amounts on line 4 of all Forms 4684" (gains).
13. **Line 14** — "Add the amounts on line 12 of all Forms 4684", unless some losses are not attributable to a
    federally declared disaster; then **Worksheet 1-1** (`i4684.pdf p. 9`):

    | Worksheet 1-1 line | Instruction |
    |---|---|
    | 1 | line 12 of all Forms 4684 reporting losses **not** attributable to a federally declared disaster |
    | 2 | line 12 of all Forms 4684 reporting losses attributable to a federally declared disaster |
    | 3 | smaller of line 1 or Form 4684 line 13 |
    | 4 | line 2 + line 3 → Form 4684 line 14 |

14. **Line 15** (`f4684.pdf p. 1`; `i4684.pdf p. 9`):
    - Line 13 > line 14: the difference → Schedule D, split by holding period: "Combine your short-term gains with
      your short-term losses and include the net short-term gain or (loss) on Schedule D … line 4 … Combine your
      long-term gains with your long-term losses and include the net long-term gain or (loss) on Schedule D … line 11."
      Holding period starts the day after acquisition and includes the casualty day; inherited property is long-term.
      Stop.
    - Line 13 = line 14: 0; stop.
    - Line 13 < line 14 **with** qualified disaster losses subject to the $500 reduction: "Subtract line 13 from line
      14. Enter the smaller of this difference or the amount on line 12 of the Form 4684 listing those qualified
      disaster losses. The amount is your net qualified disaster loss" → Schedule A line 16 ("Net Qualified Disaster
      Loss"), itemizing or with the increased standard deduction. "Don't complete the rest of this section if all your
      personal casualty and theft losses are qualified disaster losses."
    - Line 13 < line 14 **without** such losses: 0; go to line 16.
15. **Line 16** — "Add lines 13 and 15. Subtract the result from line 14."
16. **Line 17** — "Enter 10% of your adjusted gross income from Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR, line 11b."
17. **Line 18** — line 16 − line 17; zero or less → 0 → **Schedule A line 15**.

### Increased standard deduction with a net qualified disaster loss (`i4684.pdf p. 5`; `i1040sca_2025.pdf p. 11`)

A non-itemizer: (1) enter Form 4684 line 15 on the dotted line next to Schedule A line 16 as "Net Qualified Disaster
Loss"; (2) enter the standard deduction there as "Standard Deduction Claimed With Qualified Disaster Loss"; (3) combine
them on Schedule A line 16 and on **Form 1040 line 12e**. "Do not enter an amount on any other line of Schedule A."
Form 6251: enter zero on line 2a and include the unincreased standard deduction on line 3 (`i4684.pdf p. 9`).

### Section B Part I — one per casualty (lines 19–28) (`f4684.pdf p. 2`; `i4684.pdf p. 10–11`)

18. **Line 19** — description, location and date acquired for each property; for a fraudulent investment arrangement
    without Section C, the name, TIN and address of the person who ran it.
19. **Line 20** — "Cost or adjusted basis usually means original cost plus improvements, minus depreciation allowed or
    allowable (including any section 179 expense deduction), amortization, depletion, etc." A partial MACRS
    disposition uses the adjusted basis of the disposed portion.
20. **Line 21** — reimbursement (same rules as line 3).
21. **Line 22** — gain if line 21 > line 20; "enter the difference here and on line 29 or line 34, column (c), except
    as provided in the instructions for line 33. Also, skip lines 23 through 27."
22. **Lines 23–25** — FMV before, after, and the decrease. "A casualty loss to business or income-producing property
    must be figured separately for each item" (a building and its trees separately).
23. **Line 26** — smaller of line 20 or line 25; "If the property was totally destroyed by casualty or lost from
    theft, enter on line 26 the amount from line 20."
24. **Line 27** — line 26 − line 21; zero or less → 0. A home used for business with Form 8829: enter Form 8829 line 35
    and "See Form 8829"; a rental home or business without Schedule C: section 280A(c)(5) statement.
25. **Line 28** — sum of line 27 → line 29 or 34, allocated by holding period; from Section C line 51 for a Ponzi loss
    (skip 19–27).

### Section B Part II — one, combining all Part I's

26. **Line 29** — casualties of property held **one year or less**, one row each: (a) name, (b)(i) loss on trade,
    business, rental or royalty property, (b)(ii) loss on income-producing property, (c) gain (line 22).
27. **Line 30** — totals of line 29.
28. **Line 31** — line 30 (b)(i) + (c) → **Form 4797 line 14**; "If Form 4797 isn't otherwise required, enter the
    amount from this line on your Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 4 and check the '4684' box" (`i4684.pdf p. 12`).
29. **Line 32** — line 30 (b)(ii) → **Schedule A line 16** (not property used as an employee).
30. **Line 33** — "Casualty or theft gains from Form 4797, line 32": a gain on depreciable property held more than one
    year that is subject to recapture goes through Form 4797 Part III first; the part not recaptured comes back here
    "instead of Form 4684, line 34" (`i4684.pdf p. 12`).
31. **Line 34** — casualties of property held **more than one year**, columns as line 29.
32. **Line 35** — total losses, (b)(i) and (b)(ii).
33. **Line 36** — total gains: line 33 + line 34 column (c).
34. **Line 37** — line 35 (b)(i) + (b)(ii).
35. **Line 38** — if the loss on line 37 is more than the gain on line 36: **38a** = line 35 (b)(i) + line 36 →
    **Form 4797 line 14** (or Schedule 1 line 4 with the "4684" box when Form 4797 is not otherwise required);
    **38b** = line 35 (b)(ii) → **Schedule A line 16**.
36. **Line 39** — if the loss on line 37 is less than or equal to the gain on line 36: line 36 + line 37 →
    **Form 4797 line 3** (section 1231 netting).

### Section C — Ponzi-type investment scheme (Rev. Proc. 2009-20) (`f4684.pdf p. 3`; `i4684.pdf p. 12–13`)

| Line | Instruction |
|---|---|
| 40 | Initial investment (excluding unrepaid borrowings from the responsible group, deducted fees, unreported income, fund-level investments) |
| 41 | Subsequent investments (including reinvestments) |
| 42 | Income from the arrangement reported in years before the discovery year |
| 43 | 40 + 41 + 42 |
| 44 | Withdrawals for all years |
| 45 | 43 − 44: total qualified investment |
| 46 | 0.95 with no potential third-party recovery; 0.75 with one |
| 47 | 46 × 45 |
| 48 | Actual recovery |
| 49 | Potential insurance / SIPC recovery |
| 50 | 48 + 49 |
| 51 | 47 − 50: deductible theft loss → Section B line 28 (skip 19–27), then Part II |

Part II of Section C: the required statements and declarations (name, TIN, address of the arrangement; written
documentation; qualified investor; no pursuit of third-party recovery when using 95%).

### Section D — election to deduct a federally declared disaster loss in the preceding year (`i4684.pdf p. 4, 13`)

Part I (election) is attached to the **preceding-year** return (for a 2025 disaster year, the 2024 return) by six months
after the regular due date of the disaster-year return (October 15, 2026 for a 2025 loss); Part II revokes a prior
election on an amended preceding-year return within 90 days after the election deadline. On a 2025 return Section D is
used to bring a **2026** disaster loss back into 2025.

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Postponing a gain (section 1033) | Buy similar property, or 80% control of a corporation owning it, within 2 years after the end of the first year the gain is realized; gain recognized only to the extent reimbursement exceeds replacement cost; limits for related-party replacement ($100,000 aggregate gain for individuals) | `i4684.pdf p. 3` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

The Form 4684 instructions have no separate rule for married couples: one Form 4684 per casualty on the return that
reports the property. On a joint return both spouses' casualties are on the return, the $100 applies per casualty
(not per spouse) and line 17 uses the joint AGI. On separate returns each spouse reports the property and loss that
is theirs; for jointly paid itemized expenses Schedule A refers to Pub. 504 ("If you and your spouse paid expenses
jointly and are filing separate returns for 2025, see Pub. 504 to figure the portion of joint expenses that you can
claim", `i1040sca_2025.pdf p. 1`). Pub. 504 / 547 are not in `docs\books`, so the exact split of a jointly owned
property's loss is not quoted here.

## 11. Worked example

**Lee Sample** (000-00-0021), single, Form 1040 line 11b AGI **$80,000**.

*Casualty 1 — flood in a federally declared disaster (DR-0000), declared October 2025 (after September 2, 2025, so not a
qualified disaster).*

| Line | Car (A) | Furniture (B) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 basis | 20,000 | 6,000 |
| 3 insurance | 5,000 | 0 |
| 5 FMV before | 15,000 | 4,500 |
| 6 FMV after | 3,000 | 500 |
| 7 | 12,000 | 4,000 |
| 8 = min(2, 7) | 12,000 | 4,000 |
| 9 = 8 − 3 | 7,000 | 4,000 |

Line 10 = 11,000; line 11 = 100; **line 12 = 10,900**.

*Casualty 2 — jewelry stolen, not a disaster:* basis 3,000, insurance 4,500 → **line 4 = 1,500** (gain; lines 5–9
skipped); line 12 = 0.

*Casualty 3 — bicycle stolen, not a disaster:* basis 800, FMV before 800, after 0, no insurance → line 9 = 800,
line 12 = **700**.

Worksheet 1-1: line 1 = 700; line 2 = 10,900; line 3 = smaller of 700 and line 13 (1,500) = 700; line 4 = **11,600**.

1. Line 13 = 1,500; line 14 = 11,600.
2. Line 15: line 13 < line 14, no $500 losses → **0**.
3. Line 16 = 11,600 − (1,500 + 0) = **10,100**.
4. Line 17 = 10% × 80,000 = **8,000**.
