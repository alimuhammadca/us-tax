# Form 4972 — Tax on Lump-Sum Distributions (From Qualified Plans of Participants Born Before January 2, 1936)

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\forms\4972.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\forms\4972.md`. The lines it changes are documented in `C:\us-tax\new_lines\5a.md` and
`C:\us-tax\new_lines\5b.md` (the distribution leaves those lines) and line 16 (the tax is added there, box 2).

---

## 1. At a glance

A **qualified lump-sum distribution** is the payment, within one tax year, of a plan participant's entire balance from
all of an employer's qualified plans of one kind (pension, profit-sharing or stock bonus). When the participant was
born before January 2, 1936, Form 4972 lets the recipient tax it with two special formulas instead of adding it to
ordinary income:

- **Part II — 20% capital gain election:** the pre-1974 "capital gain" part (Form 1099-R box 3) is taxed at a flat 20%.
- **Part III — 10-year tax option:** the ordinary-income part is taxed as if received over 10 years, using a fixed
  1986 single-filer rate schedule printed in the instructions, after a "minimum distribution allowance".

The two can be used together or separately. Whatever part is taxed on Form 4972 is **not** reported on Form 1040
lines 5a / 5b, so it is not in total income, AGI or taxable income. The Form 4972 tax (line 30) is added to Form 1040
line 16 with **box 2** checked. One Form 4972 per participant; on a joint return each spouse with a distribution files
their own, and the two taxes are combined.

```text
Part II   line 7  = box 3 capital gain (line 6) × 20%
Part III  line 8  = box 2a − box 3 (if Part II used) or box 2a        (+ NUA if elected)
          line 10 = line 8 − death benefit exclusion (line 9)
          line 12 = line 10 + actuarial value of an annuity (line 11, box 8)
          line 16 = min(50% × line 12, $10,000) − 20% × max(0, line 12 − $20,000)     (minimum distribution allowance)
          line 19 = line 12 − line 16 − federal estate tax (line 18)
          line 25 = 10 × tax on (10% × line 19)                        (1986 schedule)
          line 28 = 10 × tax on (10% × [line 11 − line 16 × line 11 ÷ line 12])   (only if line 11 > 0)
          line 29 = line 25 − line 28
line 30   = line 7 + line 29   →  Form 1040 line 16 (check box 2)
```

Terms used below:

- **Participant** — the employee who earned the plan benefit. The recipient may be the participant, a beneficiary
  after the participant's death, or an alternate payee (a spouse or former spouse under a qualified domestic relations
  order, "QDRO").
- **Capital gain part** — the taxable amount attributable to participation before 1974 (Form 1099-R box 3).
- **NUA (net unrealized appreciation)** — the growth in value of employer securities while held in the plan (Form
  1099-R box 6); normally taxed only when the securities are sold.
- **Minimum distribution allowance (MDA)** — a reduction for small distributions (lines 13–16); zero once line 12
  reaches $70,000.
- **Multiple recipients** — a lump sum shared by several people (each Form 1099-R shows the recipient's share and
  the percentage in box 9a).

## 2. The IRS wording

**Purpose** (`f4972.pdf p. 2`): "Use Form 4972 to figure the tax on a qualified lump-sum distribution (defined below)
you received in 2025 using the 20% capital gain election, the 10-year tax option, or both. These are special formulas
used to figure a separate tax on the distribution that may result in a smaller tax than if you reported the taxable
amount of the distribution as ordinary income. You pay the tax only once, for the year you receive the distribution,
not over the next 10 years. The separate tax is added to the regular tax figured on your other income."

**Qualified lump-sum distribution** (`f4972.pdf p. 2`): "It is the distribution or payment in 1 tax year of a plan
participant's entire balance from all of an employer's qualified plans of one kind (for example, pension,
profit-sharing, or stock bonus plans) in which the participant had funds. The participant's entire balance doesn't
include deductible voluntary employee contributions or certain forfeited amounts. The participant must have been born
before January 2, 1936."

**Where to report** (`f4972.pdf p. 2`): "1. If you don't use Form 4972 … Report the entire amount from box 1 (Gross
distribution) of Form 1099-R on line 5a, and the taxable amount on line 5b. … 2. If you don't use Part III of Form
4972, but use Part II, report only the ordinary income portion of the distribution on Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR,
lines 5a and 5b … The ordinary income portion is the amount from box 2a of Form 1099-R, minus the amount from box 3 of
that form. 3. If you use Part III of Form 4972, don't include any part of the distribution on Form 1040, 1040-SR, or
1040-NR, lines 5a and 5b."

**How often** (`f4972.pdf p. 2`): "After 1986, you can use Form 4972 only once for each plan participant. If you
receive more than one lump-sum distribution for the same participant in 1 tax year, you must treat all those
distributions the same way. Combine them on a single Form 4972. … File a separate Form 4972 for each participant's
distribution."

**Joint return** (`f4972.pdf p. 3`): "If you and your spouse are filing a joint return and each has received a
lump-sum distribution, complete and file a separate Form 4972 for each spouse, combine the tax, and include the
combined tax in the total on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 16. Be sure to check box 2 on Form 1040 or 1040-SR, line 16."

**Form 1040 instructions** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 30`, lines 5a/5b): "If you or the plan participant was born before
January 2, 1936, you could pay less tax on the distribution. See Form 4972." Line 16 (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 34`):
include "Tax from Form 4972 (relating to lump-sum distributions). Check the appropriate box."

## 3. What goes on this form

- One qualified lump-sum distribution (or several for the **same** participant in the same year, combined) from a
  qualified pension, profit-sharing or stock bonus plan, when the Part I tests are met (`f4972.pdf p. 1–2`).
- Distributions received as a beneficiary of a participant born before January 2, 1936 (`f4972.pdf p. 2`).
- Distributions to an alternate payee under a QDRO when the participant was born before January 2, 1936
  (`f4972.pdf p. 2`).
- The current actuarial value of an annuity contract included in the lump sum (box 8) — used only to figure the tax
  on the ordinary income part (lines 11, 20–22, 26–28) (`f4972.pdf p. 2`).
- NUA (box 6) if the recipient elects to include it in taxable income (`f4972.pdf p. 3`).

## 6. How to fill it in, line by line

**Header.** Name and identifying number of the **recipient** of the distribution (`f4972.pdf p. 3`).

**Part I — Can you use Form 4972?** (`f4972.pdf p. 1`)

1. Line 1: "Was this a distribution of a plan participant's entire balance … from all of an employer's qualified plans
   of one kind …? If 'No,' don't use this form."
2. Line 2: "Did you roll over any part of the distribution? If 'Yes,' don't use this form."
3. Line 3: "Was this distribution paid to you as a beneficiary of a plan participant who was born before January 2,
   1936?"
4. Line 4: "Were you (a) a plan participant who received this distribution, (b) born before January 2, 1936, and (c) a
   participant in the plan for at least 5 years before the year of the distribution?" — "If you answered 'No' to both
   questions 3 and 4, don't use this form." Note line 4 is **all three conditions together**; the 5-year condition
   does not apply to a beneficiary (line 3), and a distribution "made because the participant died" is exempt from the
   first-5-years bar (`f4972.pdf p. 2`). A QDRO alternate payee qualifies when the participant was born before
   January 2, 1936 (`f4972.pdf p. 2`).
5. Line 5a: "Did you use Form 4972 after 1986 for a previous distribution from your own plan? If 'Yes,' don't use this
   form for a 2025 distribution from your own plan." Line 5b: the same question for a previous distribution received as
   a beneficiary of the same deceased participant. (An earlier pre-1987 election does not bar a post-1986 one if the
   participant was under 59½ at the time — `f4972.pdf p. 2`.)

**Part II — 20% capital gain election** (`f4972.pdf p. 1, p. 3–4`)

6. Line 6: Form 1099-R box 3. Leave blank if there is no capital gain or you do not elect Part II. With NUA elected
   into income, use the NUA Worksheet line G (box 3 + the capital gain share of box 6: box 6 × box 3 ÷ box 2a). With a
   death benefit exclusion, use the Death Benefit Worksheet line F (box 3 less the exclusion share allocated to capital
   gain). Reduce for the share of federal estate tax that applies to the capital gain.
7. Line 7: line 6 × 20%. If Part III is not used, line 7 is the Form 4972 tax; include it on Form 1040 line 16 and
   check box 2.

**Part III — 10-year tax option** (`f4972.pdf p. 1, p. 4`)

8. Line 8: "If you completed Part II, enter the amount from Form 1099-R, box 2a, minus box 3. If you didn't complete
   Part II, enter the amount from box 2a." If box 2a is blank, figure the taxable amount first (Pub. 575). With NUA
   elected: add box 6 (no Part II) or add NUA Worksheet line F, the ordinary part of NUA (with Part II); write "NUA"
   and the amount next to line 8. "Community property laws don't apply in figuring tax on the amount you report on
   line 8."
9. Line 9: death benefit exclusion — only if the participant died before August 21, 1996; "you may be able to exclude
   up to $5,000". With Part II, enter Death Benefit Worksheet line D minus line E.
10. Line 10: line 8 − line 9.
11. Line 11: current actuarial value of an annuity, Form 1099-R box 8 (else 0).
12. Line 12: line 10 + line 11. "If this amount is $70,000 or more, skip lines 13 through 16, enter this amount on
    line 17, and go to line 18."
13. Line 13: line 12 × 50%, not more than $10,000.
14. Line 14: line 12 − $20,000 (0 if line 12 is $20,000 or less).
15. Line 15: line 14 × 20%.
16. Line 16: minimum distribution allowance = line 13 − line 15.
17. Line 17: line 12 − line 16.
18. Line 18: federal estate tax attributable to the lump sum (beneficiaries only; from the estate's administrator).
19. Line 19: line 17 − line 18. "If line 11 is zero, skip lines 20 through 22 and go to line 23."
20. Line 20: line 11 ÷ line 12 as a decimal (at least three places).
21. Line 21: line 16 × line 20.
22. Line 22: line 11 − line 21.
23. Line 23: line 19 × 10%.
24. Line 24: tax on line 23 from the **Tax Rate Schedule** (§12 below).
25. Line 25: line 24 × 10.0. "If line 11 is zero, skip lines 26 through 28, enter this amount on line 29, and go to
    line 30."
26. Line 26: line 22 × 10%.
27. Line 27: tax on line 26 from the same schedule.
28. Line 28: line 27 × 10.0.
29. Line 29: line 25 − line 28. Multiple recipients: see §7.
30. Line 30: "Tax on lump-sum distribution. Add lines 7 and 29. Also, include this amount in the total on Form 1040 …
    line 16 (check box 2)".

**Rounding.** The form gives no special rounding rule; the general Form 1040 option to round to whole dollars on
forms and schedules applies (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 23`). Rounding line 24 before multiplying by 10 and keeping cents
until line 30 are both acceptable; they can differ by a few dollars.

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** a separate Form 4972 for each spouse who received a lump sum; combine the line 30 taxes on Form
  1040 line 16, box 2 (`f4972.pdf p. 3`). Each spouse's eligibility is tested separately (each is a different
  participant or beneficiary).
- **Separate returns:** each spouse reports only their own distribution and Form 4972. Community property laws do not
  apply to line 8 (`f4972.pdf p. 4`).

## 10. Where the amount goes next

| From Form 4972 | To | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Line 30 | **Form 1040 line 16**, box 2 checked | Added to the tax |
| Part III used | **Form 1040 lines 5a and 5b** | The whole distribution is **left out** (box 1 off 5a, box 2a off 5b) |
| Part II only | Lines 5a and 5b | Only the ordinary part (box 2a − box 3) is reported |
| Lines 5a / 5b reduced | Line 9, line 11 (AGI), line 15 and every AGI-based figure | The lump sum is never in AGI; AGI-based limits and phase-outs use the AGI without it |
| Line 30 (inside line 16) | Form 6251 line 10 | Subtracted when figuring regular tax for the AMT |
| Line 16 | Line 18 → credit limits (e.g. Schedule R Credit Limit Worksheet) | Credits can offset the Form 4972 tax |

## 11. Worked example

Edith Sample (000-00-0005), single, born March 3, 1934, retired in 2025 and received her entire balance from her
employer's pension plan: Form 1099-R box 1 $50,000, box 2a $50,000, box 2b "Total distribution" checked, box 3
$5,000, box 7 code 7A; nothing rolled over; she joined the plan in 1970 and never used Form 4972 before. She elects
both parts.

| Line | Figure | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Part I | 1 Yes, 2 No, 3 No, 4 Yes, 5a No, 5b No | eligible |
| 6 | box 3 | $5,000 |
| 7 | 20% × 5,000 | **$1,000** |
| 8 | 50,000 − 5,000 | $45,000 |
| 9, 10 | no death benefit | 0; $45,000 |
| 11, 12 | no annuity | 0; $45,000 |
| 13 | 50% × 45,000 = 22,500, max 10,000 | $10,000 |
| 14, 15 | 45,000 − 20,000 = 25,000; × 20% | $25,000; $5,000 |
| 16 | 10,000 − 5,000 | $5,000 |
| 17 | 45,000 − 5,000 | $40,000 |
| 18, 19 | no estate tax | 0; $40,000 |
| 23 | 10% × 40,000 | $4,000 |
| 24 | $260.50 + 14% × (4,000 − 2,270) = 260.50 + 242.20 | $502.70 |
| 25 = 29 | × 10 (line 11 is zero) | $5,027.00 |
| 30 | 1,000 + 5,027 | **$6,027** |

Form 1040: line 5a and 5b leave out the whole distribution (Part III used); line 16 = tax on her other taxable
income + $6,027, box 2 checked. (Rounding line 24 to $503 first gives line 25 $5,030 and line 30 $6,030 — both are
acceptable whole-dollar conventions.)
