Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\forms\6252.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\forms\6252.md`. Where line 26 / 37 lands next is in `C:\us-tax\new_lines\forms\schedule-d.md`

The 2025 form and its instructions are one document: `docs\books\f6252--2025.pdf` (pages 1–4; the instructions are

## 1. At a glance

An **installment sale** is "a disposition of property where at least one payment is received after the end of the
tax year in which the disposition occurs" (`f6252--2025.pdf p. 2`). Instead of reporting the whole gain in the year
of sale, the seller reports each year only the part of the gain contained in the principal received that year. The
part is fixed by the **gross profit percentage** — gross profit ÷ contract price — figured once, in the year of sale.
Depreciation recapture (ordinary income) is never deferred: it is all reported in the year of sale (line 12).

One Form 6252 per sale, filed every year from the year of sale until the year the last payment is received.

```text
Part I   line 7  = 5 − 6
         line 10 = 8 − 9                     (adjusted basis)
         line 13 = 10 + 11 + 12              (12 = Form 4797 Part III recapture, year of sale)
         line 14 = 5 − 13                    (zero or less → don't use Form 6252)
         line 16 = 14 − 15                   (gross profit; 15 = excluded main-home gain)
         line 17 = max(0, 6 − 13)            (debt assumed in excess of basis)
         line 18 = 7 + 17                    (contract price)
Part II  line 19 = 16 ÷ 18, 4+ decimals      (later years: the year-of-sale percentage)
         line 22 = 20 + 21                   (20 = line 17 in the year of sale, else 0)
         line 24 = max(0, 22 × 19)           (installment sale income)
         line 26 = 24 − 25                   → Schedule D (capital asset) or Form 4797 line 4 / 10 (business property)
Part III related party, second disposition:
         line 32 = min(30, 31);  34 = max(0, 32 − 33);  35 = 34 × 19 (year of first sale);  37 = 35 − 36
         → Schedule D or Form 4797, like line 26
```

Terms used below:

- **Selling price** — "the total of any money, the face amount of the installment obligation, and the FMV [fair
  market value] of other property or services that you received or will receive", including debt the buyer assumed,
  without any interest (`f6252--2025.pdf p. 3`, line 5).
- **Gross profit** — selling price less adjusted basis, selling expenses and year-of-sale recapture, less any excluded
  main-home gain (lines 14–16).
- **Contract price** — selling price less the seller's debt the buyer assumed, plus the part of that debt that exceeds
  the seller's basis (line 18).
- **Gross profit percentage** — gross profit ÷ contract price (line 19).
- **Related party** — "your spouse, child, grandchild, parent, or sibling; or a related corporation, S corporation,
  partnership, estate, or trust" (`f6252--2025.pdf p. 2`).
- **Second disposition** — the related buyer's later "sale, exchange, gift, or cancellation of installment note"
  before all payments on the first sale are made (`f6252--2025.pdf p. 2`).
- **Contingent payment sale** — a sale whose total selling price cannot be determined by the end of the year of sale
  (`f6252--2025.pdf p. 3`, line 4).

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 6252 (2025)** header (`f6252--2025.pdf p. 1`): "Installment Sale Income. Attach to your tax return. Use a
separate form for each sale or other disposition of property on the installment method."

**Purpose** (`f6252--2025.pdf p. 2`): "Use Form 6252 to report an installment sale under the installment method. File
Form 6252 for the year of the disposition and all subsequent years, regardless of whether a payment is received in
such year, until and including the year final payment is received or the obligation is disposed of."

**When not to file** (`f6252--2025.pdf p. 2`):

> "Don't file Form 6252 for sales that don't result in a gain, even if you will receive a payment in a tax year after
> the year of sale. Instead, report the entire sale on Form 4797 … Form 8949 … or the Schedule D for your tax return,
> whichever applies."
>
> "Don't file Form 6252 to report sales during the tax year of stock or securities traded on an established securities
> market. Instead, treat all payments as received during the year of sale."
>
> "Don't file Form 6252 if you elect not to report the sale on the installment method. To elect out, report the
> selling price on a timely filed return (including extensions) on Form 4797, Form 8949, or the Schedule D …"

**Which parts** (`f6252--2025.pdf p. 2`): "Complete lines 1 through 4, Part I, and Part II … for each year of the
installment agreement, including the year of final payment, even if a payment wasn't received during the year. Note:
If the property was sold to a related party, also complete Part III for the year of sale and 2 years after the year of
sale unless you received the final payment during the tax year."

**Interest** (`f6252--2025.pdf p. 2`): "Don't report interest received, carrying charges received, original issue
discount, or unstated interest on Form 6252."

Schedule D instructions, installment sales (`i1040sd.pdf p. 6`): "If you sold property (other than publicly traded
stocks or securities) at a gain and you will receive a payment in a tax year after the year of sale, you must
generally report the sale on the installment method unless you elect not to. Use Form 6252 … Also, use Form 6252 to
report any payment received in 2025 from a sale made in an earlier year that you reported on the installment method."

## 6. How to figure it, line by line

### Top of the form (`f6252--2025.pdf p. 1, 3`)

1. **Line 1** — "Description of property". Enter a code: "1. Sale property is timeshare or residential lot. 2. Sale by
   an individual of personal-use property … 3. Sale of any property used or produced in the trade or business of
   farming … 4. All other installment sales not listed." (`p. 3`).
2. **Line 2a / 2b** — date acquired and date sold (mm/dd/yyyy). The holding period (more than one year or not) decides
   where line 26 goes.
3. **Line 3** — "Was the property sold to a related party? … If 'Yes,' complete Part III for the year of sale and 2
   years after the year of the sale unless you received the final payment during the tax year."
4. **Line 4** — "Can the total selling price be determined by the close of the tax year in which such sale or other
   disposition occurs?" "No" makes it a contingent payment sale; "If you checked 'No,' the answer for all subsequent
   years must be 'No.'" (`p. 3`).

### Part I — Gross Profit and Contract Price ("Complete this part for all years of the installment agreement")

5. **Line 5** — "Selling price including mortgages and other debts. Don't include interest, whether stated or
   unstated." Contingent sale with a stated maximum: enter the maximum. "If there is no stated maximum selling price,
   attach a schedule showing the computation of gain. Enter the taxable part of the payment on line 24 and also on
   line 35 if Part III applies." (`p. 3`).
6. **Line 6** — "Mortgages, debts, and other liabilities the buyer assumed or took the property subject to." "Don't
   include new mortgages the buyer gets from a bank, the seller, or other sources." (`p. 3`).
7. **Line 7** — line 5 − line 6.
8. **Line 8** — "Cost or other basis of property sold": "the original cost and other expenses you incurred in buying
   the property. Add the cost of improvements, etc., and subtract any casualty losses and any of the following credits
   previously allowed" (energy, adoption, DC homebuyer, disabled access, plug-in vehicle and other listed credits)
   (`p. 3`).
9. **Line 9** — "Depreciation allowed or allowable": "all depreciation or amortization you deducted or were allowed to
   deduct from the date of purchase until the date of sale", plus §179 expense, commercial revitalization, clean-fuel,
   §190 / §193 and investment-credit basis reductions, minus §179 / §280F recapture and listed credit recaptures
   (`p. 3`).
10. **Line 10** — adjusted basis = line 8 − line 9.
11. **Line 11** — "Commissions and other expenses of sale": "sales commissions, advertising expenses, attorney and
    legal fees, and other selling expenses" (`p. 3`).
12. **Line 12** — "Income recapture from Form 4797, Part III": "Any ordinary income recapture under section 1245 or
    1250 (including sections 179 and 291) is fully taxable in the year of sale even if no payments were received. To
    figure the recapture amount, complete Form 4797, Part III. The ordinary income recapture is the amount on line 31
    of Form 4797. Enter it on line 12 of Form 6252 and also on line 13 of Form 4797. Don't enter any gain for this
    property on line 32 of Form 4797." (`p. 3`). Entered only in the year of sale; in later years line 13 carries the
    same total so the percentage does not change.
13. **Line 13** — lines 10 + 11 + 12.
14. **Line 14** — line 5 − line 13. "If zero or less, don't complete the rest of this form." "Don't file Form 6252 if
    line 14 is zero or less. Instead, report the entire sale on Form 4797, Form 8949, or the Schedule D" (`p. 1, 3`).
15. **Line 15** — "If the property described on line 1 above was your main home, enter the amount of your excluded
    gain … Otherwise, enter -0-" (Pub. 523) (`p. 1, 4`).
16. **Line 16** — gross profit = line 14 − line 15.
17. **Line 17** — line 6 − line 13; "If zero or less, enter -0-". This is debt assumed in excess of basis, treated as
    a payment in the year of sale.
18. **Line 18** — contract price = line 7 + line 17.

### Part II — Installment Sale Income ("Complete this part for all years of the installment agreement")

19. **Line 19** — "Gross profit percentage (expressed as a decimal amount). Divide line 16 by line 18. (For years
    after the year of sale, see instructions.)" Instructions: "Enter the gross profit percentage determined for the
    year of sale even if you didn't file Form 6252 for that year. Enter it as a decimal rounded to at least 4 digits,
    and include all zeros (for example, 25% = 0.2500, 35.25% = 0.3525, or 100% = 1.0000)." (`p. 4`).
20. **Line 20** — "If this is the year of sale, enter the amount from line 17. Otherwise, enter -0-."
21. **Line 21** — "Payments received during year … Don't include interest". Instructions (`p. 4`): "Enter all money and
    the FMV of any property or services you received during this tax year. Include as payments any amount withheld to
    pay off a mortgage or other debt or to pay broker and legal fees. Generally, don't include as a payment the buyer's
    note, a mortgage, or other debt assumed by the buyer. However, a note or other debt that is payable on demand or
    readily tradable on an established securities market is considered a payment. … If in prior years an amount was
    entered on the equivalent of line 34, don't include it on this line. Instead, enter it on line 23." Pledge-rule
    proceeds also count (§ 7 below).
22. **Line 22** — lines 20 + 21.
23. **Line 23** — "Payments received in prior years … Don't include interest". "Include allocable installment income
    and any other deemed payments from prior years", which include amounts deemed received because of "A second
    disposition by a related party, The pledge rule of section 453A(d), or Liabilities assumed by the buyer in excess
    of the seller's basis" (`p. 4`). Line 23 does not enter line 24; it feeds Part III line 33 and shows how much of
    the contract price is still owed.
24. **Line 24** — "Installment sale income. Multiply line 22 by line 19. This amount cannot be less than zero."
    "Under section 453 and Rev. Rul. 70-430, you cannot claim a loss under the installment method. Don't file Form 6252
    if the amount calculated for line 24 is less than zero." (`p. 1, 4`).
25. **Line 25** — "Enter the part of line 24 that is ordinary income under the recapture rules." Instructions (`p. 4`):
    "Enter here and on Form 4797, line 15, any ordinary income recapture on section 1252, 1254, or 1255 property for the
    year of sale or all remaining recapture from a prior year sale. Don't enter ordinary income from a section 179
    expense deduction. … Also report on this line any ordinary income recapture remaining from prior years on section
    1245 or 1250 property sold before June 7, 1984. Don't enter on line 25 more than the amount shown on line 24. Any
    excess must be reported in future years …"
26. **Line 26** — "Subtract line 25 from line 24. Enter here and on Schedule D or Form 4797." Instructions (`p. 4`):
    - "For trade or business property held more than 1 year, enter this amount on **Form 4797, line 4**."
    - "If the property was held 1 year or less or you have an ordinary gain from the sale of a noncapital asset (even if
      the holding period is more than 1 year), enter this amount on **Form 4797, line 10**, and enter 'From Form 6252.'"
    - "If the property was section 1250 property (generally, real property that you depreciated) held more than 1 year,
      figure the total amount of unrecaptured section 1250 gain included on line 26 using the Unrecaptured Section 1250
      Gain Worksheet in the Instructions for Schedule D."
    - "For capital assets, enter this amount on **Schedule D** as a short- or long-term gain on the lines identified as
      from Form 6252" — Schedule D **line 4** (short-term) or **line 11** (long-term).

### Part III — Related Party Installment Sale Income ("Don't complete if you received the final payment this tax year")

27. **Line 27** — name, address and taxpayer identifying number of the related party.
28. **Line 28** — "Did the related party resell or dispose of the property ('second disposition') during this tax
    year?" No → stop.
29. **Line 29** — if Yes, complete lines 30–37 "unless one of the following conditions is met":
    a. "The second disposition was more than 2 years after the first disposition (other than dispositions of marketable
       securities)" — enter its date;
    b. "The first disposition was a sale or exchange of stock to the issuing corporation";
    c. "The second disposition was an involuntary conversion and the threat of conversion occurred after the first
       disposition";
    d. "The second disposition occurred after the death of the original seller or buyer";
    e. "It can be established to the satisfaction of the IRS that tax avoidance wasn't a principal purpose for either of
       the dispositions. If this box is checked, attach an explanation."
    "If one of the conditions is met, check the appropriate box and skip lines 30 through 37." Box 29e generally applies
    to an involuntary second disposition (foreclosure, bankruptcy) or a resale on installment terms "substantially equal
    to or longer than those for the first sale" that do not speed up collection (`p. 4`).
30. **Line 30** — "Selling price of property sold by related party": "the amount realized from the part resold. If part
    was sold in an earlier year and part was sold this year, enter the cumulative amount realized" (money + FMV of
    property + liabilities assumed) (`p. 4`). For a non-sale disposition, the FMV (`p. 2`).
31. **Line 31** — "Enter contract price from line 18 for year of first sale."
32. **Line 32** — smaller of line 30 or line 31.
33. **Line 33** — "Total payments received by the end of this tax year": "the sum of lines 22 and 23 from Part II …
    Include allocable installment income and any other deemed payments from prior years. Don't include interest"
    (`p. 4`).
34. **Line 34** — line 32 − line 33; zero or less → 0.
35. **Line 35** — line 34 × the gross profit percentage on line 19 for the year of first sale.
36. **Line 36** — "the part of line 35 that is ordinary income under the recapture rules"; same rules as line 25, not
    more than line 35 (`p. 4`).
37. **Line 37** — line 35 − line 36. "Enter here and on Schedule D or Form 4797. See instructions" (same destinations as
    line 26). The amount on line 34 is a deemed payment: in later years it goes on line 23, not line 21 (`p. 4`).

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Election out | Report the full gain on a timely return (Form 4797 / 8949 / Schedule D); late election only on an amended return within 6 months of the due date, marked "Filed pursuant to section 301.9100-2" | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 2` |
| Contingent payment sale | Line 4 "No"; stated maximum on line 5; without a maximum, attach a gain computation and enter the taxable part on line 24 (and 35) | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 3` |
| Mortgage over basis | The excess (line 17) is a payment in the year of sale (line 20) and a deemed prior payment afterwards (line 23) | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 1, 4` |
| Depreciation recapture | All §1245 / §1250 (incl. §179, §291) recapture taxed in the year of sale via Form 4797 Part III → line 12 | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 3` |
| Related-party second disposition | Treat the related party's amount realized as received by you (lines 30–37), unless a line 29 exception applies; Part III for the year of sale and 2 years after | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 1, 2, 4` |
| Depreciable property to a related person (§453(g)) | Installment method not allowed unless no tax-avoidance purpose is shown; report on Form 4797 / 8949 / Schedule D with all payments treated as received in the year of sale; contingent payments at FMV | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 2` |
| Pledge rule (§453A(d)) | Net proceeds of a debt secured by the installment obligation are a payment (capped at contract price less earlier payments); applies to sales after 1988 over $150,000, except personal-use property of an individual, farm property, timeshares / residential lots | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 2` |
| Interest on deferred tax (§453A) | Due when the sales price is over $150,000 and the aggregate face of nondealer obligations arising in, and outstanding at the end of, the year is over $5 million; also in later years while those obligations are outstanding; not for farm property or personal-use property of an individual; figured outside Form 6252 → Schedule 2 line 15 | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 2–3`; `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 113` |
| Timeshares and residential lots (§453(l)) | Interest on tax due → Schedule 2 line 14 | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 3`; `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 113` |
| Disposition of the obligation (§453B) | Sale, gift, cancellation, repossession, distribution → gain or loss on Form 4797 / 8949 / Schedule D | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 3` |
| Main home | Excluded gain on line 15 (Pub. 523) | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 4` |
| QSB stock (section 1202) | An installment sale of QSB stock not traded on an established market → long-term gain from Form 6252 on Schedule D line 11; the exclusion is allowed in proportion to the eligible gain reported each year | `i1040sd.pdf p. 8` |
| QOF deferral | Eligible gain reported on Form 6252 may be invested in a QOF and deferred (Form 8949 / Form 8997) | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 3` |
| Business property held more than one year | Line 26 → Form 4797 line 4 (section 1231 netting and lookback) | `f6252--2025.pdf p. 4` |
| Unrecaptured §1250 gain on an installment sale | Schedule D §1250 worksheet line 4: Step 1 smaller of depreciation and total gain; Step 2 less §1250 ordinary recapture; Step 3 each year the smaller of line 26 / 37 and what remains — "the entire amount of gain … included in each installment payment is treated as unrecaptured section 1250 gain until the total … has been used in full" | `i1040sd.pdf p. 11–12` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

Form 6252 is per sale, not per person; the header carries the name(s) and identifying number shown on the return. On
a joint return, each spouse's installment sales are reported on the joint return (one Form 6252 per sale). On separate
returns, each spouse reports the sales of property they owned. The form instructions add no per-person limits; the
$150,000 and $5 million §453A tests and the related-party rules apply per filer (related party includes "your spouse",
`f6252--2025.pdf p. 2`).

## 11. Worked example

**Lee Sample** (000-00-0021), single, sells vacant land held as an investment since 2015 (a capital asset) on
03/01/2025 for $100,000: $20,000 down, the rest in yearly notes with interest. No debt assumed. Basis $40,000; broker
and legal fees $5,000.

| Line | Figure |
|---|---|
| 1 | code 4 — vacant land |
| 3 / 4 | No / Yes |
| 5 | 100,000 |
| 6 / 7 | 0 / 100,000 |
| 8 / 9 / 10 | 40,000 / 0 / 40,000 |
| 11 / 12 / 13 | 5,000 / 0 / 45,000 |
| 14 / 15 / 16 | 55,000 / 0 / 55,000 |
| 17 / 18 | 0 / 100,000 |
| 19 | 55,000 ÷ 100,000 = **0.5500** |
| 20 / 21 / 22 | 0 / 20,000 / 20,000 |
| 23 | 0 |
| 24 | 20,000 × 0.5500 = **11,000** |
| 25 / 26 | 0 / **11,000** → Schedule D line 11 (held more than 1 year) |

In 2026, if Lee receives $10,000 of principal: line 19 = 0.5500 (year-of-sale percentage), line 21 = 10,000, line 23 =
20,000, line 24 = 5,500.

**Business real property, mortgage over basis** — **Robin Sample** (000-00-0022) sells a rental building held since
2012 on 06/15/2025 for $200,000. The buyer assumes Robin's $90,000 mortgage. Cost $100,000, depreciation (straight-line)
$40,000, selling expenses $10,000. Robin receives $10,000 of principal in 2025.

1. Line 7 = 200,000 − 90,000 = 110,000. Line 10 = 60,000. Line 12 = 0 (straight-line real property: Form 4797 line 26a
   additional depreciation is zero, so no §1250 ordinary recapture). Line 13 = 70,000. Line 14 = 130,000 = line 16.
2. Line 17 = 90,000 − 70,000 = **20,000** (mortgage over basis). Line 18 = 110,000 + 20,000 = 130,000.
3. Line 19 = 130,000 ÷ 130,000 = **1.0000**. Line 20 = 20,000; line 21 = 10,000; line 22 = 30,000; line 24 = **30,000**
   = line 26.
4. Business property held more than one year → Form 4797 line 4 (section 1231).
5. Unrecaptured §1250 gain (Schedule D worksheet line 4): Step 1 smaller of depreciation 40,000 and gain 130,000 =
   40,000; Step 2 less §1250 ordinary recapture 0 = 40,000; Step 3 smaller of line 26 (30,000) and 40,000 = **30,000**
   this year; 10,000 remains for 2026.
