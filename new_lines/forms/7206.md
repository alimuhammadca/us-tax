## 1. At a glance

A self-employed person (or a partner with self-employment earnings, or a more-than-2% S-corporation shareholder) may
deduct, on **Schedule 1 line 17**, the health insurance premiums paid for themselves, their spouse, their dependents
and any child under 27 at the end of 2025 — medical, dental, vision, qualified long-term care (LTC, limited by age)
and voluntary Medicare premiums — when the plan is established under the business. The deduction is the smaller of
the premiums (lines 1–3) and the earned income from **that** business, reduced by the business's share of the
deductible part of self-employment tax and of the self-employed retirement deduction (lines 4–13). Months in which the
person could join an employer-subsidized plan do not count. Form 7206 is required instead of the short worksheet in the
Form 1040 instructions when there is more than one source of self-employment income, a Form 2555, or LTC premiums.

```text
line 3  = line 1 (health premiums, eligible months, no LTC) + line 2 (LTC premiums, each person ≤ age limit)
line 4  = net profit + other earned income of the business under which the plan is established
line 6  = line 4 ÷ line 5 (all net profits of profitable SE businesses)
line 7  = Schedule 1 line 15 × line 6
line 10 = line 4 − line 7 − line 9 (Schedule 1 line 16 of the same business)
   (S corporation: line 11 = W-2 box 5 Medicare wages from that corporation, used instead of line 10)
line 13 = line 10 (or 11) − line 12 (Form 2555 line 45 exclusion attributable)
line 14 = smaller of line 3 and line 13 → Schedule 1 line 17
```

Terms used below:

- **Earned income** — "net earnings and gains from the sale, transfer, or licensing of property you created. However,
  it doesn't include capital gain income" (`f7206.pdf p. 1` footnote **); for a more-than-2% shareholder, "your
  Medicare wages (box 5 of Form W-2) from that corporation" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 94`).
- **Established under your business** — a policy in the name of the business or of the individual (Schedule C / F);
  for a partner, premiums paid by the partnership as guaranteed payments or reimbursed and reported that way; for a
  more-than-2% shareholder, paid or reimbursed by the S corporation and reported as W-2 box 1 wages (`i7206.pdf p. 1`).
- **Qualified LTC insurance contract** — guaranteed renewable, covering only qualified LTC services, no cash surrender
  value, refunds only to reduce premiums or increase benefits (`i7206.pdf p. 2`).
- **Subsidized employer plan** — a plan maintained by your, your spouse's, your dependent's or your under-27 child's
  employer; a QSEHRA counts (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 94`).
- **Optional methods** — the Schedule SE farm and nonfarm optional methods of figuring net earnings.

## 2. The IRS wording

**Purpose** (`i7206.pdf p. 1`): "Use Form 7206 to determine any amount of the self-employed health insurance deduction
you may be able to report on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 17. You may be able to deduct the amount you paid for health
insurance, which includes medical, dental, and vision insurance and qualified long-term care insurance for yourself,
your spouse, and your dependents. The health insurance can cover your child who was under age 27 at the end of 2025,
even if the child wasn't your dependent."

**Who** (`i7206.pdf p. 1`): "One of the following statements must be true. • You were self-employed and had a net
profit for the year reported on Schedule C (Form 1040) or Schedule F (Form 1040). • You were a partner with net
earnings from self-employment for the year reported on Schedule K-1 (Form 1065), box 14, code A. • You used one of the
optional methods to figure your net earnings from self-employment on Schedule SE (Form 1040). • You received wages in
2025 from an S corporation in which you were a more-than-2% shareholder."

**When Form 7206 is required** (`i7206.pdf p. 1`; same list `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 95`): "Generally, you can use the
worksheet in the Form 1040 instructions to figure your deduction. However, if any of the following apply, you must use
Form 7206. • You had more than one source of income subject to self-employment tax. • You file Form 2555. • You are
using amounts paid for qualified long-term care insurance to figure the deduction."

**Marketplace** (`i7206.pdf p. 2`): "See Pub. 974 if the insurance plan was considered to be established under your
business and was obtained through the Marketplace, and advance payments of the premium tax credit were made or you are
claiming the premium tax credit." Form 8962 instructions: "You cannot deduct the portion of your health insurance
premium on your tax return that is paid for by the PTC or APTC" (`i8962_2025.pdf p. 2`).

**More than one plan and business** (`i7206.pdf p. 2`): "If you have more than one health plan during the year and each
plan is established under a different business, you must use a separate Form 7206 to figure each plan's net earnings
limit. … For a plan that provides long-term care insurance, the total of the amounts entered for each person on line 2
of all Form(s) 7206 can't be more than the appropriate limit shown on line 2 for that person."

**Other coverage** (`i7206.pdf p. 2`): "You can't take the deduction for any month you were eligible to participate in
any employer (including your spouse's) subsidized health plan at any time during that month, even if you didn't
actually participate. … These rules are applied separately to plans that provide long-term care insurance and plans
that don't provide long-term care insurance. However, any medical insurance payments not deductible on Schedule 1
(Form 1040), line 17, can be included as medical expenses on Schedule A (Form 1040) if you itemize deductions."

**Effects** (`i7206.pdf p. 2`): "Don't include the amount on line 14 when figuring any medical expense deduction on
Schedule A." "You can't subtract the self-employed health insurance deduction when figuring net earnings for your
self-employment tax from the business under which the insurance plan is established."

**Form footnote** (`f7206.pdf p. 1`): "* If you used either optional method to figure your net earnings from
self-employment from any business, don't enter your net profit from the business. Instead, enter the amount
attributable to that business from Schedule SE (Form 1040), Part I, line 4b." Schedule SE: "The optional methods of
computing net earnings from self-employment may be used to figure your self-employed health insurance deduction"
(`i1040sse.pdf p. 5`).

## 3. What goes on this form

- Premiums paid in 2025 for 2025 coverage under a plan established under the business, for you, your spouse, your
  dependents and your under-27 children: medical, dental, vision (line 1); qualified LTC (line 2); Medicare premiums
  you voluntarily pay for insurance in your name similar to qualifying private insurance (line 1) (`i7206.pdf p. 1`).
- The earned income of that business (line 4) and of all profitable SE businesses (line 5), the deductible half of
  SE tax (line 7 input), the SE retirement deduction of that business (line 9), S-corporation Medicare wages (line 11),
  the Form 2555 exclusion attributable (line 12).

## 6. How to figure it, step by step (every line of the 2025 form)

**Before you begin.** Use the Form 1040 worksheet (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 94`: line 1 premiums; line 2 net profit and
other earned income of the business "minus any deductions on Schedule 1, lines 15 and 16"; line 3 the smaller) unless
one of the three Form 7206 cases applies. Use a separate Form 7206 for each business under which a plan is established.

1. **Line 1** — total paid in 2025 for 2025 health coverage established under the business (or the S corporation) for
   you, your spouse, your dependents (and under-27 children), **excluding** months eligible for a subsidized employer
   plan, up to $3,000 paid from tax-free retired-public-safety-officer distributions, and LTC premiums (those go on
   line 2) (`f7206.pdf p. 1`).
2. **Line 2** — for each person covered by qualified LTC insurance, the smaller of (a) premiums paid for that person
   and (b) the age limit for that person's age at the end of 2025: $480 (40 or younger), $900 (41–50), $1,800
   (51–60), $4,810 (61–70), $6,020 (71 or older); excluding months eligible for an employer-subsidized LTC plan; then
   total the persons (`f7206.pdf p. 1`; `i7206.pdf p. 2`).
3. **Line 3** = line 1 + line 2.
4. **Line 4** — net profit and other earned income from the business under which the plan is established; no CRP
   payments exempt from SE tax; Schedule SE line 4b amount instead when an optional method was used; for an S
   corporation skip to line 11 (`f7206.pdf p. 1`).
5. **Line 5** — total of all net profits from Schedule C line 31, Schedule F line 34 and K-1 (1065) box 14 code A plus
   other income of the profitable businesses; **no net losses** (`f7206.pdf p. 1`).
6. **Line 6** = line 4 ÷ line 5.
7. **Line 7** = Schedule 1 line 15 × line 6 — the business's share of the deductible SE tax.
8. **Line 8** = line 4 − line 7.
9. **Line 9** — Schedule 1 line 16 attributable to the same business.
10. **Line 10** = line 8 − line 9.
11. **Line 11** — S corporation: Medicare wages (W-2 box 5) from the corporation under which the plan is established.
12. **Line 12** — Form 2555 line 45 exclusion attributable to the line 4 or line 11 income.
13. **Line 13** = line 10 (or line 11) − line 12.
14. **Line 14** = smaller of line 3 and line 13 → Schedule 1 line 17; not included in any Schedule A medical expense
    (`f7206.pdf p. 1`).

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- The limit is per business, so each self-employed spouse figures the deduction on their own Form 7206 against the
  earned income of the business under which their plan is established; premiums may cover the other spouse
  (`i7206.pdf p. 1`).
- An employer plan of **either** spouse that the self-employed person could join stops the deduction for those months
  (`i7206.pdf p. 2`).
- Married filing separately: the premium tax credit is generally not allowed, so the Pub. 974 interaction does not
  arise (Form 8962 instructions; detail in Pub. 974).

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Line 14** → **Schedule 1 line 17** → Schedule 1 line 26 → Form 1040 line 10 → line 11 (AGI).
- Schedule A medical expenses are reduced by line 14 ("Reduce the insurance premiums by any self-employed health
  insurance deduction you claimed on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), line 17", `i1040sca_2025.pdf p. 1`); premiums not
  deducted on line 17 may be included there (`i7206.pdf p. 2`).
- Through AGI: the premium tax credit (household income), every AGI-based limit.
- Not into Schedule SE (`i7206.pdf p. 2`).

## 11. Worked example

Dana Sample (000-00-0001), age 45, single, runs two sole proprietorships in 2025: a design studio (Schedule C net
profit $40,000) under which a health plan and an LTC policy are established, and a craft shop (Schedule C net profit
$10,000). Dana is never eligible for an employer plan. Schedule 1 line 15 (half of SE tax on $50,000) = $3,532;
Schedule 1 line 16 for the studio = $0. Premiums: medical $9,000; LTC $1,200.

1. Two SE sources and LTC → Form 7206 required.
2. Line 1 = **$9,000**.
3. Line 2: smaller of $1,200 and the age 41–50 limit $900 = **$900**.
4. Line 3 = **$9,900**.
5. Line 4 = **$40,000** (studio). Line 5 = 40,000 + 10,000 = **$50,000**.
6. Line 6 = 40,000 ÷ 50,000 = **0.80**.
7. Line 7 = 3,532 × 0.80 = **$2,826**.
8. Line 8 = 40,000 − 2,826 = **$37,174**. Line 9 = 0; line 10 = **$37,174**.
9. Line 12 = 0 (no Form 2555); line 13 = **$37,174**.
10. Line 14 = smaller of 9,900 and 37,174 = **$9,900** → Schedule 1 line 17. The $300 of LTC premium above the age limit
    is not deductible anywhere.
