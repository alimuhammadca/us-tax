# Form 8606 — Nondeductible IRAs

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\forms\8606.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\forms\8606.md`. The Form 1040 lines it feeds are documented in `C:\us-tax\new_lines\4a.md`,
`4b.md` and `4c.md`; this file covers the form itself — basis tracking from year to year, the line 6–15 pro-rata
computation, Part II conversions, Part III Roth distributions, one form per person, and when the form must be filed
even though it changes no tax. Form 5329 (the additional taxes) gets its own file.

---

## 1. At a glance

Form 8606 keeps the running record of a person's **basis** in traditional IRAs (money already taxed) and in Roth IRAs,
and uses it to split this year's IRA money into a taxable and a nontaxable part. It has three parts:

- **Part I (lines 1–15c)** — nondeductible contributions to traditional IRAs and distributions from them. This year's
  nondeductible contributions are added to the basis carried from last year's form; if money left the traditional
  IRAs (a distribution or a Roth conversion), the basis is spread **pro rata** over everything in or out of the
  traditional IRAs this year, and what is left of the basis (line 14) is carried to next year.
- **Part II (lines 16–18)** — 2025 conversions from traditional IRAs to Roth IRAs: the amount converted less the
  basis that went with it is taxable.
- **Part III (lines 19–25c)** — nonqualified distributions from Roth IRAs: they come out of regular contributions
  first, then conversions and rollovers, then earnings; only the part past both kinds of basis is taxable.

```text
Part I   line 3   = line 1 (2025 nondeductible contributions) + line 2 (basis from last year's line 14)
         no 2025 distribution or conversion  →  line 14 = line 3; stop
         line 5   = line 3 − line 4 (contributions made Jan 1 – Apr 15, 2026 for 2025)
         line 9   = line 6 (Dec 31 value + outstanding rollovers) + line 7 (distributions) + line 8 (conversions)
         line 10  = line 5 ÷ line 9, at most 1.000 (rounded to at least 3 places)
         line 11  = line 8 × line 10        (basis in the conversion)
         line 12  = line 7 × line 10        (basis in the other distributions)
         line 13  = line 11 + line 12
         line 14  = line 3 − line 13        →  basis carried to the next Form 8606 (line 2)
         line 15a = line 7 − line 12;  line 15b = qualified-disaster share;  line 15c = 15a − 15b (− certain repayments)
Part II  line 16 = line 8;  line 17 = line 11;  line 18 = line 16 − line 17
Part III line 21 = line 19 (nonqualified Roth distributions) − line 20 (first-home, ≤ $10,000 lifetime)
         line 23 = line 21 − line 22 (Roth contribution basis)          (≥ 0; > 0 → Form 5329 may apply)
         line 25a = line 23 − line 24 (conversion / rollover basis)   (≥ 0)
         line 25c = line 25a − line 25b (qualified-disaster share) (− certain repayments)

Form 1040 line 4b includes line 15c + line 18 + line 25c (when more than zero).
```

Terms used below:

- **Traditional IRA** — any IRA that is not a Roth IRA, including traditional SEP and SIMPLE IRAs (`i8606.pdf p. 1`,
  `p. 3`).
- **Basis (traditional)** — "the total of all your nondeductible contributions and your nontaxable amounts included in
  rollovers made to these IRAs minus the total of all your nontaxable distributions" (`i8606.pdf p. 3`).
- **Nondeductible contribution** — a traditional IRA contribution not deducted on Schedule 1 line 20 (because of the
  income limits or by choice). Employer SEP / SIMPLE contributions are never nondeductible contributions
  (`i8606.pdf p. 1–2`).
- **Pro-rata rule** — with basis, every non-excluded traditional IRA distribution and conversion is partly basis and
  partly taxable in the ratio of basis to (year-end value + distributions + conversions) — lines 6–13. All of a
  person's traditional IRAs are treated as one.
- **Conversion** — moving traditional IRA money to a Roth IRA; taxable except for the basis in it.
- **Qualified Roth distribution** — after the 5-year period and on or after age 59½, after death, for disability or for
  a first home (`i8606.pdf p. 3`); not taxable and not reported in Part III.
- **Roth basis** — regular Roth contributions (line 22) and conversions / rollovers from plans (line 24) not yet
  distributed.
- **"Certain 2025 retirement plan distribution repayments treated as rollovers"** — repayments of 2025 qualified
  disaster, birth or adoption, emergency personal expense, domestic abuse and terminal illness distributions
  (`i8606.pdf p. 5`).

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 8606 (2025)** (`C:\us-tax\docs\IRS-Forms\f8606.pdf p. 1`): "Name. If married, file a separate form for each
spouse required to file 2025 Form 8606." Address block: "Fill in Your Address Only if You Are Filing This Form by
Itself and Not With Your Tax Return." Part I lead-in: "Complete this part only if one or more of the following apply.
• You made nondeductible contributions to a traditional IRA for 2025. … • You took distributions from a traditional IRA
in 2025 and you made nondeductible contributions to a traditional IRA in 2025 or an earlier year. … • You converted
part, but not all, of your traditional IRAs to Roth IRAs in 2025 and you made nondeductible contributions to a
traditional IRA in 2025 or an earlier year." After line 3: "In 2025, did you take a distribution from a traditional
IRA, or make a Roth IRA conversion? No → Enter the amount from line 3 on line 14. Do not complete the rest of Part I.
Yes → Go to line 4." Line 10: "Divide line 5 by line 9. Enter the result as a decimal rounded to at least 3 places. If
the result is 1.000 or more, enter '1.000'." Line 14: "Subtract line 13 from line 3. This is your total basis in
traditional IRAs for 2025 and earlier years."

**Purpose** (`i8606.pdf p. 2`): "Use Form 8606 to report: • Nondeductible contributions you made to traditional IRAs;
• Distributions from traditional IRAs, if you have a basis in these IRAs; • Conversions from traditional IRAs to Roth
IRAs; and • Distributions from Roth IRAs."

**No basis, no form** (`i8606.pdf p. 2`): "If you received distributions from a traditional IRA in 2025 and you have
never made nondeductible contributions (including nontaxable amounts you rolled over from a qualified retirement plan)
to these IRAs, don't report the distributions on 2025 Form 8606."

**Who must file** (`i8606.pdf p. 2`): "File 2025 Form 8606 if any of the following apply. • You made nondeductible
contributions to a traditional IRA for 2025. … • You received distributions from a traditional IRA in 2025 and your
basis in these IRAs is more than zero. … • You or your spouse transferred all or part of their traditional IRA in 2025
to the other spouse under a divorce or separation agreement where the transfer resulted in a change in the basis of the
IRA of either spouse. • You converted an amount from a traditional IRA to a Roth IRA in 2025. • You received
distributions from a Roth IRA in 2025 (other than a rollover, recharacterization, or return of certain contributions …).
• You received a distribution from an inherited traditional IRA that has a basis, or you received a distribution from an
inherited Roth IRA that wasn't a qualified distribution. You may need to file more than one Form 8606." And: "You don't
have to file Form 8606 solely to report regular contributions to Roth IRAs."

**Filing alone** (`i8606.pdf p. 2`): "If you aren't required to file an income tax return but are required to file
2025 Form 8606, sign 2025 Form 8606 and send it to the IRS at the same time and place you would otherwise file." Pub.
590-A: "You must file Form 8606 to report nondeductible contributions even if you don't have to file a tax return for
the year" (`p590a.pdf p. 16`).

**Penalties** (`i8606.pdf p. 8`): "If you are required to file Form 8606 to report a nondeductible contribution to a
traditional IRA for 2025 but don't do so, you must pay a $50 penalty, unless you can show reasonable cause." "If you
overstate your nondeductible contributions, you must pay a $100 penalty." Pub. 590-A adds the consequence of not
reporting: "all of the contributions to your traditional IRA will be treated like deductible contributions when
withdrawn" (`p590a.pdf p. 17`).

**One form per person and per decedent** (`i8606.pdf p. 8`): "If you file a joint return, enter only the name and SSN of
the spouse whose information is being reported on Form 8606. … If both you and your spouse are required to file 2025
Form 8606, file a separate 2025 Form 8606 for each of you. If you are required to file 2025 Form 8606 for IRAs inherited
from more than one decedent, file a separate 2025 Form 8606 for the IRA from each decedent." Pub. 590-B: an inherited
IRA's basis "remains with the IRA. Unless you are the decedent's spouse and choose to treat the IRA as your own, you
can't combine this basis with any basis you have in your own traditional IRA(s)" (`p590b.pdf p. 6`).

**Records** (`i8606.pdf p. 8`): keep "Forms 8606 and any supporting statements, attachments, and worksheets for all
applicable years", Forms 5498 showing contributions and year-end values, and Forms 1099-R "until all distributions are
made".

## 3. What goes on this form

- The person's 2025 nondeductible traditional IRA contributions, including those made January 1 – April 15, 2026 for
  2025, and repayments of qualified reservist distributions (line 1; the 2026 part also on line 4).
- The basis carried from the last Form 8606 (line 2), adjusted for returned excess contributions, divorce transfers and
  unreported after-tax rollovers from plans.
- The December 31, 2025 value of all the person's traditional, SEP and SIMPLE IRAs plus outstanding rollovers (line 6).
- 2025 traditional IRA distributions other than the excluded kinds (line 7) and net Roth conversions (line 8 / 16).
- 2025 nonqualified Roth IRA distributions (line 19), first-home expenses (line 20) and the two Roth basis figures
  (lines 22 and 24).
- The share of lines 15a and 25a that comes from qualified disaster distributions on Form 8915-F (lines 15b, 25b).

## 6. How to fill it in, line by line

**Header.** Name and SSN of the one person whose IRAs are reported — on a joint return, only that spouse
(`i8606.pdf p. 8`). Address only when the form is filed by itself (`f8606.pdf p. 1`).

**Part I — when to complete it.** Only if (a) nondeductible contributions were made for 2025, (b) there was a 2025
traditional IRA distribution and basis from 2025 or earlier, or (c) part (not all) of the traditional IRAs was
converted and there is basis (`f8606.pdf p. 1`). "Distributions" here leave out rollovers, QCDs, the HSA funding
distribution, conversions, recharacterizations and returns of certain contributions, but include the 2025 retirement
plan distributions whose repayments are treated as rollovers.

1. **Line 1 — 2025 nondeductible contributions.** "If you used the IRA Deduction Worksheet in the Form 1040
   instructions …, subtract line 12 of the worksheet (or the amount you chose to deduct on Schedule 1 (Form 1040),
   line 20, if less) from the smaller of line 10 or line 11 of the worksheet. … You can't deduct the amount included on
   line 1." Include repayments of a qualified reservist distribution; leave out contributions returned with their
   earnings (`i8606.pdf p. 9`). A person may designate even a deductible contribution as nondeductible
   (`p590a.pdf p. 16`). The IRA Deduction Worksheet itself says the rest of the contribution may be "treat[ed] … as a
   nondeductible contribution (see Form 8606)" and "If the total of your IRA deduction on line 20 plus any
   nondeductible contributions to your traditional IRAs shown on Form 8606 is less than your total traditional IRA
   contributions for 2025, see Pub. 590-A for special rules" (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 95, 98`).
2. **Line 2 — total basis.** "Generally, if this is the first year you are required to file Form 8606, enter -0-.
   Otherwise, use the Total Basis Chart" (`i8606.pdf p. 9`): last form for 2024 → its line 14 increased by line 6 of the
   Line 15c Worksheet completed that year, if any; 2001–2023 → line 14; 1993–2000 → line 12; 1989–1992 → line 14;
   1988 → lines 7 + 16; 1987 → lines 4 + 13. Adjust for returned excess contributions (`p. 7–8`), divorce transfers
   (`p. 10`) and nontaxable plan amounts rolled into a traditional IRA not yet reported (`p. 9`).
3. **Line 3** = line 1 + line 2. **Decision:** no 2025 traditional distribution and no conversion → enter line 3 on
   line 14 and stop Part I (`f8606.pdf p. 1`).
4. **Line 4** — the part of line 1 contributed January 1 – April 15, 2026. You may choose which 2025-year contributions
   are treated as the nondeductible ones (`i8606.pdf p. 9`); the line 4 amounts "aren't included in figuring the
   nontaxable part of any distributions you received in 2025".
5. **Line 5** = line 3 − line 4.
6. **Line 6** — "the total value of all your traditional IRAs as of December 31, 2025, plus any outstanding rollovers",
   taking into account recharacterizations made after December 31 (`i8606.pdf p. 9`). An outstanding rollover is
   "generally the amount of any distribution received in 2025 after November 1, 2025, that was rolled over in 2026, but
   within the 60-day rollover period"; not one to a qualified plan (`p. 10`). Include Part IV Form 8915-F home-purchase
   distributions repaid in time; **reduce** by 2025 repayments of 2025 distributions treated as rollovers (not below 0);
   do not reduce for repayments of earlier-year distributions (`p. 10`). In the year of the owner's death the value is
   figured at the date of death (`p590b.pdf p. 16`).
7. **Line 7** — 2025 traditional IRA distributions, leaving out: conversions, recharacterizations, rollovers to another
   traditional IRA (even an outstanding one) or to a plan, the HSA funding distribution, returned contributions,
   QCDs, returned excess contributions of earlier years, repaid Part IV Form 8915-F distributions and divorce transfers
   — but including all 2025 distributions whose repayments are treated as rollovers, "even if they were later repaid"
   (`i8606.pdf p. 10`). **Special computation:** "If you received a distribution in 2025 from a traditional IRA and you
   also made contributions for 2025 to a traditional IRA that may not be fully deductible because of the income
   limits, you must make a special computation before completing the rest of this form" — Pub. 590-B Worksheet 1-1
   (`i8606.pdf p. 10`; `p590b.pdf p. 16–17`; see section 7).
8. **Line 8** — "If, in 2025, you converted any amounts from traditional IRAs to a Roth IRA, enter on line 8 the net
   amount you converted" (`i8606.pdf p. 10`); also on line 16.
9. **Line 9** = lines 6 + 7 + 8.
10. **Line 10** = line 5 ÷ line 9, "rounded to at least 3 places", 1.000 at most.
11. **Line 11** = line 8 × line 10 — "the nontaxable portion of the amount you converted"; also on line 17.
12. **Line 12** = line 7 × line 10 — the nontaxable part of the distributions not converted.
13. **Line 13** = lines 11 + 12.
14. **Line 14** = line 3 − line 13 — "your total basis in traditional IRAs for 2025 and earlier years". This is next
    year's line 2. If a 2025 Line 15c Worksheet has an amount on its line 6, that amount is added to line 14 "in the
    next year after 2025 you report traditional IRA distributions on Part I" (`i8606.pdf p. 10`, `p. 11–12`).
15. **Line 15a** = line 7 − line 12.
16. **Line 15b** — the part of line 15a from qualified disaster distributions: 0 if none; all of line 15a if all are;
    otherwise line 15a × (qualified disaster distributions ÷ line 7). Also entered on Form 8915-F line 18
    (`i8606.pdf p. 10–11`; Example 1: $30,000 × $22,000 / $33,000 = $20,000).
17. **Line 15c** = line 15a − line 15b, reduced by certain 2025 repayments treated as rollovers (other than Form 8915-F
    ones) through the **2025 Line 15c Worksheet** when a 2025 birth/adoption, emergency, domestic-abuse or
    terminal-illness distribution was repaid within 3 years (`i8606.pdf p. 11–12`). If more than zero, it is included on
    Form 1040 line 4b. Under age 59½ it may carry the 10% (25% for a SIMPLE IRA in its first 2 years) additional tax —
    Schedule 2 line 8 / Form 5329 (`p. 11`).

**Part II — 2025 conversions** ("Complete this part if you converted part or all of your traditional IRAs to a Roth
IRA in 2025", `i8606.pdf p. 13`).

18. **Line 16** — line 8 if Part I was completed; otherwise the net amount converted.
19. **Line 17** — line 11 if Part I was completed; otherwise "the amount from line 2 (or the amount you would have
    entered on line 2 if you had completed that line) plus any contributions included on line 1 that you made before
    the conversion" (`i8606.pdf p. 13`).
20. **Line 18** = line 16 − line 17, the taxable conversion. "If your entry on line 18 is zero or less, don't include
    the result on … line 4b. Include the full amount of the distribution on … line 4a" (`i8606.pdf p. 13`). Conversions
    cannot be recharacterized (since 2018, `p. 6`). Line 18 is subtracted from AGI for the Roth contribution MAGI
    (`p. 4`).

**Part III — Roth distributions** ("Complete this part only if you took a distribution from a Roth IRA in 2025",
`f8606.pdf p. 2`).

21. **Line 19** — total nonqualified Roth distributions, including first-time-homebuyer distributions and 2025
    distributions whose repayments are treated as rollovers. Leave out: rollovers (including outstanding ones),
    recharacterizations, returned contributions, distributions on or after 59½ or on death / disability **if** a
    contribution (or conversion or plan rollover) was made for any year 1998–2020, the HSA funding distribution, QCDs,
    repaid Part IV Form 8915-F distributions and divorce transfers. "If, after considering the items above, you don't
    have an amount to enter on line 19, don't complete Part III; your Roth IRA distribution(s) isn't taxable"
    (`i8606.pdf p. 13–14`).
22. **Line 20** — first-home expenses when a contribution was made for any year 1998–2020; at most "$10,000 reduced by
    the total of all your prior qualified first-time homebuyer distributions" (`i8606.pdf p. 14`).
23. **Line 21** = line 19 − line 20 (0 if negative).
24. **Line 22** — basis in regular Roth contributions. With no earlier Roth distribution: all regular Roth
    contributions 1998–2025 (not rollovers between Roth IRAs or returned contributions), adjusted for
    recharacterizations. With an earlier distribution: the **Basis in Regular Roth IRA Contributions Worksheet**
    (last year's line 22 less last year's line 19, plus later contributions) (`i8606.pdf p. 14, 18`). Increase for
    designated-Roth-account rollovers treated as investment in the contract, military gratuity / SGLI rollovers and a
    prior Line 25c Worksheet line 5; adjust for divorce transfers. "If line 21 is zero, stop here."
25. **Line 23** = line 21 − line 22 (0 if negative; then skip lines 24 and 25). An amount here may be subject to the
    additional tax — Form 5329 lines 1–4 (`i8606.pdf p. 14`).
26. **Line 24** — basis in conversions and plan-to-Roth rollovers: 0 if never converted or rolled over; with no earlier
    Roth distribution beyond contribution basis, the total of all conversions (line 16 of 2001–2025 Forms 8606, line 14c
    of 1998–2000) plus plan-to-Roth rollovers reported on Form 1040 (not designated Roth rollovers); otherwise the line
    24 chart (`i8606.pdf p. 14, 19–20`).
27. **Line 25a** = line 23 − line 24 (0 if negative; then skip 25b, 25c).
28. **Line 25b** — qualified-disaster share of line 25a: line 25a × (qualified disaster distributions ÷ line 21), also
    on Form 8915-F line 19 (`i8606.pdf p. 14–15`).
29. **Line 25c** = line 25a − line 25b, reduced through the **2025 Line 25c Worksheet** for repaid 2025 birth/adoption,
    emergency, domestic-abuse or terminal-illness distributions; worksheet line 5 (excess repayment) is added to the
    next year's line 22 (`i8606.pdf p. 15–17`). If more than zero, it is included on Form 1040 line 4b; it goes on Form
    5329 line 1 (`i5329.pdf p. 3`).

**Roth ordering behind Part III** (`p590b.pdf p. 35`): "Order the distributions as follows. 1. Regular contributions.
2. Conversion and rollover contributions, on a first-in, first-out basis … a. Taxable portion … first. b. Nontaxable
portion. 3. Earnings on contributions." All Roth distributions of the year are added together; contributions for the
year include those made by the due date (`p590b.pdf p. 36`).

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- **Joint return:** a separate Form 8606 for each spouse required to file one; each shows only that spouse's name and
  SSN (`i8606.pdf p. 8`). Basis, the year-end value, distributions and the line 10 ratio are each spouse's own — IRAs
  are never joint. Line 4b on Form 1040 is the sum of both spouses' line 15c + 18 + 25c.
- **Separate returns:** each spouse files their own Form 8606 with their own return. Married filing separately and
  living together limits the traditional IRA deduction (phase-out $0–$10,000) and Roth contributions ($10,000 MAGI
  limit), which tends to make contributions nondeductible (line 1) (`i8606.pdf p. 1`).
- **Spousal IRA:** the contribution limit, not the basis, is shared (`i8606.pdf p. 5`).

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Line 15c + line 18 + line 25c → Form 1040 line 4b** (each when more than zero); the full distributions are on line
  4a (`f8606.pdf p. 2`; `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 27`).
- **Line 14 → next Form 8606 line 2** (Total Basis Chart, `i8606.pdf p. 9`). Line 22 / 24 and line 16 → next year's
  Roth basis figures (`p. 18–20`).
- **Line 15c, line 23 / 25c → Form 5329** Part I (additional 10% / 25% tax → Schedule 2 line 8). Line 25c and line 20
  go on Form 5329 line 1, line 20 with exception 09 on line 2 (`i5329.pdf p. 3`). **Line 19 → Form 5329 line 20**
  ("Generally, enter the amount from Form 8606, line 19, plus any qualified distributions", `i5329.pdf p. 7`).

## 11. Worked example

**Part I and Part II.** Pat Example (000-00-0081), single, age 62. Pat's 2024 Form 8606 line 14 shows $6,000 of basis.
In May 2025 Pat contributed $7,000 to a traditional IRA; Pat's income is too high to deduct any of it. In 2025 Pat
withdrew $10,000 in cash (Form 1099-R code 7, box 1 $10,000) and converted $33,000 to a Roth IRA (Form 1099-R code 2,
box 1 $33,000). On December 31, 2025, Pat's traditional IRAs were worth $87,000; no outstanding rollovers.

| 3 | $7,000 + $6,000 | $13,000 |

| 4 | Contributions made in 2026 for 2025 | $0 |
| 5 | $13,000 − $0 | $13,000 |
| 6 | December 31 value | $87,000 |
| 7 | Cash distribution (box 1 $43,000 − $33,000 converted) | $10,000 |
| 8 | Net amount converted | $33,000 |
| 9 | $87,000 + $10,000 + $33,000 | $130,000 |
| 10 | $13,000 ÷ $130,000 | 0.100 |
| 11 | $33,000 × 0.100 | $3,300 |
| 12 | $10,000 × 0.100 | $1,000 |
| 13 | $3,300 + $1,000 | $4,300 |
| 14 | $13,000 − $4,300 — basis carried to Pat's 2026 Form 8606 line 2 | **$8,700** |
| 15a | $10,000 − $1,000 | $9,000 |
| 15b | No disaster distribution | $0 |

Form 1040 line 4a $43,000; line 4b = $9,000 + $29,700 = **$38,700**. Pat is over 59½, so no Form 5329.

**Part III.** Sam Sample (000-00-0082), single, age 45, opened a Roth IRA in 2019 and contributed $8,000 over the
years; in 2022 Sam converted $3,000 (fully taxable then). In 2025 Sam withdrew $12,000 (Form 1099-R code J). No earlier
Roth distributions, no first home.
