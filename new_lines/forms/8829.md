# Form 8829 — Expenses for Business Use of Your Home

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\forms\8829.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\forms\8829.md`.

The form wording is quoted from the 2025 form itself, `f8829.pdf` (kept in `C:\us-tax\docs\IRS-Forms\`, one page,

## 1. At a glance

Form 8829 figures how much of the cost of a home can be deducted on **Schedule C line 30** by a sole proprietor who
uses part of the home for the business and claims **actual expenses** (not the $5-per-square-foot simplified method).
It works in three tiers, in a fixed order:

1. **Tier 1 — expenses that would be deductible anyway** (casualty losses, mortgage interest, real estate taxes on
   lines 9–11): the business part is allowed in full, even if it creates a loss.
2. **Tier 2 — operating expenses** (excess mortgage interest and taxes, insurance, rent, repairs, utilities, other,
   plus last year's operating carryover): allowed only up to the business income left after tier 1.
3. **Tier 3 — excess casualty losses and depreciation of the home** (plus last year's carryover of these): allowed
   only up to what is left after tier 2.

Whatever tiers 2 and 3 cannot use is carried to 2026 (Part IV). The casualty part of the allowed amount (line 35)
goes to Form 4684 instead of Schedule C.

```text
line 7  = business % (area; daycare adjusted by hours)
line 8  = Schedule C line 29 + home-use gains − unrelated losses
line 14 = direct(9..11) + indirect(9..11) × line 7              (tier 1, unlimited)
line 15 = max(0, line 8 − line 14)
line 26 = direct(16..22) + indirect(16..22) × line 7 + line 25   (tier 2)
line 27 = min(line 15, line 26)
line 28 = line 15 − line 27
line 32 = line 29 + line 30 (home depreciation, line 42) + line 31 (tier 3)
line 33 = min(line 28, line 32)
line 34 = line 14 + line 27 + line 33
line 36 = line 34 − line 35 (casualty part)      → Schedule C line 30
line 43 = max(0, line 26 − line 27)  line 44 = max(0, line 32 − line 33)   → 2026 lines 25 / 31
```

Terms used below:

- **Direct expense** — benefits only the business part of the home (for example painting the office); entered at
  100% in column (a).
- **Indirect expense** — for running the whole home (utilities, insurance, rent); entered at 100% in column (b) and
  multiplied by the business percentage.
- **Gross income limit** — the rule (IRC §280A(c)(5)) that tier 2 and tier 3 cannot exceed the business income
  from the home use; it is what lines 8, 15, 27, 28 and 33 apply.
- **Simplified method** — $5 × up to 300 square feet, figured on the Schedule C Simplified Method Worksheet instead
  of Form 8829; documented in `schedule-c.md`.

## 2. The IRS wording

**Form 8829 (2025) heading** (`f8829.pdf p. 1`): "File only with Schedule C (Form 1040). Use a separate Form 8829
for each home you used for business during the year."

**Purpose** (`i8829.pdf p. 1`):

> "Use Form 8829 to figure the allowable expenses for business use of your home on Schedule C (Form 1040) and any
> carryover to 2026 of amounts not deductible in 2025. Use a separate Form 8829 for each home you used for the
> business during the year."

**Who cannot use Form 8829** (`i8829.pdf p. 1`): partners and Schedule F filers ("complete the Worksheet To Figure
the Deduction for Business Use of Your Home in Pub. 587"); employees ("You can't deduct these expenses on your
personal return"); expenses "properly allocable to inventory costs" (Schedule C Part III); and a home for which "You
have elected to use the simplified method".

**Qualifying use** (`i8829.pdf p. 1`): "you can deduct business expenses that apply to a part of your home only if
that part is exclusively used on a regular basis: • As your principal place of business …; • As a place of business
used by your patients, clients, or customers …; or • In connection with your trade or business if it is a separate
structure that is not attached to your home." Exceptions to exclusive use: storage of inventory or product samples,
and certain daycare facilities (`i8829.pdf p. 1–2`).

**The ordering rule in plain words** (`p334.pdf p. 42`):

> "Your deduction of otherwise nondeductible expenses, such as insurance, utilities, and depreciation (with
> depreciation taken last), allocable to the business is limited to the gross income from the business use of your
> home minus the sum of the following. 1. The business part of expenses you could deduct even if you didn't use your
> home for business (such as mortgage interest, real estate taxes, and casualty and theft losses that are allowable
> as itemized deductions on Schedule A (Form 1040)). 2. The business expenses that relate to the business activity
> in the home (for example, business phone, supplies, and depreciation on equipment), but not to the use of the home
> itself. Don't include in (2) above your deduction for one-half of your SE tax."

**Line 8** (`i8829.pdf p. 2`):

> "If all the gross income from your trade or business is from the business use of your home, enter on line 8 the
> amount from Schedule(s) C, line 29, plus any gain derived from the business use of your home and shown on Form 8949
> (and included on Schedule D (Form 1040)) or Form 4797, minus any loss shown on Form 8949 … or Form 4797 that is
> allocable to the trade or business in which you use your home but is not allocable to the use of the home."

**Carryover** (`i8829.pdf p. 6`): "If your expenses are greater than the current year's limit, you can carry over the
excess to 2026. The carryover will be subject to the deduction limit for that year, whether or not you live in the
same home during that year."

## 3. What goes on this form

- The area of the home used regularly and exclusively for the business, regularly for daycare, or for storage of
  inventory or product samples (line 1), and the whole area (line 2).
- For a daycare that is not used exclusively for business: the hours of daycare use (lines 4–6).
- Schedule C line 29 (tentative profit) of the business that uses this home, adjusted for home-use gains and
  unrelated losses (line 8).
- Expenses that exist only because the home is used: casualty losses on the home, deductible mortgage interest, real
  estate taxes, excess mortgage interest, excess real estate taxes, insurance, rent, repairs and maintenance,
  utilities, other home expenses (lines 9–22), split into direct (a) and indirect (b).
- Last year's unallowed amounts (lines 25 and 31).
- The home's basis or value, land value and the depreciation percentage (lines 37–42).

## 6. How to figure it, step by step (every line of the 2025 form)

### Part I — Part of your home used for business

1. **Line 1** — "Area used regularly and exclusively for business, regularly for daycare, or for storage of
   inventory or product samples". Leave out area used for inventory costs already in Schedule C Part III
   (`i8829.pdf p. 2`).
2. **Line 2** — "Total area of home".
3. **Line 3** — line 1 ÷ line 2, as a percentage.
   "For daycare facilities not used exclusively for business, go to line 4. All others, go to line 7."
   (`f8829.pdf p. 1`)
4. **Line 4** — days used for daycare × hours per day ("Enter the total number of hours the facility was used for
   daycare during the year", example: 250 days × 12 h + 50 × 8 h = 3,400 hours, `i8829.pdf p. 2`).
5. **Line 5** — 8,760 hours; if daycare started or stopped in 2025, "multiply 24 hours by the number of days
   available" (`i8829.pdf p. 2`).
6. **Line 6** — line 4 ÷ line 5, as a decimal.
7. **Line 7** — business percentage: for a non-exclusive daycare, line 6 × line 3; otherwise line 3. If the daycare
   has both exclusive and partly-used areas, add the two percentages and attach the computation (`i8829.pdf p. 2`,
   "Special Computation for Certain Daycare Facilities").

### Part II — Figure your allowable deduction

8. **Line 8** — Schedule C line 29 + gain from the business use of the home (Form 8949 / Schedule D / Form 4797) −
   losses allocable to the business but not to the home use. If part of the income is earned at another location,
   first find the part of gross income (Schedule C line 7 plus gains) that comes from the home use, then subtract the
   Schedule C line 28 expenses and the unrelated losses. With more than one Form 8829, use only income and deductions
   of the period the home was owned (`i8829.pdf p. 2`). Schedule C line 29 is after line 13 "Depreciation and section
   179 expense deduction" and every other line 8–27 expense (`f1040sc.pdf p. 1`), so equipment depreciation and the
   §179 deduction reduce line 8 (`p334.pdf p. 42`: "depreciation on equipment").

   Columns (a) and (b) for lines 9–22: direct expenses at 100% in (a); indirect at 100% in (b); an indirect expense
   whose business share differs from line 7 goes in (a) at its business amount only (`i8829.pdf p. 2–3`). If the
   business ran only part of the year, enter only expenses of that part (`i8829.pdf p. 2`).

9. **Line 9** — casualty losses. Only the business-home part of a **federally declared disaster** loss figured on a
   worksheet Form 4684 Section A (Steps 1–2), column (b). A standard-deduction filer uses line 9 only when the
   standard deduction is increased by a net qualified disaster loss; otherwise all business casualty loss goes on
   line 29 (`i8829.pdf p. 3`).
10. **Line 10** — deductible mortgage interest, column (b) only, and **only if itemizing**: the interest that would
    be deductible on Schedule A after the Pub. 936 limits, attributable to this home. "do not claim mortgage interest
    in column (a) as a direct expense" (`i8829.pdf p. 3`).
11. **Line 11** — real estate taxes, **only if itemizing**. If income + real estate + personal property taxes are not
    more than $10,000 ($5,000 MFS), all real estate taxes on this home go in column (b). Otherwise use the **Line 11
    Worksheet** (`i8829.pdf p. 3–4`): line 5 = all SALT; line 6 = this home's real estate taxes × line 7; line 7a =
    line 5 − line 6; line 8 = the SALT limit ($10,000 / $40,000 or the iterated limit; $5,000 / $20,000 MFS);
    line 9 = max(0, line 8 − line 7a); line 10 = min(line 6, line 9) → **column (a)** of Form 8829 line 11; line 11
    = line 6 − line 10 → Form 8829 line 17. When MAGI may exceed $500,000 ($250,000 MFS) the Iteration Instructions
    repeat the Schedule C / SE / AGI computation until MAGI changes by $1 or less (`i8829.pdf p. 4`).
12. **Line 12** — lines 9 + 10 + 11, each column.
13. **Line 13** — line 12 column (b) × line 7.
14. **Line 14** — line 12 column (a) + line 13. Allowed in full.
15. **Line 15** — line 8 − line 14; "If zero or less, enter -0-".
16. **Line 16** — excess mortgage interest: a standard-deduction filer enters **all** interest on loans that bought,
    built or substantially improved this home (not, for example, a home-equity loan for a car); an itemizer enters
    the part of such interest not deductible on Schedule A because of the limits (example: $15,000 paid, $12,000
    deductible → $3,000) (`i8829.pdf p. 4`).
17. **Line 17** — excess real estate taxes: standard-deduction filer, all real estate taxes on this home in
    column (b); itemizer, Line 11 Worksheet line 11 in column (a), else nothing (`i8829.pdf p. 4–5`).
18. **Line 18** — insurance.
19. **Line 19** — rent: "If you rent rather than own your home, include the rent you paid on line 19, column (b)"
    (`i8829.pdf p. 5`).
20. **Line 20** — repairs and maintenance.
21. **Line 21** — utilities.
22. **Line 22** — other expenses: "any 2025 operating expenses not included on lines 9 through 21" (`i8829.pdf p. 5`).
23. **Line 23** — lines 16 through 22, each column.
24. **Line 24** — line 23 column (b) × line 7.
25. **Line 25** — carryover of prior-year operating expenses: 2024 Form 8829 line 43; if no 2024 Form 8829, Part IV
    of the last one filed; after a simplified-method 2024, line 6a of the 2024 Simplified Method Worksheet
    (`i8829.pdf p. 5`).
26. **Line 26** — line 23 column (a) + line 24 + line 25.
27. **Line 27** — allowable operating expenses = smaller of line 15 or line 26.
28. **Line 28** — line 15 − line 27 (room left for tier 3).
29. **Line 29** — excess casualty losses: "Multiply the casualty losses attributable to the home … that are in
    excess of the amount reported on line 9 (if any) by the business percentage of those losses" (`i8829.pdf p. 5`).
30. **Line 30** — depreciation of the home, from line 42.
31. **Line 31** — carryover of prior-year excess casualty losses and depreciation: 2024 line 44, or the last Form 8829,
    or 2024 Simplified Method Worksheet line 6b (`i8829.pdf p. 5`).
32. **Line 32** — lines 29 + 30 + 31.
33. **Line 33** — smaller of line 28 or line 32.
34. **Line 34** — lines 14 + 27 + 33.
35. **Line 35** — "Casualty loss portion, if any, from lines 14 and 33. Carry amount to Form 4684." Enter it on Form
    4684 line 27 with "See Form 8829" (`i8829.pdf p. 5`). The instructions do not say how to split line 33 between
    casualty (line 29) and depreciation (line 30) — Pub. 587 would; not available here.
36. **Line 36** — line 34 − line 35 → **Schedule C line 30**. "If your home was used in more than one business,
    allocate the amount shown on line 36 to each business using any method that is reasonable … enter on Schedule C,
    line 30, only the amount allocated to that business" (`i8829.pdf p. 5`).

### Part III — Depreciation of your home

37. **Line 37** — smaller of the home's cost or other basis (including land) or its fair market value on the date first
    used for business; not adjusted later (`i8829.pdf p. 5`).
38. **Line 38** — land: its basis or, if less, its fair market value on that date.
39. **Line 39** — line 37 − line 38 (building basis).
40. **Line 40** — line 39 × line 7. Additions and improvements placed in service after business use began are **not**
    included in lines 37–40; they are figured separately and attached (`i8829.pdf p. 5`).
41. **Line 41** — depreciation percentage (39-year nonresidential real property, mid-month): first used in 2025 —
    January 2.461%, February 2.247%, March 2.033%, April 1.819%, May 1.605%, June 1.391%, July 1.177%, August 0.963%,
    September 0.749%, October 0.535%, November 0.321%, December 0.107%; first used after May 12, 1993 and before 2025
    — 2.564%; stopped using during 2025, before May 13, 1993, or before 1987 — Pub. 946 / Pub. 534 percentages
    (`i8829.pdf p. 5`). A home first used in 2024 under the simplified method uses the table as well (example: 2.564%).
42. **Line 42** — line 40 × line 41, plus the attached computation for additions and improvements (each at its own
    month-placed-in-service percentage or 2.564%) (`i8829.pdf p. 6`). Enter on line 30. If the home was first used
    for business in 2025, or improvements were placed in service in 2025, also complete Form 4562 line 19j (columns
    (b) month/year, (c) line 40, (g) line 42) — and do not put that amount on Schedule C line 13.

### Part IV — Carryover of unallowed expenses to 2026

43. **Line 43** — operating expenses: line 26 − line 27, not below 0.
44. **Line 44** — excess casualty losses and depreciation: line 32 − line 33, not below 0.

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- Form 8829 is figured per home and per Schedule C business. On a joint return each spouse's business has its own
  Schedule C, so each home office is attached to the Schedule C of the spouse who runs that business ("Name(s) of
  proprietor(s)", `f8829.pdf p. 1`).
- A qualified joint venture (both spouses each file a Schedule C for one business) follows the simplified-method
  instruction to split the area "in the same manner you split your other tax attributes" (`i1040sc.pdf p. 11`); for
  Form 8829 the instructions say nothing more.
- The Line 11 Worksheet uses the MFS amounts ($5,000 / $20,000 / $250,000) on separate returns (`i8829.pdf p. 4`).
- Carryovers belong to the home's business use and continue "whether or not you live in the same home"
  (`i8829.pdf p. 6`); they follow the person whose Form 8829 produced them.

## 10. Where the amount goes next

- **Line 36 → Schedule C line 30** ("Enter here and on Schedule C, line 30", `f8829.pdf p. 1`), and so into
  Schedule C line 31 → Schedule 1 line 3 → Form 1040 line 8 / line 9; Schedule SE; the QBI deduction (Form 8995 /
  8995-A) through Schedule C line 31.
- **Line 35 → Form 4684 line 27** ("See Form 8829"), and through Form 4684 Section B to Form 4797 / Schedule 1
  line 4 (ordinary loss) or Schedule A (`i8829.pdf p. 5`).
- **Personal part of mortgage interest and real estate taxes → Schedule A** lines 8 / 5b; the business part,
  including Line 11 Worksheet line 6, is **not** on Schedule A line 5b (`i8829.pdf p. 3–4`).
- **Lines 43 and 44 → 2026 Form 8829 lines 25 and 31** (or 2026 Simplified Method Worksheet lines 6a / 6b if that
  method is used in 2026).
- **Line 40 / 42 → Form 4562 line 19j** in the first year of business use (`i8829.pdf p. 6`).

## 11. Worked example

Casey Example (000-00-0011) runs a bookkeeping business from a 200 sq ft office in a 2,000 sq ft house owned since
2018 and used for the business since 2022. Casey itemizes; total state income + property taxes are $8,000 (under
$10,000). Schedule C line 29 is $3,000. Last year's Form 8829 line 43 was $500, line 44 $0.

| 12 (b) | 12,000 + 4,000 | $16,000 |
| 13 / 14 | 16,000 × 10% | $1,600 |
| 15 | 3,000 − 1,600 | $1,400 |
| 18 (b) | Insurance | $1,200 |
| 20 (a) | Painting the office | $400 |
| 21 (b) | Utilities | $3,000 |
| 23 | (a) 400; (b) 4,200 | |
| 24 | 4,200 × 10% | $420 |
| 25 | 2024 line 43 | $500 |
| 26 | 400 + 420 + 500 | $1,320 |
| 27 | smaller of 1,400 / 1,320 | $1,320 |
| 28 | 1,400 − 1,320 | $80 |
| 37 / 38 / 39 | basis $300,000 (FMV $350,000) / land $50,000 | $250,000 |
