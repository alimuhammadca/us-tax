## 1. At a glance

A health savings account (HSA) is a tax-favored account for medical costs, open to an "eligible individual" — someone
covered by a high deductible health plan (HDHP) with no other health coverage (apart from disregarded coverage), not
enrolled in Medicare and not someone else's dependent. Form 8889 does three things, each per account beneficiary:

- **Part I** — the deduction for contributions you (or others on your behalf) made: the 2025 limit ($4,300 self-only /
  $8,550 family, +$1,000 at age 55), reduced by employer contributions (Form W-2 box 12 code W), Archer MSA
  contributions and qualified HSA funding distributions from an IRA. Line 13 → Schedule 1 line 13.
- **Part II** — distributions (Form 1099-SA): what was not used for qualified medical expenses is taxable (line 16 →
  Schedule 1 line 8f) and, unless the beneficiary died, became disabled or was 65+, carries a 20% additional tax
  (line 17b → Schedule 2 line 17c).
- **Part III** — failing the 12-month testing period after using the last-month rule or making an HSA funding
  distribution: the amount is income (line 20 → Schedule 1 line 8f) plus a 10% additional tax (line 21 → Schedule 2
  line 17d).

```text
Part I   line 3  = limit by coverage and months (worksheet)      line 5 = max(0, 3 − 4 Archer MSA)
         line 6  = line 5, or the spouse allocation (family)     line 7 = married 55+ family catch-up
         line 8  = 6 + 7     line 11 = 9 employer (W-2 box 12 W) + 10 HSA funding distribution
         line 12 = max(0, 8 − 11)       line 13 = min(line 2 own contributions, line 12)  → Schedule 1 line 13
Part II  line 14c = 14a 1099-SA box 1 − 14b rollovers / excess withdrawn
         line 16 = max(0, 14c − 15 qualified medical expenses)   → Schedule 1 line 8f
         line 17b = 20% × (line 16 not meeting an exception)     → Schedule 2 line 17c
Part III line 20 = 18 last-month-rule failure + 19 funding-distribution failure → Schedule 1 line 8f
         line 21 = 10% × line 20                                 → Schedule 2 line 17d
```

Terms used below:

- **HDHP** — high deductible health plan: for 2025 at least a $1,650 (self-only) / $3,300 (family) deductible and
  out-of-pocket costs of at most $8,300 / $16,600 (`i8889.pdf p. 3`).
- **Account beneficiary** — "the individual on whose behalf the HSA was established" (`i8889.pdf p. 2`).
- **Last-month rule** — eligible on December 1 → treated as eligible all year for the limit (`i8889.pdf p. 2`).
- **Testing period** — December 1, 2025 through December 31, 2026 for the last-month rule; for an HSA funding
  distribution, the month of the contribution through the 12th following month (`i8889.pdf p. 2, p. 8`).
- **Qualified HSA funding distribution (HFD)** — a once-in-a-lifetime direct trustee-to-trustee transfer from a
  traditional or Roth IRA to the HSA (`i8889.pdf p. 7`).

## 2. The IRS wording

**Who must file** (`i8889.pdf p. 1`): "You must file Form 8889 if any of the following applies. • You, your employer,
or someone else made contributions to your HSA. • Your HSA made a distribution. • You failed to be an 'eligible
individual' during a 'testing period' and must recognize income. • You acquired an interest in an HSA because of the
death of the account beneficiary." And: "If you (or your spouse, if filing jointly) received HSA distributions in
2025, you must file Form 8889 … even if you have no taxable income."

**Eligible individual** (`i8889.pdf p. 2`): "you must be covered under a high deductible health plan (HDHP) and have
no other health coverage except certain disregarded coverage. … you cannot be enrolled in Medicare or be another
person's dependent. … You must be, or be considered, an eligible individual on the first day of a month to take an
HSA deduction for that month."

**Limits** (`i8889.pdf p. 4`): "If you have self-only coverage, your maximum contribution is $4,300. If you have family
coverage, your maximum contribution is $8,550. Note. If you are age 55 or older at the end of your tax year, you can
make an additional contribution of $1,000. Your maximum contribution is reduced by any employer contributions to your
HSA, any contributions made to your Archer MSA, and any qualified HSA funding distributions. You cannot deduct any
contributions for any month in which you were enrolled in Medicare. Also, you cannot deduct contributions if you are
someone else's dependent for 2025."

**Line 13** (`i8889.pdf p. 8`): "Generally, enter the smaller of line 2 or line 12 on line 13 and on Schedule 1 (Form
1040), Part II, line 13."

**Line 16** (`f8889.pdf p. 1`): "Taxable HSA distributions. Subtract line 15 from line 14c. If zero or less, enter -0-.
Also, include this amount in the total on Schedule 1 (Form 1040), Part I, line 8f".

**Schedule 1 / Schedule 2** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 91, p. 93, p. 113`): line 8f "Enter the total of the amounts from
Form 8889, lines 16 and 20"; line 13 "You may be able to take this deduction if contributions (other than employer
contributions, rollovers, and qualified HSA funding distributions from an IRA) were made to your HSA for 2025";
Schedule 2 line 17c "additional tax on health savings account (HSA) distributions … from Form 8889, line 17b"; line 17d
"additional tax for failure to remain an eligible individual during the testing period from Form 8889, line 21".

## 3. What goes on this form

- HDHP coverage type (line 1) and the beneficiary's SSN in the heading.
- Own and third-party contributions for 2025, including those made by April 15, 2026 (line 2).
- The contribution limit and its reductions (lines 3–12) and the deduction (line 13).
- All HSA distributions in 2025 (Form 1099-SA box 1), rollovers / returned excess, and qualified medical expenses
  paid with them (lines 14a–16), with the 20% additional tax (lines 17a–17b).
- Income and 10% tax for failing a testing period (lines 18–21).

## 6. How to fill it in, line by line

### Part I — HSA contributions and deduction (one Part I per spouse with an HSA)

1. **Line 1 — coverage.** Check the plan in effect longer; covered by both at once → family; "If, on the first day of
   the last month of your tax year (December 1 for most taxpayers), you had family coverage, check the 'family' box"
   (`i8889.pdf p. 4`).
2. **Line 2 — contributions you made** for 2025, including those by April 15, 2026; not employer or cafeteria-plan
   contributions, rollovers or HFDs (`i8889.pdf p. 4`).
3. **Line 3 — limit** (`i8889.pdf p. 4-6`): family amount if you or your spouse had family coverage; under the
   last-month rule you are treated as eligible all year with your December 1 coverage; eligible all year with no
   change → $4,300 / $8,550; changed coverage → the greater of the Line 3 Limitation Chart and Worksheet result and the
   December 1 coverage amount; not eligible on December 1 → the worksheet. **Line 3 worksheet** (`p. 6`): for each
   month — enrolled in Medicare → $0; not eligible on the 1st → $0; self-only → $4,300 ($5,300 if 55+ at year end);
   family → $8,550 ($9,550 if unmarried and 55+); total ÷ 12. Rule 6: if 55+ and unmarried, or married with
   self-only coverage all year, add $1,000 to line 3; married with family coverage at any time → the $1,000 goes on
   line 7 instead (`p. 4-5`).
4. **Line 4 — Archer MSA** contributions by you and your employer (Form 8853 lines 1 and 2), plus your spouse's Archer
   MSAs if either of you had family HDHP coverage (`f8889.pdf p. 1`).
5. **Line 5** = line 3 − line 4, not below 0.
6. **Line 6** = line 5, except spouses with separate HSAs and family coverage: divide the family limit equally "unless
   you both agree on a different allocation"; for part-year family coverage, Steps 1–4 refigure the family part and
   add any other limit (`i8889.pdf p. 7`). "If you and your spouse are considered covered by a family HDHP, you are
   considered covered by a family HDHP regardless of whether you file jointly or separately" (`p. 4`).
7. **Line 7 — additional contribution** for someone 55+, married, with family coverage: $1,000 if every month
   qualified, else the Additional Contribution Amount Worksheet ($1,000 × eligible months ÷ 12, months not in
   Medicare) (`i8889.pdf p. 7`).
8. **Line 8** = line 6 + line 7.
9. **Line 9 — employer contributions** (including cafeteria plan), "shown on Form W-2, box 12, code W"; use the
   Employer Contribution Worksheet when 2024 contributions are in the 2025 W-2 or 2025 contributions were made in 2026
   (`i8889.pdf p. 7`).
10. **Line 10 — qualified HSA funding distribution.** Not income, not deductible, reduces the limit; one per lifetime
    (a second allowed in the same year after switching from self-only to family coverage); not from an ongoing SEP or
    SIMPLE IRA (`i8889.pdf p. 7`).
11. **Line 11** = line 9 + line 10. **Line 12** = line 8 − line 11, not below 0.
12. **Line 13 — deduction** = smaller of line 2 and line 12 → Schedule 1 line 13 (`i8889.pdf p. 8`). On a joint return
    with two Forms 8889, "Combine the amounts on line 13 of both Forms 8889" (`p. 4`). Excess contributions = line 2 −
    line 13 (`p. 8`); excess employer contributions = employer contributions over line 8 (reduced by any HFD) — "If the
    excess was not included in income on Form W-2, you must report it as 'Other income'" (`p. 8`). Either excess may
    be withdrawn with its earnings by the due date (including extensions) and then counts as not contributed; the
    earnings are other income (`p. 8`). A prior-year excess may be absorbed by this year's unused room (Form 5329 line
    43) (`p. 8`).

### Part II — HSA distributions

13. **Line 14a** — total distributions from all HSAs, "shown on Form 1099-SA, box 1" (`i8889.pdf p. 8`). Death of a
    beneficiary who is not the spouse: "On Part II, line 14a, enter the fair market value of the HSA as of the date of
    death", skip Part I (`p. 3`); the 1099-SA reports that value in box 4 (`i1099sa--2025.pdf p. 2`). A prohibited
    transaction: the account's FMV on January 1, 2025 goes on line 14a (`i8889.pdf p. 3`).
14. **Line 14b** — rollovers to another HSA and excess contributions (with earnings) withdrawn by the due date
    (`i8889.pdf p. 8`). **Line 14c** = 14a − 14b.
15. **Line 15** — distributions used for qualified medical expenses, not reimbursed, incurred after the HSA was
    established, of you, your spouse, your dependents (and certain would-be dependents) (`i8889.pdf p. 8-9`). Not for
    an excess taken out after the due date.
16. **Line 16** = 14c − 15, not below 0 → Schedule 1 line 8f.
17. **Lines 17a / 17b** — "HSA distributions included in income (line 16) are subject to an additional 20% tax unless
    … made after the account beneficiary: • Dies, • Becomes disabled or • Turns age 65. If any of the exceptions apply
    to any of the distributions included on line 16, check the box on line 17a. Enter on line 17b only 20% (0.20) of
    any amount included on line 16 that does not meet any of the exceptions" (`i8889.pdf p. 9`). Line 17b → Schedule 2
    line 17c. A death distribution "is not subject to the additional 20% tax" (`p. 3`).

### Part III — Failure to maintain HDHP coverage

18. **Line 18 — last-month rule.** Using the Line 3 worksheet for the year of the contribution, "Enter on line 18 the
    excess of the amount contributed over the redetermined amount" (`i8889.pdf p. 9`). Include it in the year you stop
    being eligible (other than by death or disability) (`p. 2`).
19. **Line 19** — "the total of any qualified HSA funding distribution" for a failed HFD testing period (`i8889.pdf
    p. 8-9`).
20. **Line 20** = 18 + 19 → Schedule 1 line 8f. **Line 21** = 10% × line 20 → Schedule 2 line 17d (`f8889.pdf p. 1`).

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Death, spouse beneficiary | HSA becomes the spouse's; spouse files as owner | `i8889.pdf p. 3` |
| Death, other beneficiary | "Death of HSA account beneficiary" on top; skip Part I; line 14a = FMV at death; line 15 = decedent's expenses paid within 1 year; no 20% tax | `i8889.pdf p. 3` |
| Two or more HSAs as beneficiary + own | A "statement" Form 8889 per HSA plus a controlling Form 8889 | `i8889.pdf p. 3` |
| Prohibited transaction | Account ceases to be an HSA on January 1; FMV on line 14a; generally 20% tax | `i8889.pdf p. 3` |
| Pledged as loan security | FMV of pledged assets on Schedule 1 line 8f | `i8889.pdf p. 3` |
| Mistaken distributions | May be returned in limited cases (Notice 2004-50) | `i8889.pdf p. 9` |
| Veterans' care, surprise-billing benefits, telehealth (P.L. 119-21) | Do not end eligibility | `i8889.pdf p. 1-2` |
| Combat zone | Contribution deadline extended | `i8889.pdf p. 4` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

- Each spouse with an HSA completes a separate Form 8889; line 13 amounts are added on Schedule 1 line 13
  (`i8889.pdf p. 4`).
- If both are eligible and either has family coverage, both are treated as having only the family plan; the family
  limit (line 5) is divided equally unless they agree otherwise — on joint **or separate** returns (`p. 4`, `p. 7`).
- The $1,000 additional amount is the individual's own and "is not allocable among spouses" (`p. 4`).
- Medical expenses of a spouse and dependents qualify (`p. 2`, `p. 8`).

## 9. Forms to attach or keep

- **Attach** Form 8889 (one per spouse with an HSA; statements + controlling form when there are several HSAs) whenever
  any filing condition applies (`i8889.pdf p. 1, p. 3-4`).
- **Keep** the Line 3 Limitation Chart and Worksheet, the Additional Contribution Amount Worksheet, the Employer
  Contribution Worksheet (`p. 6-7`), receipts for qualified medical expenses.
- Form 5329 Part VII when excess contributions remain (`p. 8`).

## 11. Worked example

Sam Okafor (fictional, SSN 000-00-0002), single, age 57 on December 31, 2025, self-only HDHP all year, not in
Medicare, not a dependent. He contributed $3,000; his W-2 box 12 code W shows $1,500. He received one Form 1099-SA
(box 1 $2,000, code 1) and paid $1,200 of qualified medical expenses with it.

1. Line 1 self-only. Line 2 = $3,000.
2. Line 3: eligible all year, unmarried and 55+, so the $1,000 is added on line 3 → $4,300 + $1,000 = **$5,300**.
3. Line 4 = 0; line 5 = $5,300; line 6 = $5,300; line 7 = 0 (line 7 is only for married filers with family
   coverage); line 8 = $5,300.
4. Line 9 = $1,500; line 10 = 0; line 11 = $1,500; line 12 = $3,800.
5. Line 13 = smaller of $3,000 and $3,800 = **$3,000** → Schedule 1 line 13.
6. Line 14a = $2,000; 14b = 0; 14c = $2,000; line 15 = $1,200; line 16 = **$800** → Schedule 1 line 8f.
7. Line 17b: no exception (age 57, alive, not disabled) → 20% × $800 = **$160** → Schedule 2 line 17c.
8. Part III: none.
