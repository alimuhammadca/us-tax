# Form 982 — Reduction of Tax Attributes Due to Discharge of Indebtedness (and Section 1082 Basis Adjustment)

Tax year 2025. What the IRS requires. How the app does it: `C:\us-tax\new_knowledge\forms\982.md`. Picture:
`C:\us-tax\new_flowcharts\forms\982.md`. The income line it reduces is documented in `C:\us-tax\new_lines\8.md`
(Schedule 1 line 8c).

(Rev. March 2018)" (`i982.pdf p. 1`); no later revision exists in `C:\us-tax\docs\books\`. The form face itself is not

## 1. At a glance

A canceled ("discharged") debt is normally income: Form 1040 instructions tell you to enter it on Schedule 1 line 8c
("Canceled debt may be shown in box 2 of Form 1099-C. However, part or all of your income from cancellation of debt may
be nontaxable", `i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 91`). Section 108 of the Internal Revenue Code lets you leave part or all of it
out of income in five situations — a bankruptcy (title 11) case, insolvency, qualified farm debt, qualified real
property business debt (QRPBI) and qualified principal residence debt (QPRI). Form 982 is where you claim that
exclusion (Part I) and, in return, give up "tax attributes" — carryovers and basis — in a fixed order (Part II).
Part III is for corporations only.

```text
Part I     line 1a–1e  = why the debt is excluded (check boxes)
           line 2      = total canceled debt excluded from gross income   (each box limited — see §6)
           line 3      = Yes/No election to treat dealer real property as depreciable (section 1221(a)(1))
Schedule 1 line 8c     = canceled debt − Form 982 line 2                  (the taxable remainder)

Part II    the amount excluded under 1a / 1b / 1c reduces, in this order unless line 1d or a line 5 election applies:
           line 5  (election) basis of depreciable property first
           line 6  net operating loss (NOL)                       $1 for $1
           line 7  general business credit carryover              33⅓ cents per $1
           line 8  minimum tax credit                             33⅓ cents per $1
           line 9  net capital loss and carryover                 $1 for $1
           line 10a basis of property                             $1 for $1   (farm debt: lines 11a–11c instead)
           line 12 passive activity loss (and credit) carryovers  $1 for $1 (credits 33⅓ cents)
           line 13 foreign tax credit carryover                   33⅓ cents per $1
           line 4  QRPBI exclusion → basis of depreciable real property (its own track)
           line 10b QPRI exclusion → basis of the main home, if you still own it (its own track)
```

Terms used below:

- **Discharge of indebtedness** — "forgiveness of, or release from, an obligation to repay" (`i982.pdf p. 1`).
- **Title 11 case** — a bankruptcy case under title 11 of the U.S. Code, "but only if you are under the jurisdiction of
  the court in the case and the discharge of indebtedness is granted by the court or is under a plan approved by the
  court" (`i982.pdf p. 1`).
- **Insolvent** — your liabilities exceed the fair market value (FMV) of your assets immediately before the discharge;
  you are insolvent "to the extent" of that excess (`i982.pdf p. 2`).
- **Tax attribute** — a tax benefit carried to later years (NOL, credit carryovers, capital loss carryover, passive
  loss carryover) or the basis of property; Part II reduces them.
- **Basis** — generally what you paid for property plus improvements; it sets gain or loss when the property is sold.
- **QPRI** — qualified principal residence indebtedness: a mortgage taken out to buy, build or substantially improve
  your main home and secured by it (`i982.pdf p. 3`).
- **QRPBI** — qualified real property business indebtedness: debt incurred or assumed in connection with real
  property used in a trade or business, secured by it, for which you elect the exclusion (`i982.pdf p. 3`).

## 2. The IRS wording

**Purpose** (`i982.pdf p. 1`): "Generally, the amount by which you benefit from the discharge of indebtedness is
included in your gross income. However, under certain circumstances described in section 108, you can exclude the
amount of discharged indebtedness from your gross income. You must file Form 982 to report the exclusion and the
reduction of certain tax attributes either dollar for dollar or 33⅓ cents per dollar."

**When to file** (`i982.pdf p. 1`): "File Form 982 with your federal income tax return for a year a discharge of
indebtedness is excluded from your income under section 108(a)." The section 108(b)(5) basis election and the line 1d
QRPBI election "must be made on a timely filed return (including extensions) and can be revoked only with the consent
of the IRS"; a late election can be made "by filing an amended return within 6 months of the due date".

**Form lines** (`docs/IRS-Forms/f982.pdf p. 1`): "1 Amount excluded is due to (check applicable box(es)): a Discharge
of indebtedness in a title 11 case; b … to the extent insolvent (not in a title 11 case); c … of qualified farm
indebtedness; d … of qualified real property business indebtedness; e … of qualified principal residence
indebtedness". "2 Total amount of discharged indebtedness excluded from gross income". Part II heading: "Reduction of
Tax Attributes. You must attach a description of any transactions resulting in the reduction in basis under section
1017."

**QPRI for 2025** (`i982.pdf p. 1`): "Qualified principal residence indebtedness can be excluded from income for
discharges before January 1, 2026 … The maximum amount you can treat as qualified principal residence indebtedness is
$750,000 ($375,000 if married filing separately)."

**Attribute order** (`i982.pdf p. 2`): "The reduction must be made in the following order unless you check the box on
line 1d … or make the election on line 5 …: 1. Any net operating loss (NOL) for the tax year of the discharge (and
any NOL carryover to that year) (dollar for dollar); 2. Any general business credit carryover to or from the tax year
of the discharge (33⅓ cents per dollar); 3. Any minimum tax credit as of the beginning of the tax year immediately
after the tax year of the discharge (33⅓ cents per dollar); 4. Any net capital loss for the tax year of the discharge
(and any capital loss carryover to that tax year) (dollar for dollar); 5. The basis of property (dollar for dollar);
6. Any passive activity loss (dollar for dollar) and credit (33⅓ cents per dollar) carryovers from the tax year of the
discharge; and 7. Any foreign tax credit carryover to or from the tax year of the discharge (33⅓ cents per dollar)."

**Schedule 1 line 8c** (`i1040gi_ty2025.pdf p. 91`): "Cancellation of debt. Enter any canceled debt. Canceled debt may
be shown in box 2 of Form 1099-C. However, part or all of your income from cancellation of debt may be nontaxable. See
Pub. 4681 …"

## 3. What goes on this form

- Which of the five exclusions applies (line 1, one or more boxes).
- The total canceled debt you exclude (line 2), each part within its own limit.
- The Yes/No section 1221(a)(1) election (line 3), if you hold real property for sale to customers.
- How the exclusion reduced each tax attribute (lines 4–13), with a statement describing basis reductions.
- For a corporation, the section 1081(b) consent (Part III).

## 6. How to fill it in, line by line

The instructions start with a chart (`i982.pdf p. 1–2`): a **QPRI** filer checks 1e, enters the excluded QPRI on line
2 and, if still owning the home, the smaller of that amount and the home's basis on line 10b. A **nonbusiness debt**
filer with no tax attributes other than basis in nondepreciable property checks 1a or 1b, enters the excluded amount
on line 2 ("If you were insolvent, don't include more than the excess of your liabilities over the fair market value
of your assets") and on line 10a "the smallest of (a) the basis of your nondepreciable property, (b) the amount of the
nonbusiness debt included on line 2, or (c) the excess of the aggregate bases of the property and the amount of money
you held immediately after the discharge over your aggregate liabilities immediately after the discharge". **Any
other debt** uses the full Part I / Part II walk below.

### Part I — General information

1. **Line 1a — title 11 case.** Check it when the discharge is granted by a bankruptcy court or under a court-approved
   plan (`i982.pdf p. 1`). The other exclusions give way: insolvency "doesn't apply to any discharge that occurs in a
   title 11 case" (`p. 2`), nor do the farm, QRPBI (`p. 2–3`) or QPRI (`p. 3`, "You must check the box on line 1a and
   not this box") exclusions. The whole discharge in the case is excluded.
2. **Line 1b — insolvency.** "You were insolvent to the extent that your liabilities exceeded the fair market value
   (FMV) of your assets immediately before the discharge. For details and a worksheet to help calculate insolvency,
   see Pub. 4681" (`i982.pdf p. 2`). The exclusion is limited to the amount of insolvency. Example from the
   instructions: $5,000 credit card debt released; assets $7,000, liabilities $10,000 → insolvent by $3,000 → "Check
   the box on line 1b and include $3,000 on line 2". Insolvency does not apply to QPRI "unless you elect to have the
   insolvency exclusion apply instead of the exclusion for qualified principal residence indebtedness" (`p. 2`).
   **Insolvency worksheet:** the instructions point to Pub. 4681 for it; that publication is not in the source set,
   so its line items are not reproduced here. What the instructions fix is the measure: total liabilities minus total
   FMV of assets, both immediately before the discharge.
3. **Line 1c — qualified farm indebtedness.** Debt "incurred directly in connection with the trade or business of
   farming", and "50% or more of your aggregate gross receipts for the 3 tax years preceding the tax year in which the
   discharge … occurs must be from the trade or business of farming"; the lender must be a "qualified person"
   (actively and regularly lending money, not related to you, not the seller of the property, or a government)
   (`i982.pdf p. 2`). Not available in a title 11 case or "to the extent you were insolvent". Limit: "You can't
   exclude more than the total of your (a) tax attributes …, and (b) basis of property used or held for use in a trade
   or business or for the production of income. Any excess is included in income." Attributes are reduced in the
   order of lines 6–9, then lines 11a–13.
4. **Line 1d — QRPBI.** The excluded amount "is applied to reduce the basis of depreciable real property on line 4"
   (`i982.pdf p. 3`). Not available in a title 11 case or to the extent insolvent; not for a C corporation; post-1992
   debt qualifies only as refinancing of pre-1993 QRPBI or as "qualified acquisition indebtedness". Two limits: (i) no
   more than "the excess of the outstanding principal amount of the debt (immediately before the discharge) over the
   net FMV … of the property securing the debt reduced by the outstanding principal amount of other qualified real
   property business indebtedness secured by that property"; (ii) no more than "the aggregate adjusted basis … of
   depreciable real property … you held immediately before the discharge". "Any excess is included in income."
5. **Line 1e — QPRI.** Check only for a discharge before 2026 (or under a written arrangement entered into before
   January 1, 2026), not in a title 11 case (`i982.pdf p. 3`). The debt is a mortgage to buy, build or substantially
   improve the main home, secured by it; a refinance counts "only up to the amount of the old mortgage principal just
   before the refinancing". Maximum $750,000 ($375,000 married filing separately). **Ordering rule:** "If only a part
   of a loan is qualified principal residence indebtedness, the exclusion applies only to the extent the amount
   discharged exceeds the amount of the loan (immediately before the discharge) that is not qualified principal
   residence indebtedness" — e.g. $1,000,000 loan, $800,000 qualified, $300,000 discharged → only $100,000 excluded;
   the $200,000 non-qualified part "may qualify … for one of the other exclusions, such as the insolvency exclusion".
6. **Line 2 — total excluded.** "Enter the total amount excluded from your gross income due to discharge of
   indebtedness under section 108. If you checked any box on lines 1b through 1e, don't enter more than the limit
   explained in the instructions for those lines" (`i982.pdf p. 3`). Line 2 need not equal the Part II reductions,
   because the discharge "may exceed the total tax attributes", and for 1e the line 10b reduction is required only if
   you keep the home.
7. **Line 3 — section 1221(a)(1) election.** Yes / No: treat all real property held primarily for sale to customers as
   depreciable property for the line 5 election; not for QRPBI (`i982.pdf p. 3`).

### Part II — Reduction of tax attributes

8. **Line 4 — QRPBI basis reduction.** The line 1d exclusion reduces the basis of depreciable real property
   (`i982.pdf p. 3`, Line 1d).
9. **Line 5 — section 108(b)(5) election.** With box 1a, 1b or 1c checked you may elect to apply all or part of the
   exclusion first to the basis of depreciable property (including property elected on line 3); the balance then runs
   through lines 6–13 (excluding 10b). Attach a statement describing the transactions and the property
   (`i982.pdf p. 3`). The line 10a limit for title 11 / insolvency does not apply to a line 5 reduction (`p. 4`).
10. **Line 6 — NOL** for the year of discharge and carryovers to it, dollar for dollar (`i982.pdf p. 2`).
11. **Line 7 — general business credit carryover**, "by 33⅓ cents for each dollar excluded" (`i982.pdf p. 4`).
12. **Line 8 — minimum tax credit** as of the start of 2026, 33⅓ cents per dollar (`i982.pdf p. 2`).
13. **Line 9 — net capital loss** for 2025 and carryovers to 2025, dollar for dollar (`i982.pdf p. 2`).
14. **Line 10a — basis of nondepreciable and depreciable property** (if not reduced on line 5); not for farm debt
    ("DO NOT use in the case of discharge of qualified farm indebtedness", `f982.pdf p. 1`). "In the case of a title 11
    case or insolvency, the reduction in basis is limited to the aggregate of the basis of your property immediately
    after the discharge over the aggregate of your liabilities immediately after the discharge" (`i982.pdf p. 4`).
15. **Line 10b — main home.** "If box 1e is checked and you continue to own the residence after discharge, enter the
    smaller of: That part of line 2 that is attributable to the exclusion of qualified principal residence
    indebtedness, or The basis of your main home" (`i982.pdf p. 4`).
16. **Lines 11a–11c — farm debt** reduces, in order, depreciable property used in a business or for income, farm land,
    and other business / income property (`f982.pdf p. 1`; order per `i982.pdf p. 2`, Line 1c).
17. **Line 12 — passive activity loss and credit carryovers** from 2025: losses dollar for dollar, credits 33⅓ cents
    (`i982.pdf p. 2`).
18. **Line 13 — foreign tax credit carryover**, 33⅓ cents per dollar (`i982.pdf p. 2`).

Anything still unabsorbed after line 13 under a title 11 or insolvency exclusion simply stays excluded — the
instructions say line 2 "won't necessarily equal the total reductions … because the debt discharge amount may exceed
the total tax attributes" (`i982.pdf p. 3`). This is **not** true of farm debt or QRPBI, where the excess "is
included in income" (`p. 2`, `p. 3`).

### Part III — Consent of corporation (section 1082(a)(2))

19. A corporation that excluded income under section 1081(b) consents to adjust the basis of its property under the
    regulations; a variation needs a closing agreement (`i982.pdf p. 4`). Not used on an individual return.

## 7. Special cases and elections

| Case | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Title 11 displaces the others | Insolvency, farm, QRPBI and QPRI do not apply to a title 11 discharge | `i982.pdf p. 1–3` |
| QPRI vs insolvency | QPRI applies unless you elect insolvency instead (box 1b instead of 1e); the non-qualified part of a mortgage may use insolvency | `i982.pdf p. 1, p. 3` |
| Insolvency vs farm / QRPBI | Farm and QRPBI exclusions do not apply "to the extent you were insolvent" | `i982.pdf p. 2–3` |
| Section 108(b)(5) election (line 5) | Basis of depreciable property first; timely-filed return; revocable only with consent | `i982.pdf p. 1, p. 3` |
| Line 3 election | Dealer real property treated as depreciable | `i982.pdf p. 3` |
| Late elections | Amended return within 6 months, "Filed pursuant to section 301.9100-2" | `i982.pdf p. 1` |
| Section 382(l)(5) | Corporate ownership-change rule | `i982.pdf p. 3` |

## 8. Married filing jointly / separately

The instructions do not give a separate joint-return rule. A joint return files one Form 982 for the excluded debt of
both spouses. The QPRI limit is $375,000 for a married-filing-separately return (`i982.pdf p. 1, p. 3`). How
insolvency is measured for spouses (each spouse's own assets and liabilities versus combined) is explained in
Pub. 4681, which is not in the source set (unverified here).

## 9. Forms to attach or keep

- **Attach** Form 982 whenever an exclusion is claimed (`i982.pdf p. 1`), with the statement describing basis
  reductions under section 1017 (Part II heading, `f982.pdf p. 1`; `i982.pdf p. 3`).
- **Keep** the insolvency worksheet (Pub. 4681), loan and property records, and the court documents for a title 11
  case.

## 10. Where the amount goes next

| From | To |
|---|---|
| Form 982 line 2 | Reduces Schedule 1 line 8c (canceled debt − line 2) → Schedule 1 line 9 → Form 1040 line 8 |
| Line 6 | 2026 NOL carryforward (Form 172) |
| Line 7 | 2026 general business credit carryforward (Form 3800) |
| Line 8 | Minimum tax credit carryforward (Form 8801 line 26) |
| Line 9 | 2026 capital loss carryover (Schedule D) |
| Lines 4, 5, 10a, 10b, 11a–c | Basis of property held on January 1, 2026 (future depreciation and gain) |
| Line 12 | Passive loss / credit carryovers (Forms 8582 / 8582-CR) |
| Line 13 | Foreign tax credit carryover (Form 1116) |

The reductions are made after the 2025 tax is figured: the attributes "for the tax year of the discharge" are used on
the 2025 return first, and what is reduced is what carries to 2026 (line 8 says so explicitly: "as of the beginning of
the tax year immediately after the tax year of the discharge", `f982.pdf p. 1`).

## 11. Worked example

Dana Reyes (fictional, SSN 000-00-0001), single. In 2025 a card issuer cancels $12,000; she gets a Form 1099-C with
box 2 = $12,000. Immediately before the cancellation: liabilities $40,000, FMV of assets $31,000. She has a capital
loss carryover from 2024 of $7,000 and no other capital transactions; she uses $3,000 of it on the 2025 return, so
$4,000 would carry to 2026. Right after the cancellation she owns property with a total basis of $20,000, holds $1,000
in cash and owes $28,000.

1. Insolvency = $40,000 − $31,000 = $9,000. Check box 1b. Line 2 = smaller of $12,000 and $9,000 = **$9,000**.
2. Schedule 1 line 8c = $12,000 − $9,000 = **$3,000** taxable.
3. Part II (she has a capital loss carryover, so the "any other debt" order applies): line 6, 7, 8 — none. Line 9:
   reduce the $4,000 carryover to 2026 by $4,000 → **$4,000** on line 9; $5,000 of the exclusion remains.
4. Line 10a: limit for insolvency = basis $20,000 + cash $1,000 − liabilities $28,000 = −$7,000 → $0. Line 10a =
   **$0**.
5. Lines 12–13: none. The remaining $5,000 stays excluded (insolvency exclusions are not capped by attributes).
6. Result: Form 982 box 1b, line 2 $9,000, line 9 $4,000; Schedule 1 line 8c $3,000; 2026 capital loss carryover $0.
