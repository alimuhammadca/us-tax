# Form 1095-B — Health Coverage

Statement form id `1095-b`. Paths are relative to `C:\us-tax`. `TRCS` =
`us-tax-be/src/main/java/com/ustax/microservices/TaxReturnComputeService.java`; `MAP` =
`us-tax-be/src/main/java/com/ustax/microservices/statement/Form1095BMapper.java`; `UI` =
`us-tax-ui/src/app/forms/form-1095-b.component.ts`. Related: `new_mappings\1095-a.md` (Form 8962),
`new_knowledge\forms\7206.md` (D-7206-5, employer-plan months).

This statement feeds no line of the return, and the IRS does not ask for any amount from it. It matters for one
eligibility test: an individual who is eligible for minimum essential coverage other than individual-market coverage
for a month is not in the Form 8962 coverage family for that month. It can also show employer-sponsored coverage,
which bears on the self-employed health insurance deduction (Schedule 1 line 17 / Form 7206).

---

## 1. What this statement is

Health insurance issuers, government programs (Medicaid, CHIP, Medicare, TRICARE …) and employers with self-insured
plans that are not applicable large employers send Form 1095-B to the "responsible individual" to show the months each
covered person had **minimum essential coverage** (MEC). "Do not attach to your tax return. Keep for your records"
(`docs/IRS-Forms/f1095b.pdf` p. 1). "If individuals in your tax family are eligible for certain types of minimum
essential coverage, you may not be eligible for the premium tax credit" (`f1095b.pdf` p. 2). Since 2025 the provider
may furnish it only on request (`i8962_2025.pdf` p. 1, "Reporting entities no longer required to send … automatically").

IRS instructions used:

- Recipient: "Instructions for Recipient" — `docs/IRS-Forms/f1095b.pdf` p. 2 (form face p. 1, continuation sheet p. 3;
  2025, "Created 9/24/25").
- Filer: `i109495b.pdf` (Instructions for Forms 1094-B and 1095-B, 2025) p. 5 (truncation), p. 6 (lines).
- Use on the return: `i8962_2025.pdf` p. 5 ("Minimum essential coverage (MEC)" and "Eligibility for MEC");
  `i7206.pdf` / `new_knowledge\forms\7206.md` for the employer-plan months.

**Whose statement it is.** Part I line 2, "Social security number (SSN) or other TIN" of the responsible individual
(the policyholder / primary insured), stored as `responsibleSSNOrTIN`. "For your protection, this form may show only
the last four digits" (`f1095b.pdf` p. 2; `i109495b.pdf` p. 5). The new-entry pre-fill does not cover this key
(`us-tax-ui/src/app/service/statement-entry-state.service.ts:33`), so it starts blank. Form 1095-B is not offered on a
dependent's tab (`us-tax-ui/src/app/service/statement-selection.service.ts:133-138`).

**Person filter (the D-1a-1 question).** Not applicable to any amount: compute never loads `1095-b` entries (the only
`"1095-b"` references in `microservices\` outside the mapper are the catalog, `StatementFormCatalog.java:24`, the
household-SSN rule, `StatementSsnRules.java:87`, extraction, `GenericFieldMapper.java:532-547`, and bulk delete,
`UserDataBulkDelete.java:158`). The mapper lists every 1095-B of the account (`MAP:75-84`). The only compute effect is
the household-SSN check, which can **block** the return (D-1095-b-2).

**Storage.** `MAP` — parent keys `:107-142`, Part IV rows `:146-178`; entity `Se1095B` / `se_1095_b`, rows
`Se1095BCoveredIndividual` / `se_1095_b_covered_individual`. Every key bound in the UI (`UI`, both the form view and the
statement view) has a mapper column; no typed value is lost. Extraction: `GenericFieldMapper.java:532-547`
(`readCoveredIndividualGrid` for Part IV, `postProcess1095B`).

## 3. Checkboxes and codes

Line 8, origin of the health coverage (`f1095b.pdf` p. 2): **A** Small Business Health Options Program (SHOP);
**B** employer-sponsored coverage; **C** government-sponsored program; **D** individual market insurance; **E**
multiemployer plan; **F** other designated minimum essential coverage; **G** individual coverage HRA.
Destination: none is read by the app. For the PTC, eligibility for MEC "except coverage in the individual market"
removes that person from the coverage family for the month (`i8962_2025.pdf` p. 5) — so any code except D can matter;
SHOP plans "are not qualified health plans for purposes of the PTC" (`i8962_2025.pdf` p. 5). Column (d) / (e) month
checks give the months.

## 4. Questions the app asks with this statement

None. No personal form asks whether anyone in the tax family had (or was eligible for) Medicaid, CHIP, Medicare,
TRICARE or other MEC during Marketplace months. The Premium Tax Credit form asks only about an employer offer
(`employerCoverageNotAffordable`, stored only — D-1095-c-1). The self-employed deductions form asks for employer-plan
months itself (`new_knowledge\forms\7206.md` §2) instead of reading line 8 code B.

## 5. Differences from the IRS instructions

Reused: **D-7206-5** — the Schedule 1 line 17 deduction is allowed for months the person was eligible for a subsidized
employer plan unless all 12 months are ticked on the self-employed form; a 1095-B with code B is not consulted.

New:

**D-1095-b-1 — Months of other minimum essential coverage never limit the premium tax credit.**
IRS: "An individual in your tax family who is eligible for MEC (except coverage in the individual market) for a month is
not in your coverage family for that month. Therefore, you cannot take the PTC for that individual's coverage for the
months that individual is eligible for MEC" — MEC includes Medicaid, Medicare Part A or C, CHIP, TRICARE and most
employer coverage (`i8962_2025.pdf` p. 5); "If individuals in your tax family are eligible for certain types of minimum
essential coverage, you may not be eligible for the premium tax credit" (`f1095b.pdf` p. 2).
Code: Form 8962 is built from the 1095-A rows only (`TRCS:12892-12960`); no `1095-b` entry is loaded by compute and no
question asks about other coverage. A person on Medicaid or Medicare for months that are also on a 1095-A keeps the
full monthly credit. Effect: **silent overstated credit** (Schedule 3 line 9) / understated repayment. Severity:
**medium** (overlap months happen at Medicaid / Medicare transitions; the Marketplace usually stops APTC, but a
filer claiming the PTC at filing gets it).

**D-1095-b-2 — The household-SSN check can block a return on a statement that is never used.**
IRS: line 2 "may show only the last four digits" (`f1095b.pdf` p. 2; truncation allowed on furnished statements,
`i109495b.pdf` p. 5); the responsible individual is the policyholder, who may be outside the household (for example a
child covered under a former spouse's plan), and "you should provide a copy to other individuals covered under the
policy" (`f1095b.pdf` p. 2).
Code: `1095-b` / `responsibleSSNOrTIN` is in the registry (`StatementSsnRules.java:87`); a truncated SSN
("XXX-XX-1234" → "1234", `TRCS:53713-53719`) or an SSN of someone outside the household raises the **blocking**
`STATEMENT_DOES_NOT_BELONG_TO_FAMILY` (`TRCS:13892-13915`). Nothing in compute reads the 1095-B, so the block protects no
amount. Workaround: leave line 2 blank (skipped, `TRCS:13901`). Effect: **BLOCKING** flag. Severity: **low**.
