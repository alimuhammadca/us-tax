


**★ "(H&R numbering)" is not a correction to the federal form.** Commercial packages lay out their own worksheets, so a comment that shifts the Social Security Benefits Worksheet by one line is describing that package, not the IRS. The real worksheet (Form 1040 instructions, p.32) is: **7** provisional income ("subtract line 6 from line 5"), **14** smaller of line 2 or 13, **15** 85% × line 11, **16** add 14 and 15, **17** 85% × line 1, **18** taxable SS → line 6b. ★ sc_00388 arrived after FIVE consecutive scenarios that each held a genuine line-number error — four in sc_00387 alone — which is exactly when the reflex to apply a sixth is strongest. Reading the printed worksheet costs one command. [[feedback_principled_diagnosis_over_test_tweaking]]

**★ Two different taxable-SS figures in one run is the SIGNATURE of Pub. 590-A Appendix B.** Pass 1 computes taxable SS with the IRA deduction at $0 to fix MAGI and the §219(g) reduced limit; pass 2 recomputes it with the deduction actually allowed. To verify an engine solves it, don't just match line 6b — re-run the identical return with **no** IRA contribution and confirm its taxable SS equals the pass-1 figure and its AGI equals the MAGI that drove the phase-out (sc_00388: 32,550 and 79,550). A single-pass engine uses one number for both and misstates the deduction *and* the taxable SS. Pins: `Sc00388SqaScenarioTest`.

**★ The IRA deduction is CLAIMED, then reduced — seed `iraDeductionLine20` on the income-adjustments form.** There is no separate IRA-contributions form; `traditionalIraContributionAmount` alone drives the §4973 excess-contribution excise, not the deduction. Active-participant status comes from `isCoveredByWorkplaceRetirementPlanTaxpayer` **or** the W-2 box 13 checkbox. Seed it wrong and taxable SS silently returns the pass-1 figure with no deduction and no flag.

**★ Age 60 matters twice, and only one of them applies.** It raises the IRA contribution limit to $8,000 (7,000 + the age-50 catch-up), which is the base the §219(g) phase-out multiplies; it does **not** give an additional standard deduction, which requires 65.
